# Erde physical-test source notes

These sources constrain mechanisms and scales. They do **not** make Erde inherit Earth's exact chronology, coastline, climate, sea-level curve, or hominin behavior.

## Sea level

- Rachel M. Spratt and Lorraine E. Lisiecki (2016), *A Late Pleistocene sea level stack*, Climate of the Past 12:1079–1092. NOAA/NCEI study 19982 archives the 0–798 ka stack. Use: establishes that glacial-interglacial sea level repeatedly varied by many tens to over 100 m and that uncertainty is material. Erde's test curve is an authored amplitude-bounded scenario, not the Earth series copied by date.

## Long-lived arcs and later land closure

- O'Dea et al. (2016), *Formation of the Isthmus of Panama*, Science Advances 2:e1600883. Use: supports distinguishing old arc/terrane crust from much later complete terrestrial closure. The Panama Arc existed long before the final dry-land isthmus; JX1 uses that sequence as a mechanism analogue, not a copied location or date.

## Archaic hominins and persistent water barriers

- Brumm et al. (2010), *Hominins on Flores, Indonesia, by one million years ago*, Nature 464:748–752. Use: establishes hominin presence on Flores by at least 1.02 ± 0.02 Ma despite required water crossing.
- van den Bergh and colleagues' subsequent Flores work and 2025 Sulawesi evidence reinforce that archaic hominins entered oceanic Wallacean settings. Use: permits genuine water barriers in Erde's eastern radiation region. It does not establish a universal safe crossing distance, vessel technology, or founding probability.

The project therefore uses an intentionally conservative engineering target of <=80 km for each repeated-founding leg after the initial ~188 km candidate fails. This is a project design target, not a claim that 80 km is a biological or technological threshold.

## Midlatitude glaciation and refugia

- Hulton et al. (1994/2002) Patagonian ice-sheet modelling and later Patagonia reviews. Use: supports extensive mountain glaciation around 40–50°S, strong topographic/precipitation controls, and spatially heterogeneous ice margins.
- Convey et al. and British Antarctic Survey reviews of Antarctic refugia. Use: supports localized long-lived ice-free refugia while warning against assuming broad productive polar interiors.

C4 therefore uses a midlatitude maritime foreland at ~32–46°S with an inland glaciated divide. Human-scale habitat is assigned to warm-phase connected lowlands and multiple cold-phase peripheral refugia, not to the south-polar interior.

## Evidence boundary

The current tests are necessary-condition and sensitivity tests. Remaining high-fidelity work includes time-dependent ice flow, local relative sea level including isostasy/geoid effects, ocean-current/voyage modelling, and a full plate-block reconstruction. A bounded physical-test pass means the geometry no longer contains an identified contradiction at the tested scale; it does not certify a unique reconstruction.
