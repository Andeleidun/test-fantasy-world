# Erde geographic divergence proposal

Status: authorial proposal for discussion, not adopted geography. The first recommendation has now been tried and evaluated in [the offset-continent trial](geography-trial/README.md). No public coastline or named continent changes are made by this document. Earth names below identify source geometry only.

## Subsequent scope correction

The user subsequently required substantial body and coastline changes across **all six continental groups**, with historical connections and isolation preserved throughout their relevant periods. This supersedes the limited paired-continent-first scope below. The [worldwide outline study](global-geography-trial/README.md) and [historical acceptance ledger](global-geography-trial/HISTORICAL-CONSTRAINTS.md) record the new candidate and its unresolved gates. The earlier relocated hinge is not included in that candidate because its required earlier history has not been demonstrated.

The study is not adopted geography. A modern connection, or an unchanged patch of coastline, does not validate a historical route.

## Goal and recommendation

Make Erde’s continental silhouettes and relative arrangement less immediately recognizable while preserving the geological and ecological conditions supporting its established population histories. Use modest differences in plate trajectories, regional rifting, collision geometry and coastal subsidence over tens of millions of years. Preserve the functional geography of migrations, refuges and resource networks; exact source-coordinate locations may change.

The strongest first candidate is an offset continental pair with a relocated volcanic hinge and a partly drowned former coastal connection. A second priority is reshaping the mainland–shelf–island system supporting the hobbit radiation. Changes to the remaining continental silhouettes can follow without requiring a wholesale rearrangement of oceans.

This is a plausible design direction, not a validated plate reconstruction or climate result. We can retain the required habitat types and population connections as design constraints. We cannot promise unchanged climates, species assemblages or evolutionary outcomes after changing geography.

## Candidate changes

These alternatives form a menu, not six automatically compatible changes. Plate boundaries and ocean circulation must be reconciled across whichever package is selected.

| Authorial reference region | Candidate geological difference | Visual result | Preservation condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North/South America pair | Modestly different relative plate rotation and displacement; an arc docks against different neighboring coastal sectors | A staggered pair with a broader, bent junction instead of the familiar narrow waist | Keep an early traversable connection between the two continents, the equatorial basin’s catchment, and the highland-to-coast network |
| Central American/Caribbean margin | Different arc accretion plus local extension and subsidence leaves an attached land connection beside a curved chain of large islands | An island-fringed hinge with multiple bays and sheltered coastal routes | Retain volcanic highlands, freshwater refuges and terrestrial movement; avoid opening a deep unrestricted inter-ocean channel without climate review |
| Eurasian peninsular margins | Different oblique collision geometry and small continental-block rotations, established well before the human migrations | Broader or offset peninsulas, fewer familiar narrow projections, a differently oriented inland sea | Retain interior contact corridors, rain-shadow diversity and ancestral peripheral dispersal routes |
| Sunda/Wallacea/Sahul system | An offset continental fragment, altered arc curvature and partial flooding of continental margins | An asymmetric large island continent with a long gulf and a distinct arrangement of shelf islands and highland blocks | Some shelf islands reconnect, some deep channels never do; retain sufficient habitat, water and repeatable founder access |
| South-polar African-derived continent | Different peripheral rifting and basin subsidence detach one marginal block and indent another coast | A broad embayed polar landmass with offshore continental islands rather than a familiar taper | Protect the productive ancestral coastal network and its outbound connection; do not place refuges on ice merely to preserve old coordinates |
| Equatorial Antarctic-derived continent | Selective flooding of existing low basins and a limited rift embayment reshape its margins | A more lobed, asymmetric landmass | Keep substantial connected interior habitat and assess changes to neighboring currents; its ecological detail is comparatively underdetermined |

A useful exploratory displacement range is roughly 200–800 km for selected relative positions accumulated over approximately 20–40 million years. These are authorial starting ranges, not inferred motions. At 1–3 cm/year, displacement accumulates at 10–30 km per million years. Individual plate rotations must be specified around a spherical Euler pole and checked against neighboring plates; arbitrary rotations of continent polygons are not a tectonic reconstruction.

Most large shape and position differences should already be in place before the million-year-scale settlement histories. Over the later migration windows, sea level, regional uplift/subsidence, rivers and climate can open or close established approaches. A few centuries of erosion or a recent catastrophe cannot explain continental-scale rearrangement.

## Turning a coast into an island chain

Two mechanisms fit this project particularly well:

1. Flooded coastal uplands: fault-controlled subsidence and/or relative sea-level rise drown low valleys, leaving connected hills and ridges as islands. These may retain old soils, freshwater, continental organisms and shallow channels. A lower sea level can reconnect some of them.
2. A rifted continental strip: extension gradually separates a marginal block, with further faulting creating several large islands and intervening basins. This needs millions of years and an explicit plate setting. Older inherited continental habitats make it different from a new volcanic arc.

A volcanic island arc is a separate mechanism. Volcanic islands are not necessarily fragments of a drowned coast and may begin without the same soils or terrestrial organisms. The Volcanic Hinge can combine an active arc, accreted blocks and flooded coastal valleys, but each should have its own geological origin.

An island ladder is not biologically equivalent to a land bridge. It filters terrestrial animals, freshwater organisms and plants differently. Favor large islands with freshwater, sheltered landings and food resources. Several short crossings, sometimes visible from elevated departure points, are easier to support than a long exposed passage, but distance alone cannot establish feasibility: current, wind, craft, return access and founding population size matter. Accidental rafting should remain a limited mechanism, not a universal explanation for successful colonization.

For both established paired-continent founding events, the conservative choice is a shelf/island system that becomes an adequately continuous terrestrial corridor during the needed intervals. Replacing either principal founding with obligatory water crossings would revise an existing decision. Later regional maritime movement and the island radiation already provide more freedom to use genuine island hopping.

## Ecology and climate checks

- Preserve catchment area, headwater elevation, rainfall supply and outlets for the Great Watersheds and Equatorial Basin. A visually attractive gulf can destroy a required watershed if placed through its trunk.
- Preserve relief and routes between ecological elevations for Highland Spine; a mountain symbol alone does not supply a viable vertical network.
- Keep an active or recently active tectonic setting for the volcanic refuge system, with enough separate productive refuges to recover from local disasters.
- Keep the shelf cradle near suitable native latitudes and moisture sources; karst requires an appropriate carbonate history rather than decorative terrain labels.
- Protect warm/cool habitat mosaics and refuges through time, not merely their appearance on the present map.
- Treat narrow ocean gateways cautiously. A small opening or closure can have larger climatic consequences than a broad coastal indentation. A tectonic design cannot guarantee that the old rainfall or sea-level assumptions still hold.
- Estimate plausible sea-level change from Erde’s own ice storage and climate. Do not transplant Earth’s glacial chronology or lowstand magnitude automatically.

## Recommended development sequence

1. Record the protected migration and habitat relationships above as the acceptance criteria.
2. Sketch two or three candidate versions of the offset pair and volcanic hinge; compare silhouettes and route continuity in the same Equal Earth projection.
3. Fit the selected candidate to coherent plate motions, crustal blocks and boundaries. Assign geological origins to the new islands and gulfs.
4. Extend the same reconstruction to the island-radiation region and other coastlines, preserving native latitude constraints.
5. Produce geographic snapshots before the earliest million-year-scale settlement, during each principal founding window, during representative isolation intervals, around 5,000 years ago, and at present. A modern map alone cannot test the migration history.
6. Evaluate topography, bathymetry, drainage, climate tendencies and dispersal capacity. Check animal and plant exchange alongside human movement.
7. Adopt the preferred geography only after review; update map source geometry, feature positions, migration overlays and public explanations together. Keep the Earth crosswalk authorial.

## Scientific references and their limits

- [USGS: understanding plate motions](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html): ordinary plate motion provides the scale for million-year displacements. The numerical design range above is an extrapolation, not an Erde measurement.
- [USGS: magmatic evolution of the Gulf of California rift](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/magmatic-evolution-gulf-california-rift): a real example of a continental margin evolving from subduction to rifting over millions of years; an analogue for mechanism, not an instruction to copy that coastline.
- [Kaifu et al., 2020: Palaeolithic voyage for invisible islands beyond the horizon](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-76831-7): visibility and currents affect maritime feasibility, and accidental drift cannot simply be assumed to explain settlement. Its Earth routes and human capabilities do not validate the much earlier fictional migrations.
- [British Antarctic Survey: closure of the Panama Seaway](https://www.bas.ac.uk/data/our-data/publication/closure-of-the-panama-seaway-during-the-pliocene-implications-for-climate-and-northern-hemisphere-glaciation/): ocean gateways can affect circulation and climate. It does not predict the direction or magnitude of effects on Erde.

No physical reconstruction or climate simulation has yet been executed for these candidates. The original paired-continent-first next step has been superseded by the worldwide study and its historical acceptance ledger linked above.
