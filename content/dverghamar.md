## A world between day and night

**Dverghamar** is the ordinary native planetary name. **Dvergahamrar** is its archaic or highly formal form. The world is synchronously rotating: one hemisphere faces its star while the other remains in darkness.

The current working reference is a rocky planet of approximately **three Earth masses and 1.4 Earth radii**, with surface gravity near **1.53 g**. Its host is an old, relatively quiet early-M star. A viable history must still explain how the planet retained enough water and atmosphere through the star’s earlier activity.

## The surface environment

| Region | Working surface temperatures | Ecological character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central dayside | Mostly 340–375 K, with exceptional dark basins near 385 K | Exposed surface conditions exclude ordinary large Earth-like organisms; microbes remain possible in suitable niches. |
| Twilight belt | The transitional corridor between the hemispheres | Photosynthesis, agriculture and the principal exposed macroscopic biosphere. |
| Deep nightside | Mostly 205–230 K, with colder local traps | Sparse active life in brine veins, geothermal fractures and protected subsurface habitats. |

These are scenario targets, not the output of a coupled climate model. Habitability depends on water, heat, exposure and energy supply, rather than distance from the geometric terminator alone.

## Water, geology and food webs

The accepted water inventory is approximately **0.15–0.2 Earth oceans**. Twilight mountain rivers feed lakes and closed basins. Rain shadows and evaporative salt basins interrupt the living corridor, leaving several major ecological provinces joined by narrower valleys and passes.

The largest natural lakes cover roughly **50,000–150,000 km²**. Their oxygenated surface and coastal waters can lie above permanently stratified, often anoxic depths. Common large animals may reach two to five metres, with rare forms approaching one to three tonnes.

Isolated nightside geothermal oases operate on a much smaller energy budget. Geological reductants and slow oxidant delivery support sparse food webs; ordinary selected consumers are small and sparse, without a universal finger-length maximum. Irregular subglacial floods can briefly connect otherwise isolated habitats.

## Living inside the mountains

The Hamarkorar strongly prefer underground residence. Their settlements still depend on air exchange, water, food, transport and heat removal. A room high inside a mountain can have modest rock cover even when it stands far above the surrounding lowlands.

Twilight-zone river hydropower is the principal industrial energy source. Direct mechanical transmission, hydraulic systems and fuel engines coexist with limited electricity and selected geothermal use. Lit near-surface cultivation and substrate-fed fungal farming support underground life.

## Integrated physical reference

The revised scenario uses 240 million km³ water-equivalent, mostly in regional nightside ice basins. Its reservoir arithmetic is closed; its climate and sustainable water-return fluxes remain unvalidated. Wet refuge provinces require catchment runoff, viable glacier outlets or connected groundwater. The Thals’ permanent mountain homes favor moderate-elevation illuminated districts with compatible Erde-derived cultivation. The detailed reference and scientific review retain the assumptions and unresolved tests.
