## A familiar foundation, a different world

Erde uses Earth’s continental arrangement as a reference scaffold, with a selected planetary orientation that puts the north pole at Earth-reference latitude 0°, longitude 150° west. The south pole lies at the antipode, 0°, 30° east.

The resulting latitude changes reshape the questions of climate, movement and isolation. They do not justify transplanting modern Earth biomes onto a rotated map. Currents, ice, rainfall, erosion and independent geological history still need their own treatment.

## Humans and hobbits

The current history develops human and hobbit macroclades through a long, branching evolutionary scenario. The Sunda–mainland separation is important to that structure. Hobbit branches include Flores, Luzon, Papuan-highland and pelagic forms.

Rare, low-fertility interbreeding is part of the working model. Ancestry, language, culture and political belonging need not coincide. A biological lineage does not dictate one culture or a single historical outcome.

## The First American radiation

An early erectus-derived migration reaches the Americas in the working interval of roughly 650,000–450,000 years ago. Its descendants diversify into five principal regional complexes. Later sapiens contact produces varied outcomes: persistence, mixture, displacement and successor populations.

The detailed ledger explores Great Watersheds, Volcanic Hinge, Highland Spine and Equatorial Basin histories alongside northern contact. These regions have different routes, ecological pressures and forms of continuity.

## The Thal departure

The Thal catastrophe occurred on **Erde**, approximately five thousand years ago. The surviving population fled through the Otherworld to Dverghamar. It is not a catastrophe in native dwarven evolutionary history.

The remaining Neanderthal populations on Erde became extinct or were absorbed. The Thals continue as their surviving descendants on another world.
