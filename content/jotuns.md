# The Jotun

> Historical research snapshot, retained through 14 September 2026. For the current public edition, use [the public collection](public/README.md). This file is not a current publication source.

## The approved direction

**Jotun** is the current self-name for Dverghamar's mountain giants. The approved first model is a cold-adapted mountain people combining rime-associated physiology with limited stone-backed protective features. It is a design direction, not a completed species account.

Jotun settlements concentrate in productive mountain margins, valleys and connected refuge landscapes. Individuals make high-terrain excursions, but exposed summits and barren ice cannot by themselves support large permanent populations.

## Size under strong gravity

The provisional body is a heavily supported biped about 2.5–3 metres tall. Dverghamar's roughly 1.53 g surface gravity demands stout limbs, controlled proportions and conservative loads. Thirty-foot giants belong to exaggeration, exceptional magic or legend rather than the nonmagical biological baseline.

Whether the Jotun belong to the planet's six-limbed native radiation remains open. A two-armed biped cannot be assumed to share Hamarkor ancestry without an explicit evolutionary history.

## Cultural inspiration without a single culture

The initial design draws selectively on several Norse figures: Skaði for knowledge of mountain belonging, Ymir for life at environmental boundaries, Hrungnir for stony appearance and protective anatomy, and Vafþrúðnir for wisdom and intellectual authority.

These inspirations do not produce one uniform society. Jotun cultures can include hospitality, scholarship, environmental knowledge, negotiation, clan histories and remembered violence without treating any of them as inherited moral traits.

## Future branches and open gates

A semiaquatic cold-water branch inspired by Hymir's cauldron is the strongest complementary direction for later work. An ash-and-heat branch inspired by Surtr is harder to reconcile and is not selected. Neither is current canon.

Ancestry and limb count, skeletal mechanics, thermal physiology, food demand, carrying capacity, reproduction and lifespan remain open. The next step is one coherent cold-mountain Jotun model under the selected gravity and climate.
