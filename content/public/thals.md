# The Thals


## From Erde to Hamarkar


The Thals descend from an ancient human lineage of Erde. About **40,000 years ago**, a catastrophe drove a large migration away from Erde. Thal traditions, old records and later scholarship agree that the flight involved extraordinary passages associated with the Otherworld and ended in Hamarkar's twilight mountains.


Accounts disagree about the route. Some describe one vast otherworldly journey, others several realms or thresholds, and still others preserve only fragments of the crossing. Public evidence does not establish which description is literally correct.


Animals, plants and other living companions from the refugees' homeland appear repeatedly in migration traditions and in later Thal ecology. Which surviving lineages truly came with the refugees, which arrived through other contacts, and which stories combine several events remain subjects for naturalists and historians.


## Survival and change


Migration stories describe desperate practitioners, frightened communities and an overwhelming shared need to survive. Many traditions also say that travelers emerged changed. Present Thals are well adapted to their adopted world, but ordinary scholarship cannot cleanly separate changes acquired during the passage from later adaptation, inherited variation, magic, diet and tens of thousands of years of life on Hamarkar.


Whatever happened during the crossing did not remove ordinary needs. Food, water, shelter, rest, childbirth, illness and the heavier pull of Hamarkar continue to shape Thal life.


## Mountain refuge life


Thal communities are especially associated with cool illuminated mountain and cave districts with sheltered terrain, dependable water and suitable growing ground. Near-surface chambers, terraces and protected fields allow familiar crops to receive light. Fungal cultivation, preservation and trade supplement agriculture.


Knowledge brought from Erde remains culturally important, but unfamiliar Hamarkaran organisms must be judged through local experience rather than resemblance alone.


## Ancestry and identity


Thals are related to ancient human populations of Erde. Comparative anatomy and preserved histories support that broad relationship without providing the precision of a modern genetic reconstruction.


The indigenous **Dverghamar** peoples of Hamarkar do not descend from the Thal refugees. Their natural history belongs to Hamarkar itself. Jotuns are another distinct people with their own histories and traditions.


There is no single Thal culture. Regional communities differ in language, institutions, magic, subsistence, relationships with neighbors and interpretation of the migration.


## Memory of Erde


Stories of mammoths, rhinos, great eagles, cave bears, cave hyenas and cave lions preserve memories of an older homeland and its animals. Shells, fossils, crystals, feathers, talons and other objects can carry ancestral or regional significance. Such traditions do not imply that every remembered animal survives on Hamarkar.


## Magic and scholarship


Thal intellectual traditions emphasize learned magic, inherited knowledge, history, environmental expertise and scholarship rather than industrial technology or scientific institutions. Careful observation, agriculture, building, medicine and craft remain highly developed in many communities.


Accounts of the migration are therefore preserved through oral history, ritual, records, material evidence and magical traditions whose interpretations sometimes disagree.
