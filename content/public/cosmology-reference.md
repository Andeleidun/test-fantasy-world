# Spirits, souls and living worlds

## Material worlds and the Otherworld

The Otherworld is an ancient realm with an existence of its own. Material universes form coherent realities within a larger cosmological setting; the Otherworld is not simply a tunnel network inside a planet or a landscape created entirely by a traveler’s imagination.

Its routes are nonlocal. A journey can connect places separated by ordinary material distance without placing their planets next to one another. Local geography and environmental conditions still matter to a traveler’s experience.

## Crossings and travel

Crossings form under particular local and magical conditions. Stable endpoints and familiar routes make travel more predictable, but access, duration and protection depend on circumstances and practitioners. An endpoint’s persistence does not guarantee a safe journey through everything beyond it.

Travelers can encounter unfamiliar terrain, native life, unstable environmental conditions and temporal differences. Nearer, more stable regions permit more reliable navigation and retreat. Greater depth brings increased uncertainty, though it is not simply a universal vertical distance measured beneath the ground.

Protective practices differ in their architecture and in what they can sustain. A traveler’s competence, preparation and ongoing capacity shape their protection. Consequences of exposure can persist after returning to a material world.

Time becomes less predictable with depth. Different amounts of elapsed time do not permit travel into one’s own past: causal order remains forward. Ordinary route planning cannot treat temporal instability as a reliable clock or a reversible journey.

## Native life

The Otherworld has autonomous ecologies. Its organisms maintain coherence through strategies suited to their own environments, and magic can be an ordinary ecological capability within those strategies.

Travelers’ expectations and powers can influence local conditions without becoming the sole authors of every creature or place. A living community may have its own history, needs and agency before any visitor arrives.

## Living spirits and emerging souls

Every living organism possesses a spirit. Soulhood develops gradually through a more differentiated continuity of self. It is not reserved by an arbitrary list of species, nor assigned through a single intelligence score.

Within life, spirit and soul interpenetrate. Their distinction concerns how personal coherence persists and participates in larger relationships. A living spirit contributes to the world even when it does not maintain a separately coherent personal soul after death.

Collectives can also develop agency. Exceptional collective personhood requires deeper coherence than a temporary gathering or a large population count. Not every responsive ecosystem, community or planetary spirit is already a person capable of personal consent.

## Death and planetary return

Death ends the ordinary continuity of embodied life. A sufficiently differentiated soul can persist separately for a time, but domainless personal coherence normally gives way rapidly to planetary return.

Return is participation in the greater living spirit, with the loss of separate personal differentiation. It is not a punitive sentence or a guarantee that the same individual will be reborn. The planetary whole can retain resonances of lives without keeping each former person available as an independent, fully active inhabitant.

The living face an epistemic veil: ordinary evidence does not provide certain access to the complete afterlife mechanism. Religion, testimony and interpretation can therefore vary even where the setting has an underlying cosmological rule.

## Afterlife domains

An afterlife domain sustains continuing personhood through reciprocal relationships. Entry requires voluntary reception rather than automatic ownership by a founder, institution or ancestral group.

A powerful founder can seed a domain, while continuing sustenance becomes distributed among its relationships and contributors. Domains have finite reserves and capacity. Difficult succession, changing commitments and loss of support can weaken them.

Residents can experience flexible embodiment organized around self-recognition. The environment reflects collective relationships, with room for individual difference. Embodiment remains vulnerable, and healing or restoration requires a coherent basis rather than unlimited redesign.

Afterlife populations do not generate new souls through ordinary reproduction. Continued learning and activity do not imply an unlimited new supply of innate power. Rare growth can come through deep reciprocal responsibility, fundamental transformation, finite transfer from a willing giver, or durable pooling among distinct souls. Internalized growth, transformation and permanent transfers can persist after a relationship ends; access to pooled capacity ends with the bond.

## Consent, departure and failure

A soul can withdraw its consent and contribution immediately. A domain cannot turn an earlier commitment into permanent spiritual ownership. The consequences of withdrawal still depend on what capacity remains.

Withdrawal ends the structural role, not every other relationship. If the domain survives, a former contributor can remain as an ordinary resident. Choosing to leave the domain is a separate decision.

Leaving one domain does not guarantee safe reception elsewhere. A new domain must reciprocally receive the departing soul, and unsupported coherence can fail. This distinction preserves free departure without making every possible destination available on demand.

Collapse can leave fragments capable of repair under suitable conditions and renewed consent. Enough of the core self-pattern must survive for restoration to preserve personal continuity. Self-aware fragments must consent to reintegration. Healing can restore capacities without recovering every lost memory: an exact memory requires a surviving trace of that person’s experience. Domain reserves, continuity of relationships and available power shape what survives.

## Communion and smaller domains

Living practitioners can project into domain-specific communion while remaining tethered to their material bodies. The projected presence is not an ordinary second life and does not make death reversible.

A disrupted tether can force an attempted reintegration, with serious consequences including impaired consciousness or bodily harm. Successful return is not guaranteed. If the material body dies, the visitor is actually dead; continuing residence then requires the appropriate reciprocal reception.

Smaller **demidomains** can form through household, kinship and other sustained relational networks. Places, remains and objects may anchor them, but the relationship is not reducible to ownership of a bone or possession. Networks can move and reorganize while maintaining continuity.

Membership and afterlife choice remain sovereign. Shared ancestry, residence or ritual participation does not give a household an irrevocable claim over a soul.

## Planetary spirits

A living planet’s spirit is distributed through its life and relationships. It can respond through ecological, geological and spiritual patterns without having a single human-like viewpoint.

Exceptional integration can produce a planetary person. This is a gradual emergence of coherence, not an automatic result of age, biomass or population. Planetary death likewise concerns the collapse of living coherence rather than a simple absence of inhabitants from one region.

Cultivation and sustained collective action can shape these processes. Lesser collectives can influence the world without becoming planetary persons themselves.

## Integration between worlds

When two planetary spirits possess collective souls, both planetary persons must consent to integration. Inhabitants also influence the outcome through their actions, relationships and survival, although unanimous population consent is not a universal metaphysical gate.

A pre-personal spirit cannot give personal consent. Skilled observers instead interpret converging ecological, magical, geological and spiritual responses. No single sign conclusively establishes compatibility or resistance.

Populations can create compatible anchors or resistant boundaries through coordinated ritual. They can also directly attack planetary spirits, migrate, build refuges or selectively withdraw from affected regions. These interventions have material consequences for the integration process and for those who remain.

## Preserving a dying world

Preservation depends on continuing contributions at a demanding scale. Contributors may withdraw immediately, so a durable network cannot rely on permanently binding every participant.

A mature network keeps substantial reserves, overlapping contributors and trained replacements. It sheds peripheral functions before the planetary person suffers direct injury. Temporary emergency contributors can stabilize the network without accepting a permanent commitment.

These arrangements reduce the danger of ordinary departures but do not create infinite capacity. Severe losses can still force contraction, injury or failure. The right to leave and the work of sustaining others remain simultaneous realities.

## The outer boundary

The **Howling Void** lies outside coherent reality. It is not the organizing cause of ordinary geology, cultures or biological evolution. Loss of coherence there can destroy souls; it is not an everlasting residence for intact minds.
