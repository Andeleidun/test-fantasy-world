# Habitats and wildlife

## A living landscape still taking shape

Dverghamar’s food web has a developed ecological foundation: photosynthetic production in the lit twilight region, substantial lake communities, small chemically supported oases, and a native six-limbed ancestry leading to the Hamarkorar. Many organisms still await names and visible forms.

The following distinctions describe what is already present in the setting and where appearance, behavior and evolutionary detail remain open.

## The lit wet provinces

Photosynthetic organisms provide the main energy supply. They can form rooted or attached communities on moist ground, slopes and shallow lake margins. Storage organs offer a basis for tuber-like crops, while fungal organisms process organic material in soil, litter and cultivated substrates.

Their pigments, textures, reproductive structures and seasonal responses have not been fixed. A redder stellar spectrum influences useful light but does not by itself choose black leaves, a particular leaf shape or a single photosynthetic pathway.

Consumers include small grazers, detritus feeders, predators and the ancestors of larger terrestrial animals. Their coverings, senses and locomotion can vary among branches. Six limbs belong to a particular ancestry rather than a rule imposed on all fauna.

## Lakes and shorelines

The largest lakes offer oxygenated coasts and surface waters, with stratified, often anoxic depths. Large animals can reach several meters, with rare forms approaching one to three tonnes. Their ordinary feeding grounds remain connected to productive, oxygenated habitat.

Open details include whether the largest forms filter-feed, browse, hunt or combine several diets; how smaller prey move between shore and open water; and how populations recover after disruptive mixing. Buoyancy permits substantial size, but food supply determines how many large consumers a lake can sustain.

## Sunward ground

The outer dayside contains terminal brines, hot dry surfaces and cool aquifers extending from the margin. Natural macroscopic life reaches sunward through those cooler water-bearing habitats. Deeper dayside regions have microbes only in the current model.

Microbial activity is usually visually inconspicuous. Occasional favorable wet or chemical conditions can produce visible patches or brief blooms. Colors, films, crusts and other surface expressions remain to be described rather than assumed to cover the whole hemisphere.

## Nightside oases

Most active near-surface microbial life is rare and confined to brine veins or geothermal fractures. Subglacial oases draw energy from local geological reductants and a slow supply of oxidants. Their common consumers are finger-length or smaller, sparse between irregular flood connections.

Floods can carry organisms, food and dissolved chemicals between normally isolated habitats. Temporary blooms follow favorable pulses; long intervals of isolation encourage local differences. Dormancy, dispersal stages, sensory systems and the exact relationships between oases remain useful areas for further development.

## Caves and mountain interiors

Natural cave communities depend on imported organic matter, local chemical production or connections to productive water. Greater depth increases the importance of heat, oxygen and transport. A large inhabited cavern is not automatically a naturally self-feeding ecosystem.

Dwarven settlement adds maintained air, water, crops, waste streams and deliberate food storage. This creates opportunities for commensals, cultivated fungi, pests and domesticated animals. None of those groups yet has a complete species catalog.
