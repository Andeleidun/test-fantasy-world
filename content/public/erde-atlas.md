# Maps of Erde

## Erde in five views

The sheets below move from planetary orientation to latitude, relief, ancestry and regional contact. Familiar land shapes help show how the changed axis rearranges the world.

The coastlines are a reference scaffold. Climate bands, mountain axes and migration routes have different levels of certainty; each caption explains what its map can tell you. The American contact sheet represents about five thousand years before Erde’s present, while the dispersal sheet includes much older movements.

Open any map for its full-size SVG. [Reading the atlas](map-methods.md) explains coordinates, scale and schematic placement.

## Map sheets

- [E1 · Orientation comparison](../../assets/maps/E1-orientation-comparison.svg): Earth-reference land geometry is rotated into the chosen Erde frame. This is an orientation scaffold; it does not calculate the effects of an alternate geological history on shorelines.
- [E2 · Latitude and climate potential](../../assets/maps/E2-latitude-and-climate-potential.svg): The same land geometry is compared with new latitude bands. The bands represent solar-forcing potential, not a climate simulation or a finished biome map.
- [E3 · Relief and passages](../../assets/maps/E3-relief-and-passages.svg): Approximate mountain and passage axes suggest where elevation and barriers could shape movement. Relief and routes are illustrative rather than surveyed.
- [E4 · Ancestry and dispersal](../../assets/maps/E4-ancestry-and-dispersal.svg): Ancestry and dispersal overlays connect the African stem, Sunda radiation, Sahul branch and early American migration. They are historical scenarios rather than exact tracks.
- [E5 · American contact landscape](../../assets/maps/E5-american-contact-landscape.svg): The five First American regional complexes are shown at the period about five thousand years before Erde’s present. The northern successor outcome does not erase independent southern histories.
