# The Dverghamar peoples


## A broad indigenous identity


**Dverghamar** is the broad collective ethnonym for Hamarkar's indigenous dwarven peoples and also names their language family. In ordinary reader-facing English they may simply be called **dwarves**. The name is old and fossilized; modern speakers do not treat it as a transparent compound whose pieces define every person.


Rakkor/Rakkorar and Menkor/Menkorar remain narrower regional, cultural or taxonomic names whose exact assignments are still being worked out. Language, political membership, region and biological population do not automatically coincide.


## Four arms and two legs


Dverghamar peoples have four arms and two legs and belong to a native six-limbed ancestry. The upper arms are often especially useful for precision work and the lower pair for power, bracing and carrying, while both retain substantial dexterity. Holding, steadying and working on an object can happen together.


Exact adult dimensions, finger counts and external coverings vary among populations and remain incompletely described.


## Related peoples in different habitats


Naturalists currently distinguish five principal species and a deep-massif subspecies.


| Population | Principal environmental pressures |
| --- | --- |
| Central massif species | Varied mountain interiors, slopes and connected surface catchments |
| Deep-massif subspecies | Greater reliance on enclosed habitats, while retaining connections to shallower ecosystems |
| Sunward species | Dry heat, water conservation and warm-margin travel |
| Volcanic-range species | Humid heat, ash and locally distinctive chemical exposure |
| Cold-edge species | Cold-margin conditions near the productive twilight belt |
| High-mountain species | Altitude, thin air and cold |


Their ranges can overlap. Languages, communities and political affiliations do not follow species boundaries automatically. The deep-massif population remains closely related to central-massif populations, with fertile exchange where communities meet.


## Adaptation and senses


Sunward populations are relatively sparsely insulated and somewhat longer-limbed, while volcanic-range populations are comparatively slender. Cold-edge peoples tend toward short extremities and strong insulation. High-mountain peoples face both cold and thin air. Heat, ash, fumes, altitude and cold can still harm people adapted to those environments.


Vision is retained across the known populations. Hearing, vibration, smell, touch and artificial lighting also matter in enclosed or poorly lit places. Deeper communities remain connected with shallower habitats and wider food supplies.


## Care and households


Nurseries bring several kinds of caregivers together. Korvar distinguishes reproductive parentage, protective care, food provision and shared residence. One person can hold several roles, and households take many forms.


## Household life and material knowledge


Dwelling within rock encourages close attention to cracks, grain, moisture, heat and the behavior of material under load. This knowledge appears in language, construction and nursery care. Hexagonal arrangements suit some supports, compartments and markings, while other shapes serve tunnels, water channels and rooms better.
