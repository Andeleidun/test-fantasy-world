# Dverghamar

## A world of permanent day and night

**Dverghamar** is the ordinary native name of the dwarven world; **Dvergahamrar** is its archaic or highly formal form. One hemisphere faces its star continuously, while the other remains in darkness. Between them lies a broad twilight region, broken into living provinces by mountains, dry basins and stretches of salt country.

The planet is modeled as a rocky world of about **three Earth masses**, **1.4 Earth radii** and **1.53 times Earth’s surface gravity**. It orbits an old, relatively quiet early-M star, a smaller and cooler star than the Sun. These values form a working physical model; the exact atmosphere, water cycle and climate still require a coupled simulation.

## The inhabited landscapes

The central dayside lowlands are mostly 67–102°C, with exceptional dark basins approaching 112°C. The deep nightside is mostly −68 to −43°C, with colder traps below that range. Heat and desiccation on the dayside, and cold, darkness and scarce food on the nightside, prevent broad self-sustaining communities of large Earth-like animals. Cold alone would not exclude every supplied or sheltered animal. Microbes can persist in suitable protected niches.

Most visible life occupies the twilight region. Lit slopes support photosynthetic organisms. Rivers descend from mountain catchments into lakes and closed basins. Sheltered valleys and mountain passes connect larger wet provinces, while rain shadows and evaporative salt flats interrupt the belt.

The corridor is not equally hospitable throughout. The sunward side has light but increasing heat and water stress. The nightward fringe receives little useful light and depends increasingly on food carried from illuminated habitats.

## Water and life

The accessible surface and near-surface water inventory is about **0.15–0.20 Earth oceans**. A few lakes cover **50,000–150,000 km²** each, accompanied by many smaller bodies of water. Productive surface and coastal waters can support large animals above cold, poorly oxygenated depths.

Farther into the nightside, isolated geothermal oases support much smaller communities. Their energy comes from geological chemical reactions, with oxidants delivered slowly through ice and groundwater. The largest common consumers are finger-length or smaller. Occasional subglacial floods connect oases and bring brief population blooms.

## Homes inside mountains

The native **Hamarkorar** strongly prefer underground homes. A mountain settlement may stand high above a valley while having only modest rock cover. Air, water, heat removal and access to food remain central to its design.

Twilight mountain rivers provide most industrial power. Water wheels, shafts, gears, hydraulic systems and compressed air work alongside fuel engines and limited electricity. Near-surface chambers admit light to crops; fungal cultivation uses organic feedstock supplied by the wider food web.
