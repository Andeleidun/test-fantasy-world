# The Erde–Merenval–Dverghamar stellar system

## Selected architecture and evidence status

**System A**, a wide hierarchical triple, is the selected stellar architecture for current worldbuilding. It gives Erde, Merenval and Dverghamar distinct local host stars while keeping the three systems gravitationally related. The architecture passes first-order hierarchy and circumstellar-orbit screens; it is not yet a long-duration N-body solution, a demonstrated formation history or a validated common-age stellar model.

The source comparison that introduced Systems A–I remains a historical design screen. Its rejected and alternate configurations are not parallel canon. The current Merenval ledger, the Dverghamar ledgers and scientific review, the Erde geological reconstruction and the cosmology ledger govern the worlds themselves.

## Hierarchy

Merenval orbits a warm K-type star with a working mass of about 0.75 solar masses and luminosity of about 0.35 solar luminosities. Merenval lies near 0.600 AU and completes a year in roughly 196 Erden days.

Erde orbits its own star. The Erde and Merenval host stars form the inner stellar pair at a working relative semimajor axis near 80 AU and eccentricity near 0.15. The historical architecture screen used a 0.90-solar-mass, 0.75-solar-luminosity Erde host and an approximately 310-day Erde orbit as illustrative calculation inputs. Those exact Erde stellar and calendar values have not been adopted independently by the current Erde ledgers.

Dverghamar orbits a separate early-M star. Its current bounded screening case uses 0.50 solar masses, 0.040 solar luminosities, 0.1826 AU, 1.20 times Erde's bolometric flux and a 40.30-Erden-day synchronous year. This outer stellar subsystem orbits the inner pair at a working semimajor axis near 1,200 AU and eccentricity near 0.20.

The working stellar orbital periods are about 557 years for the inner pair and 28,350 years for the outer hierarchy. These rounded values are consequences of the illustrative masses and separations, not fixed calendars or measurements.

## Stability and the visible sky

The initial screen places every inhabited planet far inside its host star's approximate circumstellar stability boundary and gives the outer-to-inner stellar separation a useful margin over a coplanar prograde hierarchy criterion. That screen does not include full secular forcing, mutual inclination, the massive Merenval companion, every planet or satellite, dissipative evolution, the birth environment or later stellar encounters.

At the nominal 1,200-AU separation, the other two stars supply only about 0.001 W/m² to Dverghamar before projection and atmospheric loss. They add negligible heat but can remain conspicuous lights in the dark sky, affecting navigation, biological timing and culture without turning the nightside into a second dayside.

## The chronology conflict

Ordinary co-formation requires the three stars and their primordial planets to be approximately the same age. Erde's current geological scaffold begins near 4.54 billion years before present, while Dverghamar's current reference case uses an 8.0-billion-year star and a native biological chronology extending beyond five billion years.

System A's selection does not silently resolve that conflict. The current records preserve both chronologies pending an explicit solution. The main ordinary options are to rebuild Dverghamar as a younger coeval world or extend and reconstruct Erde's early history within an older system. A later dynamical assembly is physically possible in principle but would require a specific uncommon energy-exchange history and survival tests for all inhabited planetary systems.

## Required validation

1. Fit all three stars to one age and composition, or define and test a mixed-age assembly history.
2. Integrate the full stellar hierarchy with the actual planets, Merenval's massive companion, important moons and mutual inclinations over the selected age.
3. Test birth-environment and later encounter survival of the approximately 1,200-AU outer orbit.
4. Carry the resulting spectra and orbital variations into each world's atmospheric, climate and biospheric models.
5. Preserve each world's separate unresolved gates: Erde U1–U4, Dverghamar's escape–climate–physiology sequence and Merenval's tidal, climate, ocean and companion-retention work.
