# Merenval: planetary and demographic reference

## Stellar and orbital model

Merenval orbits a warm K-type star of about 0.75 solar masses and 0.35 solar luminosities at about 0.600 AU, producing a year near 196 Earth days. This host belongs to the 80-AU inner pair of System A. All values remain coupled reference inputs pending common-age stellar tracks and a full system integration.

| Quantity | Merenval | Living companion |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mass | About 1.05 Earth masses | About 0.25 Earth masses |
| Radius | About 1.04 Earth radii | About 4,380 km |
| Surface gravity | Approximately Earthlike | About 0.53 g |
| Ocean coverage | About 68% | About 32% |
| Surface pressure | About 1.4 bar | About 1.75 bar |
| Oxygen target | About 0.21 bar partial pressure | About 0.21 bar partial pressure |

The worlds' center-to-center separation is about 375,000 km and mutual period about 23.2 Earth days. Merenval's illustrative 21.5-day rotation would produce a roughly 300-day apparent companion circuit. Both bodies are intended to occupy long-lived near-synchronous but asynchronous spin states if coupled atmospheric, oceanic and solid-body tides allow them.

The mutual equilibrium deformation is of order 9 m on Merenval and 30 m on the companion. These are gravitational potential scales, not coastal tide heights. Basin geometry, shelves, friction, solid deformation, the final spins and faster stellar tides control the ocean response.

## Atmosphere, ocean and relief

Merenval's connected oceans are the main buffer against long solar days. The companion relies more strongly on its denser atmosphere and has much more continental geography. The companion's large scale height keeps useful air pressure on high plateaus; exact lapse rates, clouds, convection, monsoons, deserts and dust remain model outputs.

Low gravity permits large relief but not unbounded rock forms. Major escarpments can target 1–3 km of local relief, with exceptional compound systems near 3–5 km. Great pillars may approach 0.5–1 km, exceptional broad pillars 1–2 km and rare broad buttressed forms 2–3 km.

The companion is crystal-rich, not made from giant crystals. Quartz-rich sandstone and quartzite, felsic crust, granitic plutons, pegmatites, metamorphic belts, hydrothermal veins, karst and evaporite basins create the principal exposures. Load-bearing cities use competent rock and masonry, with crystalline veneers, ribs, bridges and resonant arrays. Kilometer-high single crystals are excluded from the naturalistic baseline.

## Biosphere and Otherworld relationships

Early impact transfer is the preferred explanation for the shared deep tree of life. Which body first hosted abiogenesis remains unresolved. Later ecological exchange carries more plausible invasive, symbiotic and disease risks than exchange between wholly unrelated biospheres, but compatibility must be tested rather than assumed.

Merenval has an unusually strong relationship with nearby Otherworld ecology. A slow-precessing contact belt, or Taurid, remains a regional working model. Persistent overlap can bias ecology and geology without replacing plate tectonics or imposing rigid continent-scale oscillation.

Dorrenath is the eldest known Wayroot and the progenitor of a lineage capable of stable normal-space/Otherworld overlap. Its city-scale form is an explicit magical exception to ordinary tree biomechanics. Not every Wayroot becomes a crossing.

## Elven demographic reconstruction

Use a mean parental generation interval near 150 Earth years, with 120–180 years as the main sensitivity range. This is neither a selected maturity age nor a selected lifespan.

- Before 100,000 BP, Elves form a geographically structured Merenval metapopulation with continuing gene flow.
- Around 84,000–82,500 BP, several migration waves totaling roughly 8,000–20,000 census migrants colonize the companion. The target effective founder population is 2,000–6,000.
- Deep-cave specialization intensifies around 65,000–55,000 BP. A persistent Deep/Star metapopulation exists by roughly 55,000 BP.
- Interworld admixture recurs around 43,000–41,000 BP, 19,000–18,000 BP and 5,000–4,000 BP, with background effective migration near 0.05–0.2 reproducing migrants per generation.
- A coherent Maritime/Littoral Sylvan network develops around 38,000–32,000 BP, with local adaptation continuing from about 30,000 BP through convergence, voyaging and intermarriage rather than one founder event.

Working neutral differentiation targets are FST 0.04–0.07 for Sylvan–High, 0.05–0.08 for Sylvan–Deep, 0.015–0.035 for High–Deep and 0.005–0.02 for Maritime–inland Sylvans. They are simulation targets, not measurements, species boundaries or biological definitions of culture.

## Open gates

1. Full long-duration integration of System A including the massive companion.
2. Coupled two-body tidal evolution and acceptable orbital migration.
3. Three-dimensional climates for the selected ocean and atmosphere contrast.
4. Regional ocean response to slow mutual and faster stellar tides.
5. Companion atmospheric escape, volatile history, carbon cycle, internal heat and tectonic style.
6. Regional structural, erosional, cave and crystal-landscape models.
7. Structured population-genetic simulation preserving diversity, complete interfertility and the intended relative differentiation.
8. A decision on how frequently local Otherworld techniques permit interworld travel after the founding route diminishes.
