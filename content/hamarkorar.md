## People of the great rock masses

**Hamarkor** is the singular general native people-name; **Hamarkorar** is its plural. The components are **hamar**, crag or great rock mass, and **kor**, person.

The name includes the native dwarven peoples across their different habitats and occupations. It is not a requirement that every individual live beneath a particular kind of rock or work as a stoneworker.

**Rakkor/Rakkorar** is reserved for a regional people. **Menkor/Menkorar** remains available for another narrower local, cultural or species identity. Their exact assignments remain open. **Hamkor/Hamkorar** remains a proposed everyday contraction.

## Six limbs, four arms

The native lineage descends from a six-limbed ancestry. Two limb pairs become arms while a third supports bipedal locomotion. The evolutionary history includes changes to girdles, trunk support, breathing and manipulation; it does not simply add arms to a human torso.

Four arms chiefly support simultaneous gripping, stabilization and task sharing. They do not automatically double strength at equal body mass. The higher surface gravity also makes support, posture and movement more demanding.

## A diversified genus

The current model includes five native species and a deep-massif subspecies. Mountain, volcanic, warmer and colder settings provide different selective pressures. These adaptations arise through natural selection over deep time, not through a recent common origin alongside the Thals.

Species identity does not prescribe a language or a political boundary. Regional language traditions and breeding networks can cross community boundaries.

## Stone in early development

The preferred reproductive model places an actual egg inside a parent-built, porous mineral nursery. The egg, its biological shell, the constructed casing and the whole nursery are distinct structures.

Porous regions permit gas exchange. Honeycombing can provide useful structural support where the material and function justify it. The model still requires moisture regulation, suitable temperature, nourishment and appropriate parental care. A constructed mineral casing is not a natural quartz geode turning into an animal, and stone does not replace the embryo’s food supply.

## Culture and material knowledge

Underground residence, construction, food production and nursery care make careful material distinctions useful. Hardness is not strength; porosity does not guarantee connected pores; visually similar stones can have different composition and suitability.

The common language reflects that practical knowledge without reducing dwarven life to stoneworking. Its vocabulary also covers care, play, affection, trade, travel and ordinary household life.

## Nursery air and provisioning

A ventilated air gap separates the egg’s physiological shell from the load-bearing outer casing. Short connected gas-filled pores must remain open and sufficiently dry; a thick wet mineral wall is not automatically breathable. The egg carries organic reserves, and the hatchling leaves it small and dependent on feeding. Material preparation supports reproduction but does not replace biological development. Peak oxygen demand, water loss, waste storage and safe hatching remain separate constraints.
