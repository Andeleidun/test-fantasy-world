Purpose

This document explains how the established physical parameters can produce the present biosphere, dwarven genus, and underground civilization. It is a causal synthesis rather than a second authority list. Where it differs from the numbered canon ledger, the canon ledger governs.

## Scientific framing

The planet is not an Earth copy with one unusual climate feature. Its 3-Earth-mass rocky body, 1.53 g surface gravity, early synchronization, limited water inventory, 1.5-bar atmosphere, early-M spectrum, and permanent stellar geometry shape every later stage.

The adopted ingredients can form a candidate inhabited world, but their separate plausibility does not prove that the whole parameter combination works. Its atmosphere can retain and redistribute heat, while land, lakes, water vapor, clouds, and ice determine the actual contrast. At 1.53 g, a 1.5-bar atmosphere has close to Earth’s mass per unit surface area. The old star’s quieter present must be paired with a viable early water-retention history. Near-Earth oxygen partial pressure is useful for comparison but does not reproduce every respiratory or combustion property.

The result requires tuning, not a unique inevitable outcome. The same bulk planet could have become a stagnant-lid desert, a steam greenhouse, an ice-trapped world, or a dense-atmosphere planet with a much smaller temperature contrast. The following history is the preferred path that reaches the established endpoint without invoking a recent planetary catastrophe.

## Stage 1: Accretion, differentiation, and early synchronization

The planet accretes from refractory and volatile-bearing material within the inner system of an early-M star. Energetic impacts, radioactive heating, and metal segregation create a magma ocean. Dense metal sinks to form the core while silicates form a deep mantle.

Tidal torques can establish the selected early 1:1 spin–orbit state, preferably before the long-lived surface biosphere. This depends on initial rotation, orbit, eccentricity, rheology, and competing torques; proximity alone does not guarantee a synchronous state. The adopted history has low obliquity and low enough eccentricity for persistent climatic provinces. It contains no recent catastrophe that divides a formerly Earth-like day–night biosphere into the present extremes.

The early atmosphere is likely dominated by nebular or impact-delivered hydrogen, steam, carbon compounds, and volcanic gases. It should not be imagined as the modern nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere.

## Stage 2: Atmospheric transformation and limited-water retention

Stellar radiation and early activity remove much of the light hydrogen envelope. At the same time, hydrogen interacts with magma, iron, and water. Some oxygen produced by water photolysis is absorbed into molten or newly crystallized rock rather than accumulating in the air.

A coupled atmosphere-magma history is more credible than a single unexplained stripping event. Hydrogen escape, mantle ingassing, oxidation of iron-bearing crust, impact erosion, and volcanic outgassing gradually leave a secondary atmosphere dominated by nitrogen and carbon-bearing gases.

The present 0.15–0.20 Earth-ocean exchangeable inventory is a selected outcome of delivery, escape, impacts, mantle exchange, and chemical storage. Deep mantle water is budgeted separately. The early-M star’s pre-main-sequence luminosity and radiation may otherwise desiccate the planet, so sufficient initial supply, retention, or later outgassing must be demonstrated by the eventual history. The Thal catastrophe on Erde is unrelated.

Cooling allows water to collect in basins and the subsurface and to freeze in cold regions. The chosen dry-land endpoint requires a compatible basin distribution and substantial storage in ice or groundwater; the total inventory alone does not decide the flooded area. Persistent surface habitats need return fluxes that prevent complete cold-trap sequestration over geological time.

## Stage 3: From episodic lid to long-lived mobile-lid tectonics

Super-Earth mass does not guarantee plate tectonics. Early high temperatures can produce vigorous mantle convection while the strong outer shell remains stagnant or breaks only episodically.

The preferred transition occurs through repeated weakening. Water lowers rock strength and melting temperatures. Impacts, thermal stress, gravitational spreading, dense crustal foundering, and inherited fault networks create zones where one plate can begin to descend beneath another. Once subduction becomes persistent, recycling of water and volatiles helps maintain weak plate boundaries.

The mature regime is predominantly mobile-lid but not perfectly continuous. Global or regional reorganizations occur when plate geometry, mantle plumes, continental collisions, or volatile distribution changes.

This history produces the necessary geological diversity. Basaltic crust forms at volcanic centers. Subduction zones generate volcanic arcs and chemically evolved magmas. Large intrusive bodies crystallize into granite-like rocks. Burial, collision, heating, and deformation transform older crust into gneiss and other competent metamorphic rocks. Uplift exposes broad massif cores while faulting, caldera collapse, and crustal flexure create lake basins.

These massifs are not added merely to provide fantasy mountains. They are the predictable products of a long mobile-lid history and supply the mechanically competent, comparatively dry rock in which deep settlements are most feasible.

## Stage 4: Establishment of the synchronous climate engine

Once the secondary atmosphere and liquid-water reservoirs stabilize, the permanent stellar pattern creates a global overturning circulation. Heated dayside air rises, flows toward the nightside aloft, cools, and returns near the surface. Rotation, terrain, humidity, and pressure divide this simple ideal into jets, storm tracks, convergence zones, and regional cells.

The atmosphere transports heat, but pressure by itself does not establish that transport. Column mass, opacity, humidity, clouds, circulation, topography, and the absence of a global connected ocean interact. The specified hot dayside, cold nightside, and corridor width remain simultaneous model targets; limited water makes such a regime possible in published climate models without validating these exact numbers.

The dayside is not uniformly wet. Central lowlands become lethally hot and often arid. Persistent open water survives farther outward where stellar heating is weaker. Mountains intercept moist air and create cooler rain belts. Leeward slopes descend into closed salt basins. Rivers and groundwater connect selected provinces while other basins remain endorheic.

Nightside ice accumulates where precipitation exceeds loss. Flow, marginal melting, sublimation, and groundwater can return some of that water. Local geothermal heating, insulating ice, and pressure may permit liquid reservoirs, while salt can lower freezing temperatures at the cost of water activity. Most very cold near-surface ice and brine remain dormant or sterile habitat for ordinary biochemistry; active oases require warmer local conditions and sustainable chemical energy.

## Stage 5: Origin of life and early phototrophic experimentation

The exact origin of life remains unspecified, but suitable sites include illuminated hydrothermal shores, wet volcanic sediments, shallow mineral-rich lakes, porous basalt, and chemically active aquifers.

The early microbial biosphere can explore several phototrophic strategies. The conservative later oxygenic system uses visible and some far-red photons through two coupled photochemical stages. Known far-red photochemistry supports that possibility, but usable photon supply must be evaluated after atmospheric transmission and geometry. More serial stages, uniformly black vegetation, or efficient use of deep infrared are not required.

Primary production is concentrated where usable light, liquid water, carbon, and mineral nutrients overlap. Low-light margins can be photon-limited; other provinces can be limited by water, temperature, nitrogen, phosphorus, or trace elements. Nutrient inputs, recycling, burial, and export must be tracked rather than assigning one planet-wide limiting nutrient without evidence.

For a large fraction of early history, oxygen made by phototrophs is consumed by reduced iron, volcanic gases, dissolved minerals, and organic matter. Atmospheric oxygen rises only after these sinks diminish and net burial of reduced carbon becomes sustained.

## Stage 6: Oxygenation and macroscopic aquatic life

The chronological scaffold leaves roughly one to two billion years between the onset of sustained oxygenic photosynthesis and diverse macroscopic ecosystems. This is an allowed fictional interval, not a conservative estimate derived from an M-dwarf spectrum. Oxygen sinks, nutrient cycling, cellular innovations, ecological interactions, and contingency determine the actual path; long time alone does not guarantee complex life.

Oxygenated surface waters develop first. Deep lake waters can remain anoxic because stratification blocks exchange and sinking organic matter consumes oxygen. Coastal shelves, river mouths, shallow lakes, and illuminated rock surfaces become the principal laboratories of macroscopic evolution.

The setting allows multiple animal-grade body plans. The dwarf-bearing internal-skeleton clade is now ancestrally six-limbed. Other lineages can include radial suspension feeders, many-appendaged burrowers, colonial mats, filter structures, armored crawlers, and independently four-limbed forms. Their ecological roles remain intact; limb-count changes do not require redesigning the entire food web or assigning all large fauna to dwarf ancestry.

The largest aquatic consumers evolve in productive surface and coastal waters. High gravity matters less in water because buoyancy supports mass, permitting common animals of 2–5 m and rare forms of 1–3 tonnes even while similarly large terrestrial forms remain costly.

## Stage 7: Origin of the six-limbed clade

The dwarf-bearing clade begins within the established oxygenated shallow-water macroscopic biosphere. A bilateral animal with an internal axial support develops three repeated pairs of lateral appendages: anterior, middle, and posterior. A plausible developmental route uses repeated appendage-producing fields followed by regional specialization, without requiring a sudden extra pair on a mature four-limbed body plan. Selection in structurally complex lake shallows and flowing channels favors station-holding, steering, bottom clinging, and food handling. Each pair provides useful functions before bearing terrestrial weight.

This is an inferred alien evolutionary pathway. Experimental crustacean work demonstrates that developmental regulation can alter the identities of serial appendages, but does not demonstrate the origin of six-limbed endoskeletal animals. Evidence analogue: [serial appendage specialization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2720850/).

Terrestrialization occurs through wet margins rather than a leap onto dry plains. The established basin-forming tectonics, river networks, lake margins, flood channels, and intermittently flooded caves supply shallow-water and partially buoyant intermediate habitats. Reinforced fins become increasingly useful for pushing, clinging, and crossing short exposed stretches. These environments reduce the immediate weight-bearing challenge of 1.53 g; they do not uniquely favor exactly six limbs.

The three fin pairs become six jointed, load-bearing limbs with their own girdles and muscular attachments. Initially all three pairs contribute to support and movement, while the anterior pair increasingly explores substrates and handles food. Axial muscles, ventilation, circulation, kidneys, skin barriers, and sensory coordination evolve alongside locomotion. Spinal or equivalent local motor circuits coordinate the limbs; their exact arrangement need not copy insects or require three discrete human-like spinal enlargements. Increasingly under-body support can reduce sustained muscular bending loads as animals become larger.

An enclosed embryo that can develop without standing external water must arise before radiation into dry uplands; it still requires retained water and adequate exchange surfaces. Simpler enclosed egg laying is the working ancestral route, with brood-geode incubation proposed as a much later specialization in the dwarf-parent lineage. The current limb decision does not settle all reproductive anatomy. Early forms must reproduce successfully through inherited physiology and behavior, without advanced construction, sapience, or an already complete brood geode.

## Stage 8: Terrestrial and cavern radiations

Once reproduction is independent of open water, the six-limbed clade radiates through outer-dayside wetlands, twilight vegetation, mountain valleys, talus fields, caves, and cooler uplands. Its descendants remain supported directly or indirectly by photosynthesis; six limbs do not remove the energy limits of natural deep caves.

High gravity constrains body size, limb leverage, and unsupported spans, while ecological diversity permits low-slung grazers, browsers, diggers, gorge climbers, and small leaping or gliding species. Six-legged walkers, two-handed four-legged foragers, and the later four-handed bipeds can diverge within this radiation. One limb pair could become wings while two pairs remain walking legs. Such a flyer need not occupy another phylum, but the identity of the wing pair, takeoff mechanics, skeletal integration, and flight-energy budget remain separate open questions. Four-limbed fauna can also occur independently or through secondary reduction.

The planet’s ecological provinces remain repeatedly divided by uplift, aridification, salt basins, volcanic destruction, glacier advance, river capture, and shifts in the wet corridor. These barriers create speciation opportunities while occasional reconnection prevents every province from becoming completely isolated.

The nightward aphotic fringe is a subsidy ecosystem. Rivers, groundwater, winds, migrating animals, spores, and detritus carry photosynthetic energy beyond direct light. Local geothermal chemistry adds energy but does not replace the illuminated biosphere at regional scale.

## Stage 9: The dwarf-parent clade

Within the existing provisional 80–200-million-year interval, a compact endothermic branch becomes strongly associated with stable dens, broken mountain terrain, and cool-to-variable conditions. Long-lived mobile-lid tectonics has produced successive volcanic arcs, uplifted intrusive and metamorphic massifs, eroding cliffs, talus, and river gorges. The lineage follows these recurring habitat types over time; no single cave or mountain range must survive for the whole interval.

Climbing and extractive foraging differentiate the limb pairs. The front pair reaches, probes, and manipulates food; the middle pair retains powerful gripping and support functions; the hind pair increasingly supplies propulsion and weight-bearing. Early ancestors can feed with two hands while the other four limbs maintain support. These advantages precede sapience and brood-geode engineering.

Endothermy supports activity across cool regions and dark shelters but raises food demand. The lineage therefore remains tied to productive surface-linked habitats. Social denning, food caching, cooperative defense, and repeated use of stable shelters favor memory, communication, and recognition of complex spatial networks.

Sensory evolution is multimodal. Low-light eyes remain useful at cave entrances, on illuminated slopes, during artificial-light use, and in surface travel. Hearing, smell, tactile feedback, and substrate vibration become unusually important. No stage requires total darkness or loss of eyes.

A shorter heart-to-brain distance, effective venous return, broad joints, strong tendons, and load-responsive bone help manage high gravity. The two anterior girdles become staggered arm girdles on a deep, moderately elongated thorax, with distinct muscular attachments and load paths. A dedicated ventilatory pump must remain effective while both pairs brace or pull; separated pulmonary and systemic circulation is the conservative starting model, without requiring extra hearts or an elaborate avian-style lung. The pelvis, lower spine, feet, and relatively extended stance bear the increased upper-body load. Lower-hand contact pads protect fine digits during occasional support. Exact geometry and mass distribution still require modeling.

## Stage 10: Manipulation, bipedality, and cumulative culture

Between roughly 12 and 6 million years before the present, the selected cave-and-gorge branch becomes increasingly upright during supported climbing, feeding, and short-distance transport. The middle limbs can brace against adjacent stone while the hindlimbs bear weight and the anterior hands work. Secure ledges and food processing near the trunk also reward intermittent release of the middle pair, giving four manipulators for gripping, stripping, breaking, and positioning objects.

Two hands with four walking legs would already be a successful alternative, so freeing a first hand pair does not by itself explain four-armed bipedality. The proposed additional advantage is flexible task division among two manipulative pairs in a climbing, extractive-foraging niche. Habitual unsupported bipedality evolves only where those benefits outweigh balance and energy costs. Modern tools later exploit this anatomy; future tool use is not its initial evolutionary cause. Evidence analogue: [hand-assisted bipedality](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/origin-of-human-bipedalism-as-an-adaptation-for-locomotion-on-fle/).

Habitual bipedality emerges only after pelvic, spinal, foot, balance, and muscle-leverage changes make it workable under 1.53 g. Adults normally use relatively extended legs on suitable ground. The anterior pair now forms the upper precision-biased arms, and the middle pair forms the lower power-and-bracing arms. Both pairs retain dexterity and useful strength. Lower hands supplement support during climbing, hauling, scrambling, or balance recovery; sufficiently crouched or rock-supported positions allow all six extremities to contact support. Six-point support is not the normal standing posture and does not require equal-length arms.

Patchy resources and recurring hazards reward cooperation. Flood timing, rockfall, toxic gas, unstable ice, dangerous lake water, and volcanic episodes cannot be managed by individual instinct alone. Long juvenile learning, teaching, shared route memory, and cumulative technology become decisive.

Nutritious foods and simple mechanical processing can support early brain evolution before reliable fire. Aquatic foods, seeds or storage tissues, pounding, and other accessible resources need their own foraging returns. Fermentation and environmental cooking may become local additions when useful and reproducible, rather than being assumed complete technological substitutes everywhere. Controlled-draft combustion develops within the actual fuel and atmospheric limits.

Habitual bipedality and cumulative tools appear within the existing provisional 3–4-million-year interval, followed by a recognizable dwarven genus roughly 2.5–3.5 million years before the present. All five species and the deep-massif subspecies inherit the six-limbed foundation. Uplift, river capture, closed arid basins, glacier movements, and intermittent valley connections continue to shape their divergence; the Thal migration is far later and unrelated to the origin of this anatomy.

A prolonged dependent juvenile phase supports learning four-hand coordination through play, imitation, and progressively demanding movement. In the preferred brood-geode reconstruction, a true yolk-provisioned egg develops within an externally constructed mineral nursery. A small altricial hatchling reduces the amount of growth required before emergence; parents then supply digestible organic food or evolved nutritive secretions. Smooth grips and graded supports are used only after the juvenile can control its movements. Delicate respiratory pores are not climbing holes. Egg passage, peak gas conductance, moisture loss, waste storage, and safe hatching remain independent constraints.

Four arms add task-sharing and grip redundancy, not free power. Increased muscle and supporting tissue can raise body mass, food demand, oxygen delivery, and waste-heat requirements. Retain the photosynthesis-based food web, but recalculate individual and settlement resource budgets once stature and mass are chosen. Exact girdle geometry, limb proportions, and metabolic performance remain hypotheses to test, not consequences already proved by the evolutionary narrative.

## Stage 11: Radiation of the dwarven genus

The genus spreads through connected mountain systems during favorable wet intervals. Later uplift, expanding salt basins, ice barriers, volcanic arcs, and contraction of river corridors split populations.

The central-massif lineage remains the broadest ecological generalist. A population repeatedly exploiting connected near-surface aquifers and caverns becomes the deep-massif subspecies. Its divergence is recent enough for full fertility with the central form and must not imply independent evolution in closed kilometre-deep caves.

A warm-climate branch divides after geographic isolation. The sunward species adapts to dry heat and water scarcity. The volcanic-range sister species occupies humid hot arcs, ash-prone valleys, and chemically stressful waters. Its physiology handles chronic heat, salts, particulates, and some low-dose toxins better than that of other dwarves, but lethal gases and major eruptions remain beyond biological protection.

A cold-upland branch also divides. The nightward species evolves near the cold but still photosynthetically supported edge, where insulation and heat conservation matter. The high-mountain species evolves at altitude in naturally unpressurized air, selecting for efficient oxygen uptake and embryonic development.

Most living species diverge within roughly the last 0.5–2 million years. The deep-massif subspecies differentiates within roughly 150–400 thousand years. These intervals allow pronounced adaptation without requiring six completely isolated, ancient genera.

Occasional hybridization after secondary contact is a plausible model, not a requirement for realism. Close ancestry permits but does not guarantee fertility. Persistent species boundaries require adequate geographic, ecological, behavioral, or genetic barriers, and the direction and sex-specific consequences of crosses remain open. The central/deep-massif relationship is retained as the freely fertile subspecies pair.

## Stage 12: Agricultural and industrial transition

Cultivation begins in sheltered valleys, cave mouths, terraces, and light wells. Native geophytes become the functional analogues of potato-like crops. Photosynthetic leaves remain in illuminated zones while cool, protected storage tissues grow below.

Fungi become major cultivated foods and recyclers only after dwarves can supply reduced organic feedstock. They convert crop residues, wood-like tissues, dung, spoiled food, and waste. They cannot replace illuminated primary production.

Industrial mining, pumps, ventilation, rail, drilling, metalworking, surveying, sanitation, and reliable power permit deeper settlement. Late-nineteenth- to early-twentieth-century Earth industry is a useful capability analogy, not proof that all historical mines could house permanent cities. The deepest districts require unusually cool sites or effective refrigeration, water control, modest supported spans, escape routes, and maintainable food and power systems. Temperature and heat rejection, rather than physical strength, commonly set the residential limit.

Electricity is useful but not universally necessary. Hydropower can drive shafts, compressors, pumps, mills, cable systems, and hydraulic networks directly. Fuel engines can likewise provide mechanical power without first generating electricity. Selective electrification becomes especially valuable for communication, control systems, rail traction, pumping redundancy, safer distributed lighting, and emergencies.

Deep settlements depend on imported food, ventilation, clean water, drainage, heat rejection, power, and spare parts. Rock temperature depends on surface history, local heat flow, groundwater, and three-dimensional topography. As an illustration, a 10°C surface with a 20 K/km gradient gives about 50°C rock at 2 km before excavation and cooling. Such a site requires substantial heat removal. Permanently hot deep-dayside rock cannot be kept cool indefinitely by shade or ordinary ventilation alone.

## Stage 13: Present geographic range

Most dwarves live beneath the mountain and valley systems of the habitable corridor, especially where rivers, competent rock, ore, agricultural light, and transport routes overlap.

Dayward travel retains a potential local light source; nightward travel increasingly requires food imports. This explains the ecological asymmetry but does not fix maximum settlement distances. Elevation and shade can improve dayward surface conditions, while shallow aquifers or imported cooling may support selected subsurface sites. Descending into rock is not inherently cooling on a permanently hot hemisphere. Nightside cold can be insulated against, but power, accessible water, and long supply routes still constrain outposts.

Underground preference moves housing, workshops, transport, storage, governance, and much agriculture below ground. Surface installations are accepted where they provide an unambiguous advantage: capturing river head, admitting sunlight, observing weather or astronomy, reaching routes, extracting shallow resources, exhausting heat, or defending an entrance.

Remote nightside strongholds 3,000–4,000 km from the terminator are therefore small, strategic, and supplied. Inner mountain cities above surrounding lowlands are much larger because they remain within the broader river, food, and transport economy.

## Stage 14: The later Thal arrival

The Thals arrive only after the native planet, biosphere, dwarven genus, species radiation, agriculture, and much of dwarven civilization already exist.

The Thals are Neanderthal-descended refugees from Erde. About five millennia allow substantial cultural change, founder effects, and selection on some traits, without guaranteeing a new species or comprehensive physiological redesign. The local gravity, oxygen, food, pathogens, and reproduction must permit survival from arrival onward; evolution cannot retroactively rescue an initially nonviable founding population.

Their later ecological effects depend on actual compatibility. Independently evolved native organisms are not automatically edible to Thals, and microbes need not infect unrelated hosts simply because they are new. Keep broad molecular compatibility, food processing or provisioning, and any organism transfers as explicit conditions for later contact work. This review does not invent an Erde–Dwarven-planet origin connection or redesign the migration.

## Integrated food-web logic

The planet’s biosphere is best understood as an energy gradient.

Illuminated wet provinces supply most new chemical energy through photosynthesis. Rivers, winds, organisms, and groundwater export a fraction; much is respired or buried before reaching distant consumers. Fungi and detritivores convert a diminishing organic stream rather than recovering all its energy. Productive oxygenated lake zones support the largest aquatic forms. Caves intercept local subsidies, while geothermal oases support limited chemosynthesis only where appropriate donors and acceptors continually meet.

This architecture makes the setting coherent. It also sets hard limits. A large city, predator, forest, or herd far into permanent night must have an identified energy subsidy. A kilometre-deep ecosystem must have an identified oxygen, nutrient, and waste pathway. A fungal staple system must identify its organic feedstock. A large lake animal must occupy sufficiently oxygenated and productive water.

## Scientific stress test

### Strongly plausible elements

The mass–radius choice, tidal synchronization, water-limited climates, known photochemical mechanisms, compact load-bearing anatomy, industrial mining techniques, local chemosynthesis, and geographically structured evolution provide useful empirical or theoretical analogues. They support individual components, with different uncertainties. Neither extraterrestrial life nor the complete four-armed dwarf system has been observed, and deep residential cities are a stronger engineering claim than mines.

### Plausible but tuned elements

Maintaining both a 1.5-bar atmosphere and the desired extreme hemispheric temperatures requires a particular greenhouse, humidity, cloud, surface-water, and circulation regime. Long-lived mobile-lid tectonics on a super-Earth is possible but uncertain. Sustaining near-Earth oxygen partial pressure under an M-dwarf spectrum requires long biological time and adequate nutrient cycling.

Five closely related dwarf species plus one subspecies are credible only if geographic isolation is real and recurrent. Large lakes and large aquatic animals are credible only in productive, oxygenated upper waters.

### Elements that would break credibility

Credibility would be undermined by treating target temperatures as a solved climate, deriving tidal locking from Mercury-like distance alone, assuming all moons survive because they lie inside the Hill sphere, giving dark forests no energy source, counting fungal feedstock twice, or using muscle strength to solve underground heat. A recent locking event, a wetter world, or higher carbon dioxide is not inherently forbidden by physics; it would require a different coupled history and could conflict with this chosen endpoint. The Thal migration cannot explain native dwarf ancestry.

## Recommended next validation work

First, quantify the preferred provisional brood-geode model using the real egg, the constructed casing, and dependent post-hatching care as separate physiological stages.

Second, select a consistent stellar model, incident flux, orbit, and atmosphere and test the temperature, hydrology, and corridor targets together. A simplified energy-balance calculation can screen gross inconsistencies; it cannot validate terrain-scale habitability or replace three-dimensional atmosphere–ice–water modeling.

Third, define moon and companion-body masses and orbits, then perform long-term N-body integrations and tidal-evolution checks.

Fourth, establish the principal producer body plans and the first major animal phyla. This will determine what “forest,” “grassland,” reef, burrower, flyer, grazer, predator, and crop actually mean on this planet.

Fifth, specify dwarf stature, body mass, respiratory architecture, skin covering, digits, and reproduction before designing species-level appearance.
