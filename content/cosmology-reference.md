## Established cosmological foundation

- Coherent reality exists in universe bubbles. Each universe is a stabilizing pattern embedded between the skins of unending primordial chaos and constant order.

- A universe bubble is not simply a planet, solar system, or region floating inside ordinary space. It is an entire coherent reality maintained within a deeper cosmological relationship among order, chaos, and the possibility of nonexistence.

- Absolute Void exists outside primordial chaos. It is not empty space, darkness, an elemental plane, or an unexplored region of a universe.

- Primordial chaos is the only part of reality capable of surviving contact with Absolute Void because chaos never depends on retaining one stable form. Survival does not imply immunity, control, intention, or safety.

- Absolute Void does not touch each universe. It is equally close to every universe bubble in an ontological or topological sense that has not yet been fully defined.

- The Howling Void is a mortal or cultural name for the terrifying boundary vacuum near Absolute Void. It tears at and shreds coherent reality rather than merely causing reality to weaken.

- The Howling Void operates as an event horizon. Once something crosses fully into it, no physical route leads back. It is continually drawn toward Absolute Void while remaining unable ever to reach Absolute Void itself.

- The exact status of constant order, primordial chaos, and the skins between them remains unresolved. Their descriptions may eventually require distinctions among literal structure, useful metaphor, divine interpretation, and cultural myth.

## Decision 1: Otherworld ontology and cosmic topology

- The Otherworld is a coherent liminal ecology shaped by nearby coherent worlds. It possesses environments, patterns, and relationships of its own rather than existing only as a transit map or collection of portals.

- The Otherworld is tangent to all of normal space and extends beyond it. Tangency describes a potential relationship of adjacency across normal locations, not a strict one-to-one spatial overlay.

- Nearness within the Otherworld is governed jointly by resonance among places, ecologies, histories, and meanings, and by astronomical distance and alignment. Resonance can shape which routes are available without generally erasing cosmic scale.

- Long distances in normal space therefore tend to remain long distances in the Otherworld. Cross-planet travel can exist without making planets routinely or conveniently adjacent.

- Otherworld locations near a planet do not always have strict local relationships defined by that planet’s ordinary geography. The exact degree and consequences of this local distortion remain unresolved.

- A location or path near a planet is as cosmologically distant from the Howling Void as the planet itself, which is approximately as far from the Howling Void as any location can be. This is a statement about cosmological shelter rather than ordinary measurable distance.

- The Otherworld is a child reality more closely akin to primordial chaos than to constant order. It stretches through primordial chaos rather than merely lying beside ordinary space inside a coherent universe.

- Paths can in principle extend beyond the known universe and into primordial chaos, but such routes lie entirely outside every established body of knowledge in the present setting.

- Travel in those regions requires a mental pattern capable of coherent thought while being overwhelmed by primordial chaos and a physical pattern or power capable of remaining coherent while spread across non-strictly local space.

- No known ordinary traveler satisfies both requirements. The possibility of such travel does not make it accessible, mapped, survivable, or understood.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns

- This decision supersedes the earlier unresolved alternatives in which the Otherworld might primarily be a single parallel universe, a network of independent pocket realms, or an artificial transit realm. Such ideas may survive as cultural explanations or describe limited local phenomena.

- Fixed gates, traveler intention, divine paths, and constructed roads may still influence particular crossings, but resonance and astronomical relationship are now the principal foundations of Otherworld nearness.

- The exact process by which a planet shapes nearby Otherworld ecology remains unresolved, including whether the relationship is primarily physical, biological, magical, historical, cultural, or reciprocal.

- The creation, persistence, movement, and closure of entrances remain unresolved, as do the requirements for perceiving and navigating a route.

- It remains unresolved whether interplanetary journeys cross long corridors between world-shaped regions, pass through less sheltered liminal environments, depend on rare alignments, or combine these mechanisms.

- The effects of Otherworld travel on time, causality, matter, bodily coherence, souls, memory, and magic remain unresolved.

- Deep Otherworld, direct exposure to primordial chaos, the Howling Void, and Absolute Void must remain distinct concepts. How increasing distance from a coherent world changes the relationship among them is not yet established.

- It remains unresolved whether the Otherworld can connect separate universe bubbles, even though it extends beyond the known universe into primordial chaos.

## Decision 2: Environmental authorship, depth, and nonlocal routes

- The Otherworld is normally the primary author of its own ecology. Nearby planets influence it, but most Otherworld regions do not strongly resemble adjacent physical locations in normal space.

- Geology, climate, life, and magic contribute a transformed ecological echo rather than a geographic copy. Planetary influence changes what can develop nearby without dictating a corresponding landscape.

- Divine activity and major magical events can impose lasting regional changes on the Otherworld. These exceptional imprints coexist with environments produced by the Otherworld’s own chaotic nature.

- Paths beyond strong gravitational fields are usually in continual motion. Routes, adjacencies, and apparent distances can shift even when the traveler’s immediate surroundings appear locally coherent.

- Strong gravitational fields are associated with greater local stability, but the exact mechanism and the parts of Otherworld reality that gravity stabilizes remain unresolved.

- Some planets have exceptionally strong and stable relationships with the Otherworld. The elven planet is a known example, but this degree of correspondence and stability is not normal.

- Movement away from world-shaped regions has a broad tendency toward increasing mutability and nonlocality, but it is not a single uniform or monotonic gradient.

- Deep Otherworld can contain self-sustaining coherent ecologies that do not depend on a nearby planet. These stable regions interrupt the broader gradient, and transitions between regions may differ radically.

- The Otherworld has no strong ordinary relationship with the Howling Void. Appreciable proximity to the Howling Void requires passage beyond the known universe into primordial chaos and toward its furthest reaches.

- Ordinary Otherworld instability and nonlocality are therefore not evidence of Void exposure. Primordial chaos, deep Otherworld, the Howling Void, and Absolute Void remain distinct.

- An apparently straight path through the Otherworld can have a completely nonlinear relationship with exact locations in normal space. Different regions can connect nonlocally, making some routes far shorter or longer than expected.

- Nonlocal connections are highly chaotic and generally unreliable for deliberate travel. The earlier rule that long normal-space distances tend to remain long still applies as a statistical tendency rather than an absolute law.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 2

- Tangency to normal space does not mean a fixed overlay. It means that Otherworld locations can have relationships with normal-space locations even when their respective geometries do not correspond.

- Resonance and astronomical distance remain relevant, but they operate within a topology whose actual connections can shift. Their exact influence over the probability, duration, and destination of nonlocal links remains unresolved.

- The origin, persistence, and internal laws of self-sustaining deep Otherworld ecologies remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether gravity directly stabilizes topology, strengthens the influence of coherent matter, anchors entrances, or correlates with another stabilizing property.

- It remains unresolved whether the elven planet’s exceptional relationship is natural, cultivated, divine, historically accumulated, or some combination.

- The exact boundary between deep Otherworld and direct primordial-chaos exposure remains unresolved, as does whether any anomalous route can bypass part of the expected distance.

## Decision 3: Gravitational stability, probabilistic topology, and autonomous ecologies

- Strong gravitational fields principally stabilize Otherworld topology and persistence. Routes and adjacencies change more slowly near them, but this does not force Otherworld landscapes to resemble the corresponding locations in normal space.

- Gravitational stability can make local travel conditions more persistent without establishing that gravity creates crossings, tames the ecology, or fixes every route.

- Astronomical distance supplies the baseline probability for a connection. Resonance biases which shortcuts or adjacencies are possible, while the Otherworld’s chaos determines the exact connection that actually forms.

- Resonance therefore changes probability rather than guaranteeing an outcome. A strong relationship between places can favor a shortcut without making that shortcut reliable, permanent, safe, or repeatable.

- Deep Otherworld ecologies can originate as self-maintaining attractor patterns arising from chaos.

- Inhabitants and ecosystems can collectively reinforce the coherence of a deep ecology through their continuing relationships and cycles.

- Powerful magical or divine anchors can create or sustain coherent regions.

- A surviving imprint or fragment of a lost world, civilization, or major cosmic event can also become the foundation of a deep ecology.

- These sustaining mechanisms are compatible and may overlap. Different regions can have radically different origins without requiring one universal explanation.

- Once an Otherworld ecology has formed and remains capable of sustaining itself, its continued survival does not depend on the survival or condition of tangent locations in normal space.

- Changes to a tangent planet may alter the ecology’s influences, routes, or relationships, but they do not automatically destroy an already self-sustaining Otherworld ecology.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 3

- The Otherworld can preserve autonomous living regions after their former tangent worlds, ecosystems, or civilizations have changed or disappeared. This is a possible history for some regions, not a universal origin.

- The exact mechanism by which gravity stabilizes topology, the range and falloff of that effect, and how multiple gravitational fields interact remain unresolved.

- The thresholds by which a chaotic region becomes a self-maintaining attractor or a population begins reinforcing local coherence remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved what happens when a deep ecology can no longer sustain itself, including whether it dissolves, becomes increasingly chaotic, migrates, fragments, or transforms.

- The observability and predictability of resonance-biased shortcuts remain unresolved. A probability rule does not yet provide travelers with a reliable navigation method.

- Entrances between normal space and the Otherworld, their destinations, and the conditions under which travelers and material can pass remain unresolved.

## Decision 4: Crossing formation, local endpoint stability, and navigation

- Crossings between normal space and the Otherworld can arise at naturally thin places or during recurring alignments.

- Deliberate magic or ritual can open or prepare a boundary, but this capability is rare and requires at least an expert practitioner.

- Otherworld beings or ecologies can open a crossing from the Otherworld side.

- Extreme physical, magical, or divine events can tear open the boundary. These crossings need not resemble deliberate or naturally recurring thresholds in stability or safety.

- The four mechanisms can overlap. A naturally thin location may be deliberately activated, an Otherworld inhabitant may cooperate with a practitioner, or an extreme event may permanently alter later crossing behavior.

- When a crossing repeatedly opens at the same normal-space location within a strong gravitational field, it usually enters the same local Otherworld region.

- This local endpoint stability does not extend automatically to onward travel. Routes and adjacencies beyond the entrance region may continue to shift.

- Trained travelers can navigate by perceiving resonance, topology, and ecological changes.

- Native beings and cultivated relationships with local Otherworld ecologies can provide guidance that abstract knowledge alone cannot.

- Divination, instruments, and specialized magic can estimate or predict probable connections, but probability does not guarantee the route that chaos will produce.

- Maintained roads, anchored waypoints, and constructed stabilizers have not yet been established as standard navigation methods.

## Decision 5: Perception, material passage, and crossing duration

- Magically or otherwise unusually sensitive people can perceive a naturally occurring crossing before most others, including while it is latent, partial, or beginning to open.

- Once a threshold is fully open, it can become visible and usable to ordinary people nearby. Magical sensitivity is not an absolute requirement for passage through an already open crossing.

- A sufficiently stable crossing can carry ordinary bodies, animals, and cargo intact without requiring every traveler to possess or receive individual magic.

- Physical accessibility does not make the passage safe. Traveling through the Otherworld is highly dangerous and is never a decision to be made lightly.

- The crossing’s origin principally determines how long it remains usable.

- Natural crossings tied to alignments or recurring conditions remain open according to those conditions and may reopen when they recur.

- Deliberately opened crossings require continued magical or ritual support. Because deliberate opening already requires at least an expert, maintaining one can impose a major practical bottleneck.

- Crossings torn open by extreme events decay, reshape, or close according to the damage and surrounding conditions rather than following a universal duration.

- A crossing opened from the Otherworld side can be closed from that side by the responsible being or ecology when it retains the ability to do so.

- Hybrid crossings can inherit several duration rules at once. A ritual might exploit a natural alignment, or an extreme event might leave a location more likely to reopen later.

- These rules permit the physical migration of a large population, including the Thals, without making mass interplanetary travel ordinary, safe, or easily repeatable.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 5

- It remains unresolved how much of the danger lies in crossing the threshold itself and how much arises from travel, ecology, navigation, and possible stranding after entry.

- The scale, stability, and duration required to move large groups, animals, supplies, and equipment remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether a closing threshold gives warning, whether travelers can hold it open from either side, and what happens to anything crossing at the moment of closure.

- The degree to which an ordinary traveler can retrace a route without a sensitive guide remains unresolved.

- The practical survival requirements for an Otherworld expedition remain unresolved.

## Decision 6: Ordinary hazards, temporal instability, and retreat

- Ordinary Otherworld travel exposes travelers to several independent hazards. Its danger is not reducible to the Void, and most of these hazards have no Void relationship.

- Routes can shift, destinations can change, and travelers can become stranded.

- Inhabitants may be predatory, territorial, deceptive, indifferent, or dangerous without being evil or Void-related.

- Unfamiliar climates, terrain, diseases, toxins, and ecological relationships can be debilitating or lethal.

- Otherworld nonlocality can destabilize bodies, minds, memory, or magic.

- Protective preparation is highly advisable, and magical protection more so. Neither makes a journey safe.

- Near strong gravitational fields, local time usually feels comparatively ordinary. Nonlinear routes can still produce unexpected differences between time experienced during travel and time elapsed elsewhere.

- Outside strong gravitational fields, time is appreciably less linear and stable. Temporal distortion there is part of ordinary risk rather than merely a rare anomaly.

- Exact temporal effects remain unresolved. Backward travel, paradoxes, and history-changing causation are not established.

- The crossing used for entry remains locally two-way while it is open.

- Closure or topological movement can eliminate the return path, even when travelers intended to retreat through the same threshold.

- Immediate retreat is therefore reliable only while the entry remains open and locally adjacent. The window can be lost through crossing duration or changing Otherworld topology.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 6

- Otherworld danger must remain conceptually separate from the dangers of primordial chaos and from Void exposure.

- The capabilities, preparation requirements, duration, cost, and failure modes of protective magic remain unresolved.

- The usual manifestations and hard causal limits of temporal instability remain unresolved.

- The onset, severity, reversibility, and treatment of bodily, mental, memory, and magical destabilization remain unresolved.

- Travelers still need practical ways to recognize that a route, crossing, or shared temporal frame is becoming unstable.

## Decision 7: Practitioner-dependent protection and the temporal gradient

- Magical protection can potentially stabilize a traveler’s body, mind, memory, and magic against Otherworld nonlocality.

- It can potentially buffer unfamiliar climate, toxins, disease, and hostile ecological relationships.

- It can help a group remain mutually located, resist topological separation, and preserve a shared frame of travel.

- It can conceal travelers from dangerous inhabitants or help deter and defend against them.

- Which protective functions are available depends entirely on the practitioner. No universal protection provides all of these functions, and physical protection alone cannot substitute for magical protection in the most unstable regions.

- Within most Otherworld regions gravitationally bound to a star, the primary temporal effect is unequal elapsed time between travelers and the normal-space locations they left or later reach.

- More severe temporal effects begin as a gradient near the effective edge of stellar gravitational binding. Provisionally, this is the region where an object sharing the star’s general inertial frame ceases to be gravitationally bound to that star and would no longer need to escape it in order to depart.

- Near and beyond that transition, travel without magical protection is almost certainly lethal regardless of the quality of physical protection.

- As stellar gravitational shelter weakens, travelers may experience lost, repeated, or subjectively reordered intervals; different members of one group may experience different durations; and aging, healing, spell duration, or biological cycles may fall out of sync.

- Temporal instability becomes stronger in intergalactic space, near astronomical cosmic voids, and toward the outer edges of the Otherworld’s relationship with known universes.

- Astronomical cosmic voids remain structures within coherent universes. Their relevance to temporal instability does not make them instances of Absolute Void or the Howling Void.

- Temporal instability can distort rate, duration, synchronization, and experienced sequence, but no traveler can arrive in normal space before their departure. It does not provide history-changing travel into the past.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 7

- Protective magic describes a family of functions rather than one standardized spell or school. An expedition may require several practitioners or traditions to cover different hazards.

- The effective boundary of stellar gravitational shelter needs a more precise setting-level definition, especially for multiple-star systems, overlapping gravitational fields, rogue worlds, and galactic gravity.

- The onset and severity of each temporal effect across the gradient remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved how unequal aging, healing, spell duration, biological cycles, memory, and magical instability reconcile after travelers return to a more stable region.

- It remains unresolved whether a group can restore a shared temporal frame after its members begin experiencing different durations.

- The costs, duration, failure modes, and practitioner-specific limits of each protective function remain unresolved.

- The prohibition against arrival before departure is a standing causal ceiling unless a later explicit decision supersedes it.

## Decision 8: Protective architectures, temporal predictability, and persistent consequences

- Three distinct foundations for Otherworld protection are currently valid: reinforcement of each traveler’s living self-pattern, binding a group or vessel into one coherent frame, and protective relationships with Otherworld beings or ecologies.

- These foundations can overlap, but none is universal. The practitioner’s abilities, knowledge, relationships, and limitations determine which foundation can be used and which hazards it can address.

- Individual reinforcement helps a traveler preserve bodily, mental, mnemonic, and magical coherence without making that traveler immune to environmental or inhabited dangers.

- Group or vessel binding establishes a shared coherent frame for people, animals, cargo, and the immediate traveling environment. Its exact inclusion rules and failure behavior remain unresolved.

- Relational protection works through an Otherworld being, community, or ecology rather than through the practitioner’s power alone. The terms, reliability, range, and degree of reciprocity can vary.

- Anchoring travelers to a normal-space place or object has not been selected as a general protective architecture. It remains unestablished rather than categorically impossible.

- Within star-bound Otherworld regions, skilled travelers can estimate broad ranges of elapsed-time difference.

- Exact temporal ratios cannot be selected or guaranteed. Predictions remain probabilistic and can be altered by the route, conditions, and travelers involved.

- Elapsed-time estimation therefore supports planning but does not create a precise clock, scheduled temporal shortcut, or reliable method of choosing how far into the future a traveler will emerge.

- When temporally desynchronized travelers return to a sufficiently stable region, active distortion ends and ongoing biological and magical cycles normalize.

- Time already lived remains real. Aging, healing, injuries, resource use, memories, experiences, and other physical consequences are not reversed merely because the traveler has returned.

- Normalization restores stable forward operation rather than erasing the journey or forcing travelers back to the age and condition they would have possessed without it.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 8

- The costs, preparation, duration, coverage, and characteristic failure modes of each protective architecture remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether living self-pattern reinforcement uses exactly the same underlying pattern recognized by transformation magic or only a related principle.

- Group and vessel binding requires rules for consent, inclusion, leadership, cargo, animals, births, deaths, separation, and partial failure.

- Relational protection requires decisions about communication, reciprocity, obligation, territorial authority, and what happens when a relationship is withdrawn or misunderstood.

- The knowledge and instruments needed to produce broad elapsed-time estimates, and the width of those ranges, remain unresolved.

- Stable return stops continuing temporal distortion, but severe bodily, mental, mnemonic, or magical damage acquired during exposure may still require treatment.

- The cultural, legal, religious, and political consequences of travelers returning after substantially different elapsed times remain to be developed.

## Decision 9: Native life, coherence strategies, and ecological magic

- The Otherworld supports at least two genuinely native origins for living beings: evolution within sufficiently stable ecologies and direct emergence of living patterns from chaotic ecological processes.

- In stable Otherworld ecologies, populations can reproduce, vary, adapt, and diversify across generations. The exact biological or nonbiological mechanisms can differ by region and need not reproduce Erde’s genetics.

- Some living patterns arise directly from the Otherworld’s chaos once ecological conditions can sustain them. They require neither normal-space ancestry nor deliberate creation.

- Emergent Otherworld life is not thereby a Void entity, a bodach, a disguised god, or evidence of the Void’s influence. It arises within the Otherworld’s own natural processes.

- Evolved and directly emergent beings can inhabit the same ecologies and influence one another. Whether they can interbreed, transform into one another, or become indistinguishable over time remains unresolved.

- Normal-space migrants adapting across generations and deliberate creation by gods, powerful magic, or major events were not selected as general explanations for native life. Particular cases remain possible only if established later.

- Native beings use several different means of remaining coherent as surroundings shift. No single mechanism governs every species, culture, or individual.

- Some possess an internal living pattern adapted to tolerate local instability.

- Some depend partly on relationships with other beings and with their ecosystems, which collectively reinforce individual coherence.

- Some are sustained by a home territory, ecological relationship, or resonance and may become less stable when separated from it.

- Different kinds of beings can combine these mechanisms or rely on fundamentally different mechanisms.

- Magic among Otherworld-native beings often develops through ecological relationships rather than through one universal form of spellcraft.

- Some beings perceive or manipulate topology and resonance instinctively, without requiring formal instruction or an intellectual theory of what they are doing.

- Different regions can produce unrelated magical abilities, practices, and traditions. Similar effects need not share an origin or method.

- Native origin does not guarantee magical awareness, magical ability, greater power, moral authority, or superiority over peoples from coherent worlds.

- Otherworld-native magic and ecology remain independent of the Void unless a specific later event creates an explicit connection.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 9

- The setting can support radically different Otherworld biologies without reducing every unusual being to a transformed normal-space species or divine creation.

- The boundaries among evolved life, directly emergent life, self-maintaining ecological patterns, and nonliving magical phenomena remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether directly emergent beings reproduce, age, die, possess souls, and evolve in the same ways as evolved Otherworld life.

- The relationship between an Otherworld being’s internal living pattern and the self-patterns recognized by transformation and protective magic remains unresolved.

- Ecologically reinforced coherence requires further decisions about individuality, dependency, consent, exile, ecological collapse, and the death or removal of sustaining relationships.

- Territory-dependent coherence requires rules for migration, travel, captivity, settlement, and the establishment of new sustaining resonances.

- The degree to which instinctive topology or resonance abilities can be taught, copied, inherited, bonded through a familiar relationship, or used by normal-space visitors remains unresolved.

- Otherworld cultures, religions, conflicts, subsistence, and histories must be developed as meaningful in their own right rather than primarily as obstacles or helpers for travelers.

## Decision 10: Soulhood, death boundaries, and portable coherence

- Soulhood follows sustained individual life rather than whether a being evolved within an ecology or emerged directly from chaotic ecological processes.

- Evolved and directly emergent Otherworld beings can therefore possess souls on the same foundational basis. Mode of origin does not create a higher or lower category of life.

- This rule does not yet define the minimum boundary of sustained individual life or establish whether every self-maintaining ecological pattern is a living individual with a soul.

- The Otherworld does not automatically receive, collect, or transport the dead.

- Death in normal space does not ordinarily send a soul into the Otherworld, and the Otherworld is not a universal corridor through which all souls pass.

- Death within the Otherworld likewise does not by itself establish that a soul must remain there or that the Otherworld functions as its afterlife.

- Particular gods, afterlife domains, magical practices, crossings, resonances, or local ecologies may interact with souls only through mechanisms established for those specific cases.

- Cultural or religious traditions may describe the Otherworld as a land of the dead, a road to judgment, or an ancestral realm without those beliefs automatically describing universal cosmological fact.

- Territory-dependent Otherworld beings can survive away from home through three compatible mechanisms: carrying a living fragment of their home ecology, establishing substitute resonance through new relationships, and receiving temporary reinforcement while their coherence gradually fades.

- A carried fragment is part of a living ecological relationship rather than merely an inert reminder or symbolic token. Its exact scale, care requirements, and capabilities remain unresolved.

- New relationships can create substitute resonance capable of supporting a traveler beyond the original home territory. Whether that support can become permanent depends on rules not yet established.

- Coherence loss away from home is gradual rather than automatically immediate. Temporary magical, ecological, or relational reinforcement can slow, halt, or repair that decline according to the being and the method used.

- These mechanisms can overlap. A traveler may carry part of home, rely on protection during transit, and gradually establish sustaining relationships at a destination.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 10

- The exact criterion by which sustained individual life acquires or possesses a soul remains unresolved, including its relationship to reproduction, awareness, memory, autonomy, and ecological interdependence.

- The nature of afterlife domains and the fate of souls without a domain remain foundational unresolved questions.

- The fate of a soul when death occurs inside the Otherworld remains unresolved and must not be answered merely by treating the Otherworld as an afterlife.

- Specific cultures can hold mutually incompatible beliefs about Otherworld death without any tradition automatically receiving authorial confirmation.

- Living fragments of a home ecology require rules for propagation, depletion, disease, ecological incompatibility, theft, damage to the source ecology, and possible invasive effects at a destination.

- Substitute resonance requires further decisions about reciprocity, consent, duration, community membership, and whether a new bond changes the traveler’s identity or nature.

- The rate and symptoms of coherence loss, and the cost and reliability of temporary reinforcement, remain unresolved.

- These mechanisms permit migration and diaspora among some territory-dependent beings without making every species freely mobile.

## Decision 11: The soul, personal continuity, and domain bonds

- A soul is the persisting metaphysical aspect of a living self-pattern and can separate from the body at death.

- During life, the soul and embodied living pattern are aspects of one continuing person rather than unrelated entities temporarily attached to one another.

- Ordinary death initially leaves a whole but vulnerable continuation of the person.

- Wholeness preserves personal continuity rather than producing a new copy, an impersonal essence, or only a detached bundle of memories.

- Vulnerability means that a newly separated soul can be affected by injury, fragmentation, coercion, attachment, loss of coherence, or other forces. The ordinary progression and relative likelihood of these outcomes remain unresolved.

- The soul’s survival does not make the body unimportant or make bodily death automatically reversible. Resurrection, reincarnation, and new embodiment remain separate unresolved mechanisms.

- A soul connects to an afterlife domain through a sustained reciprocal bond with a deity or with the domain itself.

- Reciprocity means the relationship is not created solely by a person naming a faith, admiring a domain’s values, undergoing a final rite, or being labeled by a community.

- Belief, worship, rites, promises, conduct, and communal recognition can cultivate or express a reciprocal bond, but none has yet been established as an automatic substitute for the bond.

- A domain bond can exist without requiring a personified deity if the domain itself is capable of sustaining a reciprocal relationship.

- Active acceptance by a deity may participate in a reciprocal bond, but unilateral divine claim has not been established as an independent general route.

- The duration, awareness, consent, maintenance, and strength required for a bond to count as sustained remain unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 11

- Death preserves the moral and emotional significance of the individual because the surviving soul remains the same continuing person, while vulnerability prevents the afterlife from being automatically safe or guaranteed.

- The relationship among soul, memory, personality, bodily experience, and the living self-pattern needs more precise definition.

- It remains unresolved whether a separated soul remains continuously conscious, experiences time, requires energy or relationship, or can act without a domain.

- The formation, confirmation, weakening, severance, repair, and possible coercion of reciprocal domain bonds remain unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether one soul can sustain bonds with several domains, how conflicting bonds are resolved, and whether the soul retains meaningful choice at death.

- Infants, non-sapient animals, people without access to a domain tradition, isolated beings, and lives incapable of deliberate worship require rules consistent with soulhood following sustained individual life.

- The fate of a soul whose deity dies, whose domain collapses, or whose sustaining bond is damaged remains unresolved.

- The fate of souls without any afterlife-domain bond is now the next central decision rather than something the Otherworld answers automatically.

## Decision 12: Domainless souls, coherence anchors, and planetary return

- A soul without an afterlife-domain bond initially exists in a nonphysical aspect of the same coherent universe.

- It remains resonantly near its death. That resonance may involve the place, circumstances, relationships, and living world associated with the death, but its exact geometry remains unresolved.

- This state is neither ordinary embodied space nor the Otherworld. The Otherworld therefore remains distinct from the normal condition of the dead.

- The newly separated soul initially remains the whole but vulnerable continuation of the person established in Decision 11.

- The soul’s own self-pattern, identity, and memory can help it remain coherent.

- Continuing relationships and recognition by the living or other beings can reinforce its coherence.

- A soul can attach itself to a body, place, or object.

- Magical, spiritual, or divine protection can sustain or protect a domainless soul.

- These sources of coherence may overlap. None automatically creates an afterlife domain or a reciprocal domain bond.

- If no afterlife domain or outside intervention receives the soul, its default long-term fate is to meld with the spirit of the planet and become part of it.

- Planetary spirit is a provisional descriptive term. Its nature, consciousness, origin, and relationship to the physical planet remain unresolved.

- Planetary return is not established as either punishment or reward. Its meaning and experience must not be assigned a moral value without a later decision.

- The timing of planetary return, the degree to which individuality persists within it, and whether it can be delayed, resisted, reversed, or retrieved all remain unresolved.

- Passing into the Void is an exceptional fate, not the ordinary destination of a domainless soul. A bodach can form only after a soul fragment actually enters the Void.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 12

- Afterlife domains are possible destinations created through relationship, while planetary return supplies a normal long-term fate for souls that no domain or intervention receives.

- The planetary spirit must be defined without automatically treating it as a deity, an afterlife domain, or a conscious person.

- It remains unresolved whether every planet has a spirit, whether life is required, and whether dead, barren, artificial, or radically altered worlds can receive souls.

- A rule is needed for which planet receives a soul whose life spans several worlds, as well as for deaths in the Otherworld, deep space, or places without an evident planetary relationship.

- The persistence of memory, identity, awareness, agency, relationship, and distinguishability after melding is now a central decision.

- The duration and lived experience of the domainless interval require definition, including whether time, communication, movement, suffering, rest, and choice remain possible.

- Attachment, haunting, ancestral presence, magical protection, and reciprocal domain bonds must be distinguished even when several occur together.

- Necromancy, soul-binding, resurrection, coercive divine action, and damage caused by prolonged attachment need rules consistent with a whole but vulnerable soul.

- The setting must determine whether a planetary spirit can refuse a soul, be damaged or altered by what joins it, become saturated, divide, merge, or die.

## Decision 13: Planetary spirits, retained lives, and resonant belonging

- A planetary spirit is a layered planetary self rather than a fully formed being that simply precedes its world.

- An early coherence arises from the planet itself. The physical, magical, or metaphysical threshold that first establishes this coherence remains unresolved.

- Life, magic, accumulated history, and the souls received through planetary return progressively deepen that early coherence into a fuller being.

- These later layers enrich and reshape the planetary spirit without replacing its planetary foundation.

- Life and received souls are therefore important to the development of a mature planetary spirit, but they have not yet been established as prerequisites for the earliest planetary coherence.

- A planetary spirit is not automatically a deity or an afterlife domain. Whether it can become divine, form reciprocal bonds, or sustain a domain remains unresolved.

- When a soul melds with a planetary spirit, its distinct personal agency ends. It no longer continues as an independently acting person within the whole.

- The person’s memories and identity-pattern remain within the planetary whole rather than being erased.

- Retention does not yet establish that those memories can be consciously accessed, expressed, separated, communicated with, or restored to individual agency.

- The strongest sustained resonance determines which planetary spirit can receive a domainless soul.

- That resonance is created through the person’s life, relationships, identity, and genuine belonging.

- Birthplace, ancestry, species origin, residence at death, and the physical location of death do not automatically override the total pattern of sustained resonance.

- Planetary resonance can deepen, weaken, and shift across a life as relationships and belonging change. The exact rate and requirements of such change remain unresolved.

- The same strongest-resonance principle applies to people connected to several worlds and to deaths away from any planet, including deep space or the Otherworld.

- This principle identifies the primary basis of planetary belonging but does not yet resolve equal or ambiguous resonances, refusal by a planetary spirit, or whether a soul can deliberately influence the outcome.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 13

- Planetary return preserves what a life contributed to the world without turning death into indefinite separate personal existence.

- Because distinct agency ends, planetary return remains meaningfully different from entering an afterlife domain, lingering as a domainless soul, haunting an attachment, or being restored through resurrection.

- A mature planetary spirit can embody a real accumulation of geology, ecology, magic, history, relationships, and lives without serving as the hidden author of every event on that world.

- Planetary belonging is relational rather than racial, ancestral, or territorial. Migration, adoption, exile, interplanetary settlement, and chosen community can all alter a soul’s deepest planetary resonance.

- A person who spends centuries on another world, including a long-lived elf settling on Erde, may eventually resonate more strongly with that world than with the world of origin. No fixed duration is yet established.

- The setting needs a threshold model for early planetary coherence and for the later development of a mature soul-receiving spirit.

- It remains unresolved whether barren, dead, newly formed, artificial, shattered, or magically transformed planets possess sufficient coherence to receive souls.

- The planetary spirit’s degree of awareness, intention, perception, memory access, communication, and capacity for magical action must be established.

- The worldbuilding must determine whether retained identity-patterns influence the planetary spirit only cumulatively or can sometimes become recognizable expressions within it without regaining separate agency.

- Mass death, large migrations, ecological collapse, divine intervention, planetary destruction, and the loss of magic may alter a planetary spirit, but their mechanisms and limits remain unresolved.

- Rules are still needed for tied or incompatible resonances, souls with no sufficiently developed planetary relationship, and any conflict between planetary reception and a reciprocal afterlife-domain bond.

## Decision 14: Distributed planetary awareness, resonant echoes, and living worlds

- A mature planetary spirit possesses a distributed, slow awareness that experiences its world as a vast whole.

- Its perspective is planetary rather than humanlike. It does not ordinarily divide attention, interpret events, or form intentions on the scale and tempo of an individual person.

- A planetary spirit rarely focuses or acts as a single agent. It therefore does not routinely speak, intervene, select champions, or direct the ordinary history of its inhabitants.

- Slow and distributed awareness does not by itself imply weakness, passivity, or lack of intelligence. Its capacities and limits operate at a different scale and remain to be defined.

- The memories and identity-patterns retained through planetary return can become perceptible as resonant echoes.

- These echoes can surface through dreams, magic, particular places, or moments of crisis.

- A resonant echo does not restore the deceased person’s separate agency or recreate that person as an independently continuing consciousness.

- An echo may carry recognizable memories, traits, emotions, perceptions, or relational patterns, but its possible content, accuracy, and completeness remain unresolved.

- An echo is not automatically a deliberate message from either the deceased person or the planetary spirit.

- Planets, dwarf planets, and major moons can each develop a mature, soul-receiving planetary spirit when sustained life sufficiently deepens their early planetary coherence.

- Astronomical classification alone does not determine spiritual maturity. A world-body’s continuing living development is the controlling principle.

- A lifeless world-body may possess early planetary coherence without yet possessing a mature, soul-receiving planetary spirit.

- The duration, complexity, scale, and continuity of life required for maturity remain unresolved.

- The status of small moons, asteroids, transient habitats, artificial worlds, and living structures that are not conventionally planetary remains unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 14

- Planetary spirits can be cosmologically real and profoundly consequential without becoming hidden rulers responsible for every natural event, magical tradition, or historical outcome.

- Nature magic and planetary religions may arise from genuine contact with planetary-scale awareness, but their interpretations remain culturally mediated and fallible.

- Dreams, sacred places, crises, and magical practices can reveal authentic fragments of retained lives while still producing uncertainty about source, context, and meaning.

- Resonant echoes can preserve emotionally and historically meaningful contact with the dead without functioning as reliable resurrection, unrestricted conversation, or proof of a culture’s complete theology.

- Different cultures may interpret the same kind of echo as an ancestor, a message from the world, a god, memory embedded in place, or an illusion. None receives automatic authorial confirmation.

- Magic may help a practitioner attract, recognize, or interpret planetary attention, but whether it can force focused awareness or manufacture a convincing false echo remains unresolved.

- The development of planetary maturity may depend on ecological continuity, diversity, magical depth, accumulated relationships, received souls, or some combination that still needs definition.

- A mass extinction or planetary catastrophe may wound or diminish a mature spirit without automatically destroying its foundational coherence. The severity, reversibility, and timescale remain unresolved.

- A living dwarf planet or major moon can possess its own mature spirit rather than being spiritually subordinate to a larger neighboring planet.

- The relationship among neighboring planetary spirits, overlapping orbital systems, and souls strongly resonant with more than one living world remains unresolved.

- A rule is still needed for souls whose strongest planetary resonance points toward a world that lacks a mature spirit capable of receiving them.

## Decision 15: Rapid planetary return, limited soul activity, and voluntary domain reception

- Immediately after ordinary death, a domainless soul remains the whole but vulnerable continuation of the person.

- Rapid planetary return is the normal pattern. Most unsupported domainless souls meld with the appropriate planetary spirit within hours or days rather than persisting for a second natural lifetime.

- The transition is nevertheless variable rather than governed by one universal deadline.

- Exceptional strength of self-pattern, identity, memory, continuing relationships, recognition, attachment, magical protection, spiritual protection, or divine protection can extend independent coherence.

- An extended interval may last much longer than the norm, but its likely ranges and ultimate limits remain unresolved.

- Extended coherence does not automatically make stable ghosthood or indefinite independent existence the ordinary fate of the dead.

- During the domainless interval, a soul has limited resonant activity.

- It can perceive people, places, bodies, objects, and circumstances with which it retains meaningful resonance, but it is not freely omnipresent or normally aware of everything around it.

- It can influence dreams, emotions, intuitions, memories, or other impressions through resonance.

- Clear communication or direct physical effects require an anchor, magic, protection, exceptional effort, or some combination of them.

- Attachment to a body, place, or object can serve as an anchor, consistent with Decision 12, but the mechanics and risks of attachment remain unresolved.

- A reciprocal afterlife-domain bond offers reception rather than establishing ownership of the soul.

- The soul can accept or refuse the domain’s reception.

- Acceptance permits the domain to receive and sustain the soul. The transition mechanism and the soul’s condition within the domain remain unresolved.

- Refusal leaves planetary return as the default outcome unless another bond, intervention, anchor, or unusual persistence changes the immediate course.

- A deity, institution, community, or ritual cannot convert an offer into rightful ownership merely by asserting authority over the dead.

- Coercive capture, soul-binding, deception, or magical compulsion may be possible, but these are distinct violations and not valid reciprocal reception.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 15

- Funerary practices are temporally important because the ordinary interval for protection, farewell, discernment, or domain acceptance may be short.

- Most deaths do not produce enduring ghosts. Long-lived domainless souls are exceptional outcomes requiring unusual coherence, relationship, attachment, or intervention.

- A dream or impression shortly after death may come from the still-independent soul, while a much later experience may instead be a planetary resonant echo. Cultures may not reliably distinguish them.

- Continuing love and recognition can support a vulnerable soul, but it remains unresolved whether grief, refusal to release, or ritual remembrance can unintentionally obstruct planetary return.

- Religious institutions can cultivate domain bonds and assist reception, but they cannot guarantee ownership of a person’s soul or prove destination through institutional declaration alone.

- Funerary magic may facilitate clear choice, protect against interference, strengthen a domain offer, encourage planetary return, or deliberately delay transition. Its reliable capabilities and ethical limits need definition.

- Necromancy and soul-binding become especially dangerous during the brief vulnerable interval, creating incentives for protective customs, rapid rites, guarded bodies, and laws concerning the dead.

- It remains unresolved how infants, non-sapient animals, impaired souls, or people unable to understand a domain offer exercise acceptance or refusal.

- Rules are needed for several simultaneous domain offers, damaged or deceptive bonds, a domain that cannot safely receive the soul, and a deity that misrepresents the consequences of acceptance.

- The setting must determine whether a soul can actively resist planetary return, how long will alone can delay it, and whether resistance causes exhaustion, fragmentation, or other harm.

- The degree of awareness, emotional clarity, memory continuity, and disorientation experienced immediately after death remains unresolved.

- The structure of existence inside an accepted afterlife domain is now the next major cosmological question.

## Decision 16: Afterlife-domain structure, continuing personhood, and voluntary departure

- An afterlife domain is a stabilized nonphysical realm within the same coherent universe.

- It is distinct from the Otherworld and does not make the Otherworld a general corridor, container, or destination for the dead.

- The exact spatial and metaphysical relationship between an afterlife domain and embodied normal space remains unresolved.

- A domain is shaped and sustained through reciprocal bonds among its deity or other governing presence, the domain itself, and its inhabitants.

- No single participant automatically owns the domain merely because that participant helped create, govern, or sustain it.

- Different domains may develop different environments, laws, cultures, and forms of existence through their governing relationships, but the range and limits of that variation remain unresolved.

- A soul received into an afterlife domain remains the whole continuing person.

- The person retains memory, identity, agency, relationships, and the capacity to learn and change.

- Reception does not automatically freeze the person at death, idealize them, dissolve them into a collective, or reduce them to a copy, fragment, or stored memory.

- The form of embodiment, perception, sensation, need, time, and activity available inside a domain remains unresolved.

- A received soul can later leave the domain or end its bond.

- A domain cannot rightfully imprison a soul merely because the soul once accepted reception or participated in sustaining the realm.

- Leaving is rare and consequential because it ends or transforms a profound reciprocal relationship, not because the soul must first secure permission, replacement coherence, or another destination.

- A soul does not need an alternate source of coherence or a predetermined destination before leaving.

- After departure, the soul becomes domainless again and is subject to the ordinary possibilities of that state, including limited resonant activity and rapid planetary return.

- Departure does not itself imply damage or fragmentation. The possible scars, losses, relational consequences, and changes produced by ending the bond remain unresolved.

- Coercive confinement may be possible as an abuse of power, but it is distinct from the legitimate nature of reciprocal domain reception.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 16

- Afterlife domains are living relational societies rather than static storage places for the dead or automatic extensions of a deity’s will.

- Because their inhabitants continue to learn and change, domains can develop histories, disagreements, institutions, cultures, reforms, and internal conflicts after their founding.

- Continued personhood allows relationships to endure and evolve, but reunion is not guaranteed when people accept different domains, refuse reception, or return to different planetary spirits.

- An afterlife domain is not automatically a paradise, reward, court of justice, or place of punishment. Those qualities depend on its actual relationships, governance, and conditions.

- Leaving without another destination preserves genuine autonomy even when the likely consequence is a brief domainless interval followed by planetary return.

- Consent to enter a domain is not permanent surrender of agency. Religious teachings that claim irrevocable ownership may therefore be mistaken, manipulative, or describing coercive practice rather than cosmological law.

- The setting needs rules for how a domain is founded, how reciprocal bonds stabilize it, how much power its inhabitants contribute, and whether it has limits on size or population.

- The authority, obligations, and vulnerabilities of a deity or other governing presence remain unresolved, including whether several beings can jointly govern a domain.

- The materiality of domain life needs definition: bodies, landscapes, food, sleep, work, creation, injury, healing, reproduction, privacy, and physical distance may function differently from embodied life.

- Time within a domain, communication across domains, communication with the living, travel between domains, and observation of normal space remain unresolved.

- A domain may weaken, transform, divide, merge, or collapse if its sustaining relationships change. The consequences for inhabitants require explicit rules.

- The fate of inhabitants when a governing deity dies, abandons the domain, becomes hostile, or loses the capacity to sustain it remains unresolved.

- Resurrection, reincarnation, embodiment in normal space, and return to a domain after departure remain separate mechanisms that require later decisions.

## Decision 17: Seeded domains, distributed sustenance, and flexible embodiment

- A new afterlife domain begins when a powerful being establishes an initial stable seed within the nonphysical aspect of the coherent universe.

- The founder may become the domain’s deity or governing presence, but the nature and origin of beings capable of creating such a seed remain unresolved.

- The initial seed is not yet a fully durable afterlife domain.

- Reciprocal bonds with the first inhabitants are required for the seed to become a lasting domain.

- The first inhabitants therefore participate in the domain’s true establishment rather than merely occupying a finished realm owned by its founder.

- If sufficient reciprocal relationships never form, the seed may remain limited, become unstable, or cease to exist. The timing and exact failure modes remain unresolved.

- In an established domain, the governing presence maintains the core structure.

- The inhabitants’ relationships, activity, and bonds maintain, develop, and enlarge the living realm around that core.

- Domain sustenance is distributed reciprocity rather than power supplied almost entirely by the governing presence, received souls, or worshippers in embodied life.

- Continued worship or ritual among the living may influence a domain through existing bonds, but it is not established as the principal fuel required for ordinary survival.

- A domain’s potential size, complexity, stability, and population may grow with its reciprocal network, but no assumption of unlimited capacity has been made.

- A received soul normally exists without a fixed body while remaining the same whole person with memory, identity, agency, relationships, and capacity for change.

- Souls can assume bodies when desired.

- Souls can also participate in forming or experiencing environments when desired.

- Assumed embodiment does not create a replacement person or interrupt continuity of identity.

- The degree to which bodies and environments are private, shared, persistent, mutable, or governed by the domain remains unresolved.

- The effort, skill, consent, rules, and limits involved in assuming or changing bodies and environments remain unresolved.

- Ordinary biological needs, bodily injury, healing, pleasure, pain, sleep, food, aging, reproduction, and death within a domain have not yet been defined.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 17

- A durable domain is jointly produced by its founder and community, preventing creation alone from establishing permanent divine ownership.

- Founding inhabitants may hold unique historical, structural, or political significance because their bonds helped transform the seed into a viable realm.

- An established domain can develop beyond its founder’s original design as inhabitants create relationships, practices, institutions, environments, and history.

- Domain growth can arise from meaningful life within the realm rather than requiring endless worship by mortals.

- Conflict, isolation, broken bonds, population loss, or declining participation can alter the realm itself, although ordinary disagreement should not automatically cause collapse.

- The governing presence remains structurally important even within distributed sustenance. Its loss, corruption, withdrawal, or replacement requires explicit rules.

- Flexible embodiment permits inhabitants from different species and bodily histories to meet without requiring one universal humanoid form.

- Because embodiment is chosen rather than fixed, appearance, recognition, disability, age, sex, species, shapeshifting, privacy, and deception all require domain-specific social and metaphysical rules.

- Shared environments may become genuine communal creations rather than scenery projected by a deity, but authority over persistent changes remains unresolved.

- It remains unresolved whether a soul can maintain several bodies, appear differently to different observers, divide attention among manifestations, or create autonomous constructs.

- The relationship between assumed form and the soul’s living self-pattern needs definition, especially when a person preferred a form different from the body held at death.

- Reproduction or the creation of new life inside a domain cannot be inferred from flexible embodiment and remains a separate foundational question.

- The domain’s capacity to support continued learning and change raises questions about memory burden, psychological healing, conflict, boredom, risk, purpose, and voluntary transformation over extremely long existence.

- The next decisions must define the baseline form of chosen embodiment, the persistence and ownership of shared environments, and whether a mature domain can survive the loss of its governing presence.

## Decision 18: Self-recognized embodiment, collective environments, difficult succession, and the epistemic veil

- A soul’s self-recognized living pattern normally guides the first body it assumes within an afterlife domain.

- This baseline is not automatically the body held at death, the youngest or healthiest remembered body, or the form assigned to the person by other people.

- The self-recognized pattern can reflect the person’s embodied history and identity while remaining subject to further deliberate change.

- Changing an assumed body is possible through intent, practice, and support from the domain.

- The range, effort, limits, reversibility, and social consequences of bodily change within a domain remain unresolved.

- An established domain contains a persistent common reality sustained collectively by the domain’s reciprocal network.

- Individuals can shape personal spaces within that shared reality.

- Major changes to public environments require consent, legitimate authority, broad reciprocal support, or an appropriate combination determined by the domain’s institutions.

- Personal shaping does not automatically grant power to rewrite another inhabitant, another person’s private space, or the common realm.

- If a mature domain loses its governing presence, the inhabitants’ reciprocal network can sustain the domain temporarily.

- Without a restored core, new anchor, successor, or governing structure, the domain gradually declines and may eventually collapse.

- Establishing a replacement governing structure, anchor, or successor is possible but fairly difficult even when participants understand a valid precedent.

- An unprecedented succession attempted by people with no prior experience is very difficult.

- Every successful succession requires an immense amount of power, whether or not the process has been performed before.

- A successor need not be assumed to be one deity or person. A being, structure, anchor, or other governing arrangement may be possible, but the viable forms remain unresolved.

- The true default fate of domainless souls is not generally available cosmological knowledge.

- Only planetary spirits know that unreceived souls normally meld into a planetary spirit and become part of it.

- Mortals, deities, domain inhabitants, Otherworld beings, and other powers do not ordinarily possess authoritative knowledge of this fate.

- Planetary spirits almost never communicate directly enough to make planetary return obvious to another being.

- This silence is not concealment. Direct individual speech is generally foreign to their distributed, planetary nature.

- A planetary spirit might communicate more directly in the most extreme circumstances, but even then it does not ordinarily explain domainless death in a clear, comprehensive, or easily verified form.

- Planetary return is therefore authorial reality while most in-world accounts of domainless death remain belief, inference, fragmentary experience, or cultural mythology.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 18

- Chosen embodiment begins from identity rather than external judgment, while still preserving meaningful effort, learning, and limits on transformation.

- Domain societies require law, custom, or shared practice concerning personal space, public creation, competing visions, consent, and legitimate authority.

- Political conflict can alter a domain’s common reality, but no single inhabitant should be able to overwrite it merely through private imagination.

- Succession planning can materially improve a domain’s chance of survival without making replacement of a governing presence routine.

- Domains with no precedent, preparation, knowledgeable participants, or accessible source of immense power are especially vulnerable after losing their governing presence.

- A failed succession may waste power, damage the core, accelerate collapse, or harm participants, but the possible failure modes remain unresolved.

- The meaning and source of the immense power required for succession must be defined without assuming that raw destructive strength alone is sufficient.

- Domain inhabitants may know how their own realm receives and sustains souls while remaining ignorant of what ultimately happens to souls outside all domains.

- Religions, necromancers, philosophers, and gods can hold sophisticated but incompatible theories about domainless death without any of them automatically possessing the truth.

- Funerary experiences, brief contact with domainless souls, and later planetary echoes can supply genuine evidence while still failing to reveal planetary return as a complete process.

- The epistemic veil reinforces the broader project principle that cultural myth and knowledge have no guaranteed relationship to reality.

- The rare direct attention of a planetary spirit should be an extreme event rather than a routine source of exposition, prophecy, religious confirmation, or magical instruction.

- It remains unresolved whether even the greatest gods can recognize a planetary spirit’s awareness, compel its attention, or distinguish a deliberate planetary response from an emergent resonance.

- The next decisions should define the survival period after a governing loss, the possible forms of succession, and the fate of inhabitants if a domain actually collapses.

## Decision 19: Domain reserves, soul-powered succession, and collapse outcomes

- After the loss of its governing presence, a mature domain survives on the reserve held within its structure and reciprocal network.

- A strong, cohesive reciprocal network may preserve a domain for years or centuries.

- An isolated, divided, depleted, or poorly integrated domain declines much faster.

- Reserve duration is variable rather than a universal countdown. Its exact thresholds and pattern of decline remain unresolved.

- A failing domain can restore its core through any of four established classes of succession.

- A single sufficiently powerful successor can form a new reciprocal governing bond with the domain.

- A failing domain can merge with another domain whose governing structure is capable of receiving and integrating it.

- A deeply bonded council can distribute the governing function among several souls or other beings.

- A constructed metaphysical anchor or institutional lattice can assume the structural role without becoming a person.

- Every form of succession still requires immense, literal metaphysical power. Knowledge, preparation, and precedent may improve the attempt but do not remove that requirement.

- A council is not empowered merely by agreement, office, reputation, symbolic authority, or moral standing. Its participants must possess enough real power to perform the governing work.

- Any soul serving in such a council must have been a genuinely powerful person during embodied life.

- Physical strength leaves its own power imprint upon the soul. That imprint can contribute to metaphysical work, but its power must be translated into a form the domain can use.

- Magical strength contributes soul power more directly and efficiently because it does not require the same translation.

- These differences describe usable capacity, not personal worth, moral rank, or a universal hierarchy among peoples.

- A constructed anchor or institutional lattice must incorporate actual souls as active sustaining participants.

- No fully inanimate mechanism can sustain an afterlife domain by itself.

- An inanimate structure may focus, organize, or mediate soul-bearing power, but it cannot replace the living or personal contribution entirely.

- The required consent, degree of incorporation, reversibility, and effect on the participating souls within a lattice remain unresolved.

- The consent, autonomy, and cultural continuity involved when one domain receives another also remain unresolved.

- If a domain declines in an orderly way, its inhabitants are released whole into domainless existence.

- Whole released souls then follow the ordinary rules of domainless existence, including possible reception by another valid domain and the otherwise rapid return toward a planetary spirit.

- If a domain collapses violently or suddenly, some inhabitants may be wounded or fragmented during release.

- A soul injured or fragmented by domain collapse does not automatically become a bodach. Bodach formation still requires actual entry into the Void.

- The persistence, repair, and eventual fate of souls fragmented by domain collapse remain unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 19

- Succession is a rare act of metaphysical engineering, communal coordination, or both, rather than an administrative transfer of office.

- A known precedent can supply technique and reduce uncertainty, but it cannot supply the immense power the attempt consumes or directs.

- Power cultivated during embodied life can affect a soul’s later capacity to sustain an afterlife domain.

- Physically formidable people may contribute meaningful soul power, while magically powerful people can usually contribute it more directly.

- A sufficiently powerful collective can perform a role that might otherwise require one extraordinary founder or successor.

- A soul-bearing lattice creates an ethically dangerous possibility because persons become structural participants rather than replaceable components.

- A purely mechanical or fully inanimate immortal afterlife is impossible under the current model.

- Domain merger can rescue inhabitants from collapse while threatening the autonomy, institutions, identity, or environment of the received community.

- Long reserves create time for succession, merger, evacuation, negotiation, and conflict. Weak reserves may leave almost no meaningful preparation period.

- Orderly decline can produce organized departure and large populations of whole domainless souls seeking reception.

- Violent failure can create wounded souls, coherent fragments, and spiritual crises without importing the Void into ordinary death.

- Even severe collapse damage remains distinct from bodach formation unless some separate mechanism carries the fragments into the Void.

- The next decisions should define consent within soul-bearing lattices, whether a soul’s usable power can grow after bodily death, and the normal fate of souls injured or fragmented by collapse.

## Decision 20: Continuing consent, postmortem power limits, and repairable collapse fragments

- A stable governing anchor or institutional lattice requires the continuing informed consent of every incorporated soul.

- Informed consent requires meaningful knowledge of the soul’s structural role, expected burdens, risks, and the consequences that participation or withdrawal may have for the soul and domain.

- Consent is an ongoing relationship rather than a one-time authorization surrendered at incorporation.

- A participating soul must therefore retain meaningful agency within the lattice. The exact procedure, timing, and structural consequences of withdrawal remain unresolved.

- A coercively constructed or maintained lattice can function, but it is inherently unstable, damaging, and abusive.

- Coercion does not make the structure immediately impossible. It makes both the participating souls and the domain vulnerable to deterioration, rupture, distortion, or other unresolved forms of harm.

- An existential emergency does not automatically convert compelled participation into stable consent, although cultures and domain governments may claim otherwise.

- A soul’s baseline usable power is established by the capacities and imprints formed during embodied life.

- After bodily death, a soul can continue learning and can refine control, efficiency, technique, judgment, and cooperation with other souls.

- Ordinary postmortem practice does not normally produce a major increase in raw power.

- Major growth in raw soul power remains possible only through rare transformation, domain-scale support, or another comparably exceptional process.

- The mechanisms, costs, reversibility, and effects on identity involved in such exceptional growth remain unresolved.

- This rule preserves the distinction established in Decision 19: physical power leaves an imprint requiring translation, while magical strength contributes to soul power more directly.

- A soul wounded or fragmented by violent domain collapse remains in domainless existence unless another force or domain receives it.

- Such fragments remain potentially repairable. Fragmentation does not by itself establish permanent destruction of the person.

- Coherent or partially coherent fragments may persist independently for a time, attach to people, places, bodies, objects, or magical structures, dissipate, or undergo planetary return separately.

- No one of these outcomes is universal. Damage, remaining self-pattern, recognition, anchors, protection, and elapsed time may all affect the result.

- Planetary return of separate fragments does not automatically imply that a planetary spirit reconstructs the whole person before receiving them.

- A fragment cannot become a bodach unless it actually enters the Void.

- A collapse fragment that later enters the Void may undergo bodach formation, but the original collapse is not itself a Void event.

- The means of finding, gathering, stabilizing, and reintegrating fragments, and the standard for restored personal continuity, remain unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 20

- Soul-bearing lattices are sustained relationships among persons, not mechanisms that convert people into permanently owned fuel.

- Withdrawal rights create a real structural vulnerability. A domain may need replacement participants, stored reserve, or a controlled transition before a consenting soul can leave safely.

- Coercive afterlives can exist without becoming metaphysically equivalent to healthy domains. Their instability and damage arise from violated reciprocity rather than automatic moral punishment imposed by the cosmos.

- Political and religious institutions may obscure coercion, redefine consent, or exaggerate emergencies even though their claims do not alter the underlying stability requirement.

- Postmortem societies can produce masters, teachers, coordinators, and refined magical traditions without allowing every long-lived soul to accumulate unlimited raw power.

- Embodied history remains consequential after death, but it does not permanently fix knowledge, skill, relationships, or personal development.

- Rare power-increasing transformations can remain extraordinary historical events and possible sources of conflict rather than routine progression.

- Repairable fragmentation supports rescue, healing, contested identity, incomplete recovery, and long-term consequences without making damage trivial.

- Separate fragments may preserve different memories, impulses, attachments, or capabilities, raising difficult questions about agency and reintegration.

- Repair must not be assumed to occur automatically through planetary return, divine reception, or the mere passage of time.

- Collapse fragments remain part of ordinary soul cosmology unless a distinct portal or other mechanism carries them into the Void.

- The next decisions should define withdrawal from a lattice, the requirements and limits of fragment repair, and the exceptional processes that can increase raw soul power after death.

## Decision 21: Absolute withdrawal, consensual restoration, and plural paths to postmortem power

- An incorporated soul may withdraw its consent from a governing anchor or institutional lattice immediately and unconditionally.

- Withdrawal requires no notice period, replacement participant, governing approval, communal vote, completed ritual, or proof of sufficient cause.

- The right to withdraw remains absolute regardless of the damage, instability, or collapse the departure may cause within the domain.

- Withdrawal ends the soul’s structural participation at once. No lattice can make continued incorporation a condition of safe release without becoming coercive.

- The resulting loss of power or structure belongs to the domain and its remaining participants; it cannot be transferred back into an obligation imposed upon the departing soul.

- Informed consent therefore means understanding foreseeable consequences, not waiving the future right to leave.

- Institutions may encourage planning, redundancy, or voluntary transition, but these practices cannot delay or prevent a soul’s chosen withdrawal.

- Enough of a fragmented soul’s core self-pattern must survive for restoration as one continuing person to remain possible.

- Restoration does not require recovery of every lost or surviving fragment.

- Every fragment that has developed sufficient self-awareness and agency must consent before being reintegrated.

- Relationships, recognition, magic, anchors, and surviving portions of the self-pattern can help heal genuinely missing portions rather than merely attaching recovered pieces.

- Successful restoration preserves continuity with the original person rather than creating a replacement defined only by an external description or collection of memories.

- Restoration may still leave gaps, scars, altered capacities, or changed relationships. Potential repair does not guarantee exact return to the state before injury.

- The treatment of fragments that lack enough awareness to consent, conflicts among self-aware fragments, and the minimum surviving core required for continuity remain unresolved.

- Deep reciprocal integration with a domain and its community can gradually expand a soul’s raw capacity through sustained, real structural responsibility.

- Residence, prestige, office, worship, or symbolic importance alone does not produce this growth.

- A fundamental transformation can remake and enlarge a soul’s capacity, but it carries serious risks to identity, stability, and continuity.

- A willing soul or governing being can permanently transfer part of its own finite power to another soul.

- A genuine transfer reduces or relinquishes power held by the giver rather than creating limitless capacity through declaration.

- Several consenting souls can combine part of their capacity through a durable bond without ceasing to be distinct individuals.

- Such a bond creates substantially greater power available through the relationship. Whether any portion becomes permanent individual growth after the bond ends remains unresolved.

- All four mechanisms are valid but distinct: reciprocal growth, transformative remaking, finite transfer, and bonded combination.

- None of these mechanisms establishes moral superiority, rightful authority, or greater personal worth merely from greater power.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 21

- A governing lattice must be designed to survive free departure rather than treating structural necessity as ownership of its participants.

- Redundancy, reserve, voluntary succession planning, and distributed responsibility become practical safeguards, but never prerequisites for withdrawal.

- A single departure may save a soul while killing a domain and releasing or injuring many others. The cosmology permits that tragedy without converting consequences into compelled consent.

- Leaders may exert social pressure, conceal risk, or frame withdrawal as betrayal even though they possess no legitimate metaphysical right to prevent it.

- Fragment restoration centers surviving personal continuity and the agency of emergent fragments rather than treating them as property of the largest piece.

- Because not every fragment is required, some aspects of memory, disposition, talent, or attachment may remain permanently absent even after genuine restoration.

- Self-aware fragments can become persons with interests that complicate the assumption that reintegration is always healing or always desirable.

- Relational healing allows loved ones and communities to aid restoration without granting them authority to define the restored person against that person’s surviving self-pattern.

- Postmortem power has several sources with different ethical and metaphysical costs, preventing all powerful souls from following one universal progression.

- Domain integration rewards actual reciprocal responsibility, while transformation, transfer, and bonding create separate traditions, risks, institutions, and abuses.

- Finite transfer permits sacrifice, inheritance, patronage, theft disguised as consent, and concentrated power, while still conserving the giver’s contribution.

- Bonded power allows collectives to rival singular great beings without erasing the individuals who compose them.

- The next decisions should define a withdrawn soul’s continuing relationship to its domain, how missing identity can heal without becoming reconstruction, and which forms of acquired power persist after their supporting relationship ends.

## Decision 22: Continued residence, evidence-bound healing, and source-specific power persistence

- Withdrawal from a governing anchor or institutional lattice ends only the soul’s structural role within that governing arrangement.

- If the domain survives the withdrawal, the former participant may remain as an ordinary inhabitant.

- Withdrawal from the lattice does not automatically sever the soul’s other relationships with the domain, its places, its inhabitants, or its governing presence.

- The soul may separately choose to leave the domain under the ordinary rules governing voluntary departure.

- Remaining and departing are distinct decisions. Neither choice may be presumed from the act of withdrawing structural consent.

- The domain’s social or political response to a former participant, including questions of status, blame, protection, or expulsion, remains a matter requiring later institutional rules rather than an automatic cosmological consequence.

- During restoration, a surviving core self-pattern can regrow basic capacities, characteristic tendencies, and enough internal organization for the person to become whole again.

- Regrowth does not grant perfect recovery of information that no longer survives anywhere.

- An exact lost memory requires a surviving trace connected to that person’s experience, such as a fragment, resonant relationship, place, object, magical record, or other anchor.

- Another person’s recollection can guide recognition and healing but does not automatically reproduce the lost person’s original subjective memory exactly.

- When no adequate trace survives, restoration may heal the capacity to remember and develop without restoring the missing event itself.

- A restored person can therefore be genuinely continuous and whole while retaining permanent gaps, altered associations, or scars.

- False, partial, contradictory, or manipulated traces may complicate restoration. The standards for identifying an authentic trace remain unresolved.

- Power gained through deep reciprocal integration and genuinely internalized by a soul remains part of that soul after the supporting role or domain relationship ends.

- A fundamental transformation permanently alters the soul unless the transformation itself is later reversed through another valid process.

- Power received through an explicitly permanent transfer remains with the recipient after the relationship with the giver ends.

- The giver does not automatically recover permanently transferred power when affection, allegiance, office, or contact ends.

- Power created through a durable bond remains pooled rather than becoming the separate permanent possession of every participant.

- When the bond ends, access to its combined capacity ends and each participant retains their own underlying contribution unless a separate transfer occurred.

- Skills, coordination habits, knowledge, and personal refinements learned through the bond can remain after pooled power is lost.

- Leaving a domain may remove domain-specific support or techniques even when internalized raw growth remains.

- Persistence therefore depends upon the source and structure of the power: internalized growth, transformation, and permanent transfer endure; relational pooling does not.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 22

- A soul can reject structural responsibility without being cosmologically exiled from its home, community, or other relationships.

- Domains may still develop laws and conflicts around withdrawal, but those institutions cannot rewrite withdrawal into automatic departure.

- A former lattice participant may remain present among people harmed by the withdrawal, creating consequences that are social, political, and personal rather than imposed by metaphysical punishment.

- Fragment healing restores a person through surviving continuity and real regrowth, not by assembling a persuasive imitation from external descriptions.

- Memory loss remains meaningful even when the person survives and heals. Wholeness does not require perfect archival restoration.

- Relationships can preserve genuine traces of another person, but their memories and interpretations cannot simply overwrite the restored person’s own perspective.

- Anchors that preserve subjective traces become important to healers, afterlife communities, funerary practices, investigators, and anyone anticipating domain failure.

- Manipulated traces create opportunities for accidental distortion, coercive identity shaping, and deliberate falsification without making restoration inherently unreliable.

- Source-specific persistence prevents temporary cooperation from becoming an effortless method of duplicating power among all participants.

- Permanent transfer has lasting stakes because the giver’s loss and recipient’s gain survive the relationship that justified it.

- Internalized domain growth allows a soul to carry genuine development elsewhere, while domain-specific scaffolding and pooled bonds still create dependence on place and relationship.

- The cosmology must next define time and ordinary activity within domains, the possibility of injury or a second death, and whether new souls or living beings can originate there.

## Decision 23: Predominantly subjective time, vulnerable embodiment, and closed soul population

- Afterlife domains remain within the causal order of the coherent universe.

- No domain can use temporal variation to make an inhabitant arrive before the event from which their journey or action began.

- Most temporal variation within an afterlife domain is perceptual or subjective rather than a large difference in objective elapsed time.

- Attention, memory, activity, contemplation, assumed bodily rhythms, and the structure of an experience can make an interval feel greatly expanded, compressed, discontinuous, or differently paced.

- Shared public events still require enough common temporal structure for inhabitants to act together, form histories, and recognize sequence.

- Limited objective differences in rate may occur between domains or between a domain and normal space, but they are secondary, constrained, and not the ordinary explanation for experienced variation.

- The causes, range, predictability, and measurement of genuine rate differences remain unresolved.

- An assumed body can experience meaningful sensation, limitation, fatigue, injury, illness, pain, healing, and bodily death.

- Ordinary bodily death within a domain dissolves or ends the assumed form rather than killing or automatically injuring the soul.

- A soul whose assumed body dies remains the same continuing person and can exist without a body.

- The ability, effort, delay, and support involved in assuming a new body after bodily death remain unresolved.

- Pain and bodily danger are real experiences even when they do not ordinarily threaten the soul. Repeated injury or bodily death may still cause fear, trauma, exhaustion, or social harm.

- Direct injury to the soul requires metaphysical violence, catastrophic strain, violent domain collapse, or another force capable of reaching beyond the assumed body.

- Soul injury can damage identity, memory, capacity, or coherence and may produce the fragmentation described in Decisions 20 through 22.

- Ordinary weapons, accidents, diseases, and bodily violence do not automatically become soul-damaging merely because they occur inside an afterlife domain.

- The boundary between harm limited to an assumed body and an attack capable of reaching the soul remains to be defined.

- An afterlife domain cannot originate a genuinely new person or soul.

- Every inhabitant must have possessed a soul before entering the domain.

- A governing presence, domain community, collective imagination, assumed biological process, or constructed environment cannot manufacture a new soul-bearing person within the realm.

- Domain-created figures, bodies, simulations, environments, or responsive constructs cannot become new persons merely through complexity or resemblance.

- A being that already possesses a soul may potentially enter a domain by a valid route, but whether living visitors can do so remains unresolved.

- Reproduction or family-making within a domain cannot create a new native child or other new person. Whether existing souls can participate in analogous forms of embodiment remains unresolved.

- The origin of souls in embodied living reality remains a separate cosmological question and is not answered by afterlife-domain mechanics.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 23

- Afterlife domains possess meaningful duration and history without becoming reliable time machines or places detached from causality.

- Most reports of centuries compressed into moments, endless conversations, or strangely brief ages may describe authentic experience without proving an equivalent objective time differential.

- Limited real rate differences still permit domains to fall somewhat out of step with normal space or one another, but this requires later limits to prevent uncontrolled chronology problems.

- Chosen embodiment remains consequential because bodily vulnerability, pain, recovery, and trauma are real even when ordinary bodily death is not final.

- Violence within a domain can matter without every weapon becoming a soul-destroying instrument.

- Metaphysical weapons, hostile governing structures, catastrophic rituals, and domain collapse occupy a distinct and much more dangerous category because they can injure the continuing person directly.

- A closed soul population means domains grow primarily through reception and migration rather than reproduction.

- Communities cannot solve demographic decline by manufacturing inhabitants, and governing presences cannot create loyal subjects from nothing.

- Family, parenthood, childhood, creation, and care may still have forms within domain cultures, but they cannot be assumed to reproduce mortal biology or generate new persons.

- The distinction between a sophisticated construct and a genuine person is cosmologically real, although inhabitants may lack perfect methods for recognizing it.

- The next decisions should define whether living beings can visit a domain, how inhabitants can communicate with the living, and whether a soul can return to embodied life in normal space.

## Decision 24: Tethered projection, domain-specific communion, demidomains, and irreversible death

- A living person can visit an afterlife domain through rare soul-projection while remaining tethered to their living body.

- The physical body remains in normal space and does not enter the domain.

- The tether preserves the living person’s continuing relationship with the body and distinguishes projection from death, domain reception, resurrection, or reincarnation.

- Projection is exceptional rather than a routine religious or magical practice. Its requirements, duration, risks, and means of return remain unresolved.

- Communication between domain inhabitants and the living depends substantially upon the nature, population, structure, and practices of the particular domain.

- The ordinary baseline is indirect contact through dreams, impressions, emotions, resonant signs, and similarly limited experiences.

- Clear two-way conversation is fairly rare and ordinarily requires an unusually favorable domain, a strong reciprocal bond, a prepared anchor or rite, exceptional power, or some combination.

- Certain cultures can maintain small local demidomains through exceptionally strong communal and familial bonds with honored elders.

- One known cultural pattern lays honored elders to rest within family homes using rites that preserve active relationships between the dead, the household, and the continuing community.

- A demidomain is categorically distinct from a fully developed afterlife domain.

- It has no powerful founder or governing presence, no resident soul holds meaningful governing power, and the demidomain itself possesses little independent power.

- A demidomain requires no immense soul-powered core. Deep communal and familial bonds are sufficient to sustain its limited coherence.

- Its perceptive reality is sparse and weakly elaborated rather than a fully developed shared world.

- Most resident souls rest with little continuous activity or environmental perception.

- Because the realm is local, limited, and not crowded by many diffuse or continuously active spirits, living relatives may sometimes speak clearly with an honored dead person to seek guidance.

- This accessibility is domain-specific and does not make clear conversation with the dead normal across the wider cosmology.

- A demidomain cannot retain souls indefinitely.

- Most souls within a demidomain eventually choose or cease to remain and undergo planetary return.

- Without direct bonds to living people, a demidomain ordinarily cannot retain a soul for more than a few hundred years.

- Even a soul maintaining strong direct bonds with the living can ordinarily remain for only a few thousand years.

- A singularly powerful individual with strong living bonds may remain longer, but this is exceptional.

- These durations are approximate ranges shaped by the strength, continuity, reciprocity, and renewal of the sustaining relationships rather than exact universal deadlines.

- Death is irreversible.

- A soul that has died cannot return to embodied life in normal space.

- True resurrection, reincarnation into a new mortal life, permanent possession as a substitute embodiment, and construction of a new living vessel for a dead soul are not valid routes around death.

- A dead soul may still influence normal space through dreams, impressions, signs, anchors, magic, or domain-specific communication without becoming embodied.

- Assuming a body within an afterlife domain does not restore mortal life.

- A living projector’s eventual return to the body is not resurrection because the living tether and original embodied life were never severed by death.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 24

- Demidomains create a culturally grounded middle state between rapid domainless planetary return and reception into a powerful, fully developed afterlife domain.

- They can arise from durable family and community practices without requiring a deity, great magical institution, or soul powerful enough to found a full domain.

- Keeping honored remains within a household can be an active relationship with the dead rather than only memorial symbolism, although cultures may misunderstand the underlying mechanics.

- Direct ancestral guidance remains local, relational, intermittent, and finite rather than granting broad public access to an omniscient dead.

- An elder retains the knowledge, limitations, personality, biases, and incomplete understanding held by that individual rather than gaining automatic cosmic truth.

- Demidomains depend heavily upon living continuity. Migration, family fracture, destroyed homes, abandoned rites, cultural suppression, or loss of recognition may weaken them.

- Their finite duration prevents ancestor communities from accumulating without limit and reinforces planetary return as the eventual outcome for most souls outside full domains.

- Exceptional individuals may outlast ordinary bounds without turning every famous or powerful ancestor into a permanent spirit.

- Rare projection permits firsthand contact with an afterlife domain while keeping the boundary consequential and dangerous.

- Irreversible death protects mortality from becoming a problem that sufficient magical power can routinely undo.

- Necromancy, divine intervention, or soul magic may produce communication, animation, echoes, constructs, or deception, but none can restore a dead person to embodied life.

- The setting must distinguish authentic contact from dreams, memories, planetary echoes, magical simulations, impostors, and culturally shaped interpretation.

- The next decisions should define what happens if a projector’s tether or body fails, what materially and relationally anchors a demidomain, and which souls may enter or remain within one.

## Decision 25: Projection death, relational demidomain anchors, and reciprocal membership

- If a living projector’s body dies while the soul is visiting an afterlife domain, death severs the tether.

- The projected soul becomes genuinely dead at that moment. It cannot return to or revive the body.

- The domain being visited does not automatically acquire or own the newly dead soul.

- The soul may remain only if the domain is capable of receiving it, the relevant reciprocal relationship exists, and the soul consents to reception.

- If the visited domain cannot or does not receive the soul, or if the soul refuses reception, the soul enters ordinary domainless existence.

- Subsequent planetary return or reception by another valid domain follows the established rules for any other domainless soul.

- Death during projection is therefore neither protection from death nor a privileged route into an otherwise unavailable afterlife.

- What happens when a tether is damaged or severed while the body remains alive is still unresolved.

- Living and ancestral relationships are the primary anchors sustaining a demidomain.

- Honored remains, the family home, funerary placement, repeated rites, household practices, and inherited objects focus, stabilize, localize, and renew those relationships.

- No single body, building, object, or ritual is indispensable to the demidomain’s continued existence.

- Destruction, removal, or abandonment of a material anchor can weaken or disorient a demidomain without automatically destroying it while sufficient relationships survive.

- A demidomain may potentially be reanchored after relocation or loss, but the process, risks, and degree of continuity remain unresolved.

- Material anchors matter because they concentrate recognition and practice, not because they contain or own the souls.

- Entry into and continued residence within a family or communal demidomain depend upon reciprocal belonging.

- The deceased soul must consent to enter or remain.

- The living and ancestral community must recognize a genuine relationship with that soul.

- Strong lived bonds matter more than biological descent, legal adoption, inherited title, formal membership, fame, or ritual designation alone.

- A deeply bonded friend, caregiver, neighbor, student, teacher, or other community member may qualify even without familial status.

- Biological or legal kinship does not guarantee entry when reciprocal belonging is absent.

- Neither the living community nor resident ancestors can install an unwilling soul.

- A soul’s eligibility for another afterlife domain does not force it to choose that domain over a demidomain, and demidomain membership does not erase the soul’s right to leave.

- How divided communities, disputed belonging, several simultaneous communities, or changing recognition affect membership remains unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 25

- A projected visitor accepts a genuine mortality risk because the body can die while the soul is absent.

- A conveniently visited domain cannot bypass the reciprocal reception rules at the moment of death.

- Projection traditions require protection for the body and tether, trustworthy attendants, time limits, and plans for interrupted return.

- Relationship-first anchoring allows demidomain traditions to survive migration, disaster, rebuilding, cremation, or changing burial practices when a community successfully carries its bonds forward.

- Material destruction can still be devastating because homes, remains, and rites organize the relationships that sustain the realm, even though no object functions as a metaphysical on-off switch.

- Conquerors or reformers may incorrectly believe that destroying remains or ancestral houses automatically annihilates the dead, while cultural suppression can still weaken a demidomain by breaking the practices and recognition that sustain it.

- Reciprocal membership makes chosen family and durable communal bonds cosmologically meaningful without making biology irrelevant to cultures that emphasize it.

- Communities can disagree about who belongs, but status, law, or ritual performance alone cannot manufacture the necessary reciprocal relationship.

- A soul may be claimed by several families, communities, religions, or domains while retaining the right to accept one relationship, move among valid possibilities if permitted, or refuse them.

- Demidomain membership remains voluntary and finite rather than a permanent ancestral obligation.

- The next decisions should define nonfatal tether failure, whether demidomains can migrate or link across households, and how conflicting communal claims are resolved when a soul belongs to several groups.

## Decision 26: Forced reintegration, networked demidomains, and sovereign afterlife choice

- When a living projector’s tether is seriously disrupted while the body remains alive, the tether normally attempts to force the soul back into embodied reintegration.

- This recoil is a protective tendency rather than a guarantee of immediate, complete, or harmless return.

- Severe disruption can delay, interrupt, or distort reintegration.

- Possible outcomes include temporary stranding, coma, memory loss, confusion, damaged continuity between bodily and projected experience, or direct injury to the soul.

- The living body may remain biologically viable while the person cannot fully awaken or reintegrate.

- A temporarily stranded soul remains tethered to the living body unless the tether is fully severed or the body dies.

- Successful return after disruption may require time, healing, ritual assistance, a domain’s cooperation, or another skilled intervention.

- Failure of reintegration does not by itself constitute death while the living tether and body endure.

- If the body subsequently dies, Decision 25 applies: the tether ends, the person becomes genuinely dead, and reception is not automatic.

- Demidomains can be gradually reanchored as families and communities migrate, rebuild, divide households, or change material practices.

- Successful reanchoring depends primarily upon continuity of living and ancestral relationships, supported by rites, recognition, and replacement material anchors.

- A gradual overlap between old and new anchors can preserve continuity more reliably than abrupt abandonment, although exact requirements remain unresolved.

- Several household demidomains can form a limited network through sustained relationships, shared rites, intermarriage, adoption, communal institutions, or regular contact.

- Networked demidomains retain distinct local identities rather than automatically merging into one ancestral realm.

- Links may permit limited contact, shared recognition, guidance, or movement among compatible local demidomains, but their exact capabilities remain unresolved.

- Excessive scale reduces clarity because reciprocal attention, recognition, local meaning, and perceptive structure become diffuse.

- A larger network does not acquire the governing core, independent power, or fully elaborated reality of a true afterlife domain merely by adding households.

- Networks that grow too broad may become quiet, indistinct, fragmented, difficult to navigate, or less capable of clear communication with the living.

- When a soul has valid reciprocal belonging with several communities, demidomains, or full domains, the soul’s own preference is decisive.

- The soul may choose one valid realm, move among compatible realms, maintain several permitted bonds, or refuse every offered relationship.

- No family, community, religion, deity, domain, demidomain, or governing presence owns the soul through the strength or age of its claim.

- The strongest bond does not automatically override the soul’s choice.

- First reception does not create permanent priority or prevent later departure.

- Maintaining several bonds does not guarantee that every realm permits simultaneous residence, free transit, or equal access.

- The soul may change its preference as relationships, identity, knowledge, or circumstances change.

- The limits of simultaneous presence, the mechanics of movement, and the effects of divided attention or belonging remain unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 26

- Soul-projection requires safeguards for both the physical body and metaphysical tether because a nonfatal failure can still cause profound disability or lasting injury.

- Projection healers may need to treat the body, tether, memory, and soul as related but distinct parts of reintegration.

- Forced return prevents routine voluntary stranding while preserving serious risk when the route home is damaged.

- Demidomain traditions can travel with displaced peoples instead of being cosmologically erased whenever they lose ancestral land or buildings.

- Reanchoring remains culturally and emotionally costly because material places and rites concentrate real relationships even though they are not individually indispensable.

- Networks allow extended families and communities to sustain plural ancestral centers without producing a centralized divine afterlife.

- The loss of clarity at scale naturally limits empires, states, and mass religions from converting demidomains into effortless continent-wide communication systems.

- Local identity can remain strong within a connected network, supporting differing household traditions, ancestors, memories, and authority.

- Soul sovereignty prevents competing institutions from resolving claims through ownership, seniority, or raw power alone.

- Plural belonging is possible without requiring every domain to support the same topology or social rules.

- A soul may disappoint, reject, or leave communities that sincerely claim it, making afterlife relationships reciprocal rather than guaranteed rewards for the living.

- The next foundational inquiry should address how souls first arise during embodied life, which living beings can possess them, and whether soul formation occurs at one moment or through gradual development.

## Decision 27: Universal living spirits and gradually emergent souls

- Spirit and soul are distinct metaphysical features of living existence.

- Every living organism automatically possesses a spirit, regardless of its size, complexity, intelligence, awareness, magical ability, mobility, or lifespan.

- An organism’s spirit is the metaphysical impression of its life force.

- Spirit in this sense does not automatically imply personhood, self-awareness, memory, agency after death, or eligibility for an afterlife domain.

- A soul is a much more personal and individualized metaphysical construct.

- A soul emerges as the metaphysical aspect of a living self-pattern once that life develops sufficient sustained coherence and individual continuity.

- No deity, planetary spirit, domain, or external reservoir must manufacture and insert the soul into the organism.

- A soul is not a preexisting person waiting to inhabit a new body.

- Soulhood follows coherent individuality rather than biological taxonomy.

- Non-sapient animals can and commonly do develop souls. Sapience, language, reflective thought, moral reasoning, and conscious magical practice are not prerequisites.

- Plants, fungi, colonies, distributed organisms, symbiotic systems, and unfamiliar forms of life may develop souls when a sustained individual self-pattern genuinely forms.

- Not every organism with a spirit necessarily develops enough individualized coherence to form a soul capable of personal continuation.

- Magical attunement may affect a living pattern but does not by itself create, prove, rank, or strengthen soulhood.

- Soul formation occurs gradually rather than at one universal instant.

- Conception, first cellular life, birth, hatching, germination, independence from a parent, first memory, and first self-awareness are not universal metaphysical switches shared by all life.

- Coherence deepens through biological development, experience, relationship, memory, self-maintenance, and the continued organization of the living pattern.

- When enough personal coherence has formed, death can leave a whole but vulnerable continuation of the individual.

- Earlier or less developed stages may possess a living spirit and an emerging soul-pattern without yet leaving the same kind of stable personal continuation after death.

- No simple external test, age, species label, developmental milestone, or cultural status has been established as a perfect measure of soul completion.

- The exact relationship between an organism’s universal spirit and its emerging soul remains unresolved.

- The fate of the organismal spirit at death, especially when a soul also survives, remains unresolved.

- How collective, colonial, symbiotic, ecological, and planetary spirits relate to individual organismal spirits and souls remains unresolved.

## Consequences and remaining unknowns from Decision 27

- Life possesses metaphysical significance at every scale without treating every living process as a separate immortal person.

- Personhood and afterlife continuity cannot be reduced to species membership, intelligence tests, language, magical talent, or social recognition.

- Animals can participate in soul bonds, afterlife relationships, domain reception, and spiritual harm without needing to resemble sapient peoples.

- The familiar system remains compatible with ordinary animals having souls while magical attunement stays a separate and rarer trait.

- Plants, fungi, colonies, and alien ecologies require investigation of actual individuality rather than automatic inclusion or exclusion.

- Gradual soul formation avoids one universal developmental boundary but creates genuine uncertainty around early death, incomplete formation, and the survival of emerging patterns.

- Different cultures may define the beginning of soulhood at conception, birth, naming, first speech, initiation, magical awakening, or another milestone without any tradition being automatically correct.

- Religious and political claims about who possesses a soul can be powerful, harmful, and factually mistaken.

- Spirit magic may interact with the universal life-force impression of organisms without automatically granting access to their personal souls.

- Soul magic must distinguish the continuing individual from the more universal spirit possessed by all life.

- The established planetary spirit may be related to living spirits while remaining a much larger layered planetary self. The exact relationship and terminology require clarification.

- The next decisions should define how spirit and soul coexist within one living being, what happens to spirit at death, and whether collectives can develop spirits or souls beyond those of their members.

## Decision 28: Differentiated souls, returning spirits, and exceptional collective personhood

### Canon established

- A living being that develops a soul retains both spirit and soul.

- The soul differentiates within and organizes part of the living spirit. It does not arrive from outside, replace the spirit, or consume it.

- The spirit remains the universal metaphysical impression of life. The soul becomes the coherent personal pattern capable of independent continuation.

- During embodied life, spirit and soul interpenetrate and affect one another. Their exact internal boundary need not be simple or fully understood.

- At death, their ordinary outcomes diverge.

- The organism's spirit rapidly returns to the local living ecology and the planetary spirit.

- The returning spirit carries the impression of that life, but it has no independent personal identity or agency.

- This return contributes the organism's lived pattern to the ecological and planetary whole.

- If the soul has developed enough coherence, it separates at death as a whole but vulnerable continuation of the person.

- The soul's personal continuation does not require the organismal spirit to remain attached.

- A living collective can develop an emergent spirit through sustained relationships, mutual influence, and coherent activity.

- A collective spirit is not automatically a collective person or collective soul.

- A true collective soul requires an exceptionally coherent individual identity and continuity at the scale of the whole.

- True collective souls are extraordinarily rare. Planetary spirits are the clearest established example because they integrate planetary coherence, living systems, magic, history, returned spirits, and received souls across millions of years.

- A mature planetary spirit can therefore possess collective personhood while retaining the distributed, slow awareness already established for planetary spirits.

- Individual souls that have melded into a planetary spirit do not regain separate agency, although their memories and identity-patterns remain within the whole.

- Smaller ecosystems and living collectives can possess collective spirits without possessing collective souls.

- The exact threshold at which a vast collective spirit becomes a true collective soul remains unresolved.

### Consequences and boundaries

- Soul is a differentiated personal organization within life's metaphysical pattern, not a foreign substance added to life.

- Spirit magic can affect vitality, ecological relationship, and the impressions carried by life without necessarily controlling a soul.

- Soul magic addresses the continuing person and therefore requires different concepts, methods, and safeguards.

- Death distributes a life along two related paths: its spirit returns quickly to ecological and planetary systems, while its soul may continue through domainless existence, a demidomain, or an afterlife domain.

- The planetary spirit receives ordinary organismal impressions and whole returned souls in different modes. Their contribution to the planetary whole should not be treated as identical.

- A forest, colony, family, community, or other living relational system may develop a real collective spirit without becoming a single person.

- No institution, nation, or large group should be assumed to possess a collective soul merely because it is old, powerful, or culturally unified.

- Planetary collective personhood emerges through immense duration and integration, not size alone.

- Whether every mature planetary spirit eventually becomes a collective soul, what happens if its world dies, and what lesser collective spirits can deliberately do remain open.

## Decision 29: Emergent planetary personhood, planetary death, and lesser collective agency

### Canon established

- A planetary spirit does not become a true collective soul at one universal event or threshold.

- Collective personhood emerges gradually as life, returned organismal spirits, received souls, memory, magic, history, and planetary self-regulation accumulate and interact.

- The relevant development is increasing coherence and continuity at the scale of the whole, not age, mass, population, or soul count by itself.

- Different living worlds can therefore develop planetary personhood along different paths and at different rates.

- There may be no externally observable instant at which a planetary spirit becomes a collective soul. The transition may be more like the gradual formation of an individual soul during embodied development.

- A mature planetary collective soul remains deeply integrated with its physical world, living systems, metaphysical history, and accumulated relationships.

- Permanent loss of life or destruction of the physical world does not necessarily extinguish the planetary spirit immediately.

- After gradual planetary death, accumulated coherence can preserve the planetary spirit for a time, but it ordinarily enters a slow dissolution as the systems and relationships that sustained it disappear.

- Sudden planetary destruction can wound, disorient, or fragment the planetary spirit rather than producing an orderly decline.

- Survival after planetary death is temporary unless some exceptional new source of coherence, relationship, or support intervenes. No such general preservation mechanism is currently established.

- A dying or disembodied planetary spirit does not automatically become an afterlife domain.

- Lesser living collectives can develop real collective spirits without developing collective souls.

- These spirits can exert diffuse tendencies through ecology, shared behavior, resonance, and magic.

- A lesser collective spirit lacks one continuous personal viewpoint, unified agency, and an independently continuing self.

- Its influence emerges through the relationships and living patterns that constitute it rather than through decisions made by a single hidden mind.

- A lesser collective spirit has no independent afterlife. If the sustaining collective dissolves, its coherence and influence also disperse or return into larger living and planetary systems.

- People may correctly perceive the presence or influence of a lesser collective spirit while incorrectly interpreting it as a speaking person, deity, ancestor, or singular will.

### Consequences and boundaries

- Planetary personhood is emergent and developmental, preserving the distinction between a universal living spirit and the rare formation of a collective soul.

- Planetary spirits are neither immortal by definition nor reducible to the immediate physical condition of the planet.

- A world can be physically dead while its planetary spirit is still declining, wounded, or fragmenting.

- A destroyed world's spiritual remains could create long-lived metaphysical consequences without automatically forming a ghost planet, god, or afterlife realm.

- Collective-spirit influence can be real without implying secret intentional control of an ecosystem or society.

- Practitioners may interact with a collective tendency through ritual, ecology, magic, or reciprocal behavior, but conversation with it may reflect interpretation rather than contact with a unified speaker.

- Worship alone does not automatically give a lesser collective spirit a soul, personhood, or divine status.

- The boundaries between strengthening a collective spirit, awakening collective personhood, and creating a new governing presence remain unresolved.

- The next decisions should clarify whether lesser collective spirits can be deliberately shaped, how their influence differs from an individual spirit's agency, and what exceptional processes might preserve or transform a dying planetary spirit.

## Decision 30: Shaping collective spirits and preserving dying worlds

### Canon established

- A lesser collective spirit can be deliberately shaped by changing the living collective that produces it.

- Changes in relationships, behavior, ecology, membership, patterns of cooperation, and recurring activity gradually change the collective spirit because they change its underlying living reality.

- Sustained rituals, traditions, and shared magical practices can reinforce selected tendencies within a collective spirit.

- Ritual reinforcement does not by itself grant the collective spirit personhood, a soul, or a unified will.

- Powerful magic can impose temporary structure or tendencies directly upon a lesser collective spirit.

- An imposed structure decays when its supporting magic ends unless the living collective adopts and sustains the corresponding relationships and patterns.

- Prolonged conflict between an imposed structure and the collective's actual life becomes unstable and can damage the collective, its members, its ecology, or the spirit itself.

- A lesser collective spirit expresses influence by biasing compatible ecological events, instincts, moods, magical effects, and patterns of coordination.

- It cannot ordinarily select a precise outcome, formulate an independent plan, or direct the collective as a hidden person.

- Its influence is strongest where several compatible tendencies already exist and weakest where the attempted outcome contradicts the collective's actual relationships or conditions.

- Surviving life and communities from a dying world can preserve portions of their planetary spirit's coherence through continuing reciprocal bonds.

- Such preservation carries genuine continuity, but a surviving people, species, ecosystem, or tradition should not automatically be treated as containing the complete planetary person.

- A sufficiently powerful and willing host world can gradually receive a dying planetary spirit.

- This reception is integration rather than simple relocation. The relationship transforms both planetary spirits and creates a new planetary condition whose exact identity depends on the process.

- A damaged planetary spirit can fragment. Its fragments may persist as lesser collective spirits, ecological inheritances, magical regions, resonant remains, or other partial continuities.

- Different fragments may preserve different tendencies, memories, relationships, or aspects of the former planetary whole without each becoming the complete original person.

- A genuinely godlike being or magical civilization operating at comparable power can preserve a complete planetary person indefinitely without changing it.

- Complete unchanged preservation requires truly exceptional power on a godlike scale. It is not a normal extension of mortal magic, ordinary divine intervention, cultural remembrance, or afterlife-domain maintenance.

- These outcomes are possibilities rather than automatic stages. A dying planetary spirit may experience one, several, or none of them.

### Consequences and boundaries

- Collective-spirit cultivation works most durably through lived systems rather than symbolic control alone.

- Ritual and magic can accelerate, focus, or distort a collective tendency, but lasting transformation normally requires corresponding change within the collective.

- The probabilistic nature of collective influence permits real spiritual causation without giving every forest, community, or ecosystem a secret commanding intelligence.

- Attempts to manufacture a compliant collective spirit through force can create ecological, social, magical, and metaphysical harm.

- Planetary death can leave descendants, fragments, regions, and traditions with real metaphysical continuity while preserving the finality of losing the integrated world.

- Integration with a host planet is not resurrection into an interchangeable vessel. It permanently changes the host and the received planetary spirit.

- Complete preservation is possible but sits near the setting's highest established power ceiling and should remain extraordinarily rare.

- Whether the dying planetary spirit must consent to integration or preservation, how planetary fragments relate to received souls, and what godlike preservation costs remain unresolved.

- The next decisions should distinguish collective-spirit magic from domination, establish consent around planetary integration, and define the power source and cost of complete planetary preservation.

## Decision 31: Collective consent, planetary integration, and preservation burdens

### Canon established

- Legitimate cultivation of a lesser collective spirit works through voluntary relationships and real changes within the living collective.

- Practices, rituals, ecological interventions, and magical traditions cultivate the spirit when the affected members and living systems actually adopt and sustain the underlying relationships.

- Metaphysical domination imposes tendencies that the collective's members and ecology do not sustain through their own living relationships.

- The distinction depends on the relationship between the imposed pattern and the collective's actual life, not merely on whether magic was used.

- A cultivated tendency can still be ethically disputed or harmful. Legitimacy at the metaphysical level does not guarantee wisdom, justice, or universal approval.

- When both a dying planetary spirit and a prospective host world possess collective souls, both planetary persons must consent before integration can occur.

- Compatibility, proximity, desperation, or overwhelming power cannot substitute for either planetary person's consent.

- Consent makes integration possible but does not prevent transformation. Both planetary persons and the resulting planetary condition are changed by the union.

- The consent requirements for a host world that has a living planetary spirit but has not developed collective personhood remain unresolved.

- Complete, unchanged preservation of a planetary person requires an ongoing source of immense power rather than a single ordinary act.

- One viable structure is a godlike preserver that continuously commits part of its own power and remains responsible for sustaining the preserved planetary person.

- The preserver's continuing responsibility is real and cannot be reduced to placing the planetary spirit inside an inert container.

- A second viable structure is a vast consensual network of powerful souls that distributes the continuing burden.

- The network must possess genuinely immense combined power. Large numbers alone do not make weak or symbolic participation equivalent to godlike capacity.

- The preserving network does not become a single collective soul merely because its members cooperate in sustaining the planetary person.

- Both preservation structures maintain the complete planetary person without absorbing, rewriting, or integrating it into the preserver.

- If continuing support fails, the preserved planetary person again becomes vulnerable to decline, injury, fragmentation, or dissolution.

### Consequences and boundaries

- Collective-spirit magic cannot become legitimate merely by describing coercion as harmony, tradition, or the will of the whole.

- Because cultivation operates through lived relationships, durable change remains accountable to the actual collective rather than solely to a priesthood, ruler, or practitioner.

- Planetary integration is a reciprocal union between persons when both worlds possess collective souls, not rescue performed upon a passive object.

- Planetary consent is metaphysically necessary but does not settle the separate ethical and political interests of the worlds' living inhabitants.

- Complete preservation creates a continuing obligation and dependency rather than a cost-free escape from planetary death.

- A godlike preserver may remain an individual custodian, while a soul network distributes power and responsibility without erasing its members' individuality.

- The preservation mechanism differs from an afterlife domain, governing lattice, planetary integration, and the ordinary survival of fragments.

- Whether individual members of a preserving network may withdraw, how replacement members join, and what happens during partial support failure remain unresolved.

- The next decisions should define consent for non-personal planetary spirits, the rights of living populations during planetary integration, and the governance and failure behavior of preservation networks.

## Decision 32: Distributed planetary response, inhabitant influence, and revocable preservation

### Canon established

- A living planetary spirit that has not developed collective personhood cannot provide personal consent because no unified planetary person yet exists.

- Such a world is not metaphysically inert. Its distributed spirit expresses compatibility or resistance through its living systems, ecological relationships, magical patterns, accumulated history, and planetary coherence.

- Integration can proceed only where the host spirit is sufficiently compatible and does not successfully resist the incoming planetary spirit.

- This compatibility is an emergent planetary response rather than a hidden personal decision.

- Power cannot simply substitute for compatibility without changing the process into forceful damage, domination, or destruction rather than consensual planetary integration.

- When both planetary spirits possess collective souls, their reciprocal personal consent remains required as established previously.

- The living populations of an integrating world do not possess a single universal veto that functions as an additional metaphysical consent gate.

- Their support, resistance, relationships, migrations, ecological practices, magical actions, and survival materially affect whether integration succeeds.

- Living populations also help determine what the integrated planetary condition becomes because they participate in the relationships and systems through which planetary coherence is sustained.

- Agreement between planetary persons does not make inhabitant suffering irrelevant or guarantee that every community experiences the integration as legitimate.

- A planetary preservation network depends upon the continuing consent of every participating soul.

- Each soul may withdraw immediately and does not require permission, advance notice, or justification.

- Withdrawal ends that soul's continuing contribution. It does not permanently confiscate power already committed from the departing soul.

- When a participant withdraws, the preservation burden redistributes among the souls that remain willing and connected.

- If the remaining network retains sufficient power and coherence, preservation continues under greater strain or reduced reserve.

- If remaining capacity becomes insufficient, preservation weakens or fails, leaving the preserved planetary person vulnerable to decline, injury, fragmentation, or dissolution.

- The right to withdraw is not suspended by the severity of those consequences.

### Consequences and boundaries

- A pre-personal planetary spirit has meaningful metaphysical boundaries without being treated as a person capable of speech, preference, or informed consent.

- Compatibility and resistance keep integration ecological and relational rather than reducing an unawakened world to vacant metaphysical territory.

- Planetary consent and inhabitant participation operate at different levels. Neither makes the other irrelevant.

- An integration opposed by much of a world's life may remain metaphysically possible but become unstable, violent, ethically catastrophic, or radically different from what the planetary persons intended.

- Preservation networks cannot become coercive prisons merely because withdrawal creates existential danger for the preserved planetary person.

- Network durability must come from sufficient willing capacity, redundancy, replacement, trust, and preparation rather than irrevocable bondage.

- Immediate withdrawal creates a genuine structural risk that distinguishes distributed preservation from preservation sustained by one godlike custodian.

- The exact signs and timetable of insufficient support, the process for joining or replacing members, and whether emergency stabilization can delay failure remain unresolved.

- The next decisions should define how distributed planetary compatibility becomes perceptible, how inhabitants can practically support or resist integration, and how preservation networks manage reserve capacity and failure.

## Decision 33: Reading planetary response, population intervention, and preservation resilience

### Canon established

- Practitioners recognize a pre-personal planetary spirit's compatibility or resistance through converging ecological, magical, geological, and spiritual responses.

- No single omen, measurement, magical sensation, species response, geological event, or spiritual manifestation is decisive by itself.

- Reliable interpretation requires patterns across several systems, sustained observation, and practitioners capable of distinguishing planetary response from ordinary variation or the effects of the attempted integration.

- Different worlds can express compatibility and resistance differently because their ecologies, magic, history, and planetary coherence are not interchangeable.

- Interpretation remains fallible. Expertise can improve confidence without turning distributed response into direct conversation with a hidden planetary person.

- Living populations can support integration through coordinated rituals that establish compatible anchors between the planetary patterns.

- They can resist integration through coordinated rituals that establish metaphysical, ecological, or magical boundaries.

- Populations can also respond through migration, refuge-building, and selective withdrawal from affected regions.

- These movements alter which relationships, communities, and living systems participate in the integration and can preserve populations that reject or cannot survive particular regional effects.

- Living populations can directly attack either planetary spirit through sufficiently powerful magic.

- An attack may obstruct, injure, destabilize, or redirect integration, but it risks harming the world, its inhabitants, the incoming planetary person, or the host spirit whose systems sustain the attackers.

- Direct attack is a possible intervention, not an inherently legitimate or precise one.

- A mature planetary preservation network maintains substantial reserve capacity beyond the minimum required for stable preservation.

- It uses overlapping contributors and trains willing replacements so ordinary departures do not immediately threaten the preserved planetary person.

- The network can enter staged degradation when capacity falls. Peripheral functions are reduced or suspended before the planetary person suffers direct injury.

- Staged degradation is a protective operating condition, not permission to rewrite, consume, or deliberately fragment the preserved planetary person.

- Emergency contributors may voluntarily supply temporary power to stabilize the network without accepting permanent membership or commitment.

- Emergency participation ends according to its own consent and cannot be converted into an irrevocable obligation.

- Redundancy, staged degradation, and emergency stabilization reduce risk but cannot guarantee survival if willing capacity remains insufficient.

### Consequences and boundaries

- Planetary diagnosis becomes a multidisciplinary and culturally variable practice rather than a single magical test.

- Conflicting interpretations are plausible because the evidence is distributed, changing, and affected by the integration process itself.

- Anchors, boundaries, migration, and refuge can make different regions participate in the same planetary event in very different ways.

- Direct attacks upon planetary spirits create a credible route for warfare, sabotage, desperate defense, and catastrophic misunderstanding at planetary scale.

- A preservation network should be designed to survive normal turnover without weakening the right of any soul to leave immediately.

- Staged degradation creates warning states and time for response before direct harm reaches the preserved planetary person.

- Temporary emergency support allows crisis cooperation without disguising short-term consent as permanent service.

- The distinction between peripheral support functions and the preserved person's own integrity requires further definition.

- The next decisions should define the time and evidence needed for a credible planetary diagnosis, the behavior of regional anchors and boundaries, and the stages and visible consequences of preservation-network degradation.
