> This reference contains open possibilities and selected working models. An option being discussed is not automatically established canon.

Purpose

This document prevents settled questions from being accidentally reopened and organizes the unresolved choices that should drive the next interactive sessions. It also identifies evolutionary branch points through which the planet’s wildlife can be designed without copying Earth species.

## How to use this map

The scientific canon ledger governs settled numerical and conceptual decisions. The evolutionary synthesis governs the preferred causal explanation. This map records only genuine unknowns, recommended next questions, and tests that could force revision.

A topic should be reopened only if a new answer creates a contradiction, numerical modeling rejects the current range, or the user deliberately supersedes it.

## Questions already closed

The planet is a roughly 3-Earth-mass, 1.4-Earth-radius rocky super-Earth with surface gravity near 1.53 g.

It is synchronously rotating around an old quiet early-M star.

The atmosphere is roughly 1.5 bar, nitrogen-dominated, and 14–15 percent oxygen by volume.

The water inventory is roughly 0.15–0.20 Earth oceans.

Hostile central-day and deep-night surface ecologies are selected. The 4,500 km corridor and its 3,900 km dayward/600 km nightward partition are provisional mapping targets, not model-derived biological boundaries. Nightside large-animal absence depends jointly on cold, food, and accessible water; the stated temperatures alone are not universally lethal to cold-adapted Earth endotherms.

Photosynthesis supplies nearly all global primary production. Chemosynthesis dominates only small isolated systems.

The native dwarf-bearing clade is bilateral, internally skeletonized, and ancestrally six-limbed. All native dwarf species inherit four arms and two legs. Adults are habitual bipeds with upper precision-biased arms and lower power-and-bracing arms, retaining climbing and occasional crouched multi-limb support. This supersedes the former four-limbed dwarf-bearing-clade assumption.

The native dwarves evolved naturally over deep time and form a diversified genus of five species and six recognized forms.

The Thal catastrophe occurred on Erde. It is not a planetary catastrophe and did not cause native dwarf evolution.

Dwarven civilization strongly prefers underground construction, relies principally on hydropower, uses fuel engines and selective electricity, and supports major settlements hundreds of metres to roughly two kilometres below the surface.

## Highest-priority biological validation

### Terrestrial reproduction

The early terrestrial six-limbed clade needs embryos protected from desiccation, with adequate respiratory and waste-handling surfaces; it need not reproduce without water in the physiological sense. Enclosed egg laying is the ancestral working route. The brood-geode model below is the preferred provisional dwarven endpoint after the user’s stone-reproduction and six-limb decisions. The remaining priority is to quantify it, not repeatedly default to live birth. Alternatives are retained for comparison rather than silently reinstated.

Comparative alternative 1. Enclosed ancestral eggs followed by derived internal gestation in dwarves.

As a comparative alternative, live birth provides substantial reproductive flexibility: an egg-laying ancestral radiation can later evolve egg retention, yolk-fueled live birth, and eventually placenta-like exchange. If this route were chosen, a small number of dependent infants would be a reasonable hypothesis. It is not the current preferred dwarven endpoint; that remains brood-geode incubation with post-hatching parental care.

Option 2. Internal gestation evolves before the major terrestrial radiation and is ancestral to most large six-limbed animals.

This is possible but makes much of the familiar large fauna live-bearing and narrows later reproductive diversity. It moves the difficult evolutionary transition very early and requires a convincing ancestral physiological mechanism.

Option 3. Most species retain aquatic larvae and must return to water.

This works for amphibious radiations but conflicts with the established dry, high-mountain, volcanic, and deep-cave dwarven range unless the dwarf branch later evolves a fully water-independent embryo. It is the weakest fit for the current setting.

### Stone-dependent reproduction and maturation concepts

Status

The following are scientifically screened brainstorming options, not settled canon.

### Biological boundary

Stone cannot supply the embryo or juvenile with metabolic energy. Calories must still come from yolk, maternal transfer, milk-like or crop-like secretions, parental food, or later feeding.

Stone can nevertheless be biologically indispensable as a source of calcium, phosphate, iron, magnesium, trace elements, or toxin-binding clay; as a porous gas-exchange structure; as thermal and humidity buffering; as physical protection; as a substrate for inherited microbes; or as the mechanical environment in which the juvenile skeleton develops.

A genus-wide dependency should be based on properties that many rocks can provide, or on a portable prepared composite, rather than one unique mineral deposit. Otherwise the dependency would prevent the dwarven genus from colonizing its established range.

### Concept A: Lithic crèche after live birth

Plausibility: strong analogues for protected postnatal care; obligate dependence on stone and the exact alien life history remain speculative.

A dwarf gives birth to one extremely altricial infant after a relatively short or intermediate internal gestation. The infant has functional organs but incomplete skeletal ossification, weak temperature control, and limited mobility. It spends its first intensive growth phase in a carved porous-stone cradle or nursery alcove.

The cradle buffers temperature and humidity, protects the infant from falls under 1.53 g, allows controlled ventilation, and can hold removable mineral-rich and microbially seeded liners. Parents provide all calories through nursing, glandular secretions, or prepared food.

This makes stone central to maturation without asking the infant to obtain energy from rock. It also supports large adult brains by shifting substantial growth beyond birth and reduces the mass a pregnant parent must carry.

The principal costs are prolonged parental care, vulnerability of stationary infants, infection risk in reused cradles, and dependence on secure settlements. Natural selection would favor strong family or communal defense, careful cleaning and reseeding, and few offspring at a time.

### Concept B: Near-term egg in a stone incubator

Plausibility: supported components, but prolonged egg retention needs a compatible maternal gas-exchange mechanism.

Internal fertilization is followed by prolonged egg retention. The parent carries the embryo through much of development, then lays one large mineralized egg in a prepared stone chamber shortly before hatching.

The shell remains porous enough for oxygen and carbon-dioxide exchange. It protects the embryo, limits water loss, and supplies some calcium to the developing skeleton. The surrounding stone chamber provides the stable temperature and humidity that the shell cannot regulate alone.

Natural geothermal warmth, warm groundwater, compost-like microbial heat, or later engineered hydronic systems can incubate the egg. Earth megapodes demonstrate that vertebrate eggs can evolve to rely on environmental rather than parental body heat.

This option makes dwarves clearly nonmammalian while retaining a gradual evolutionary path from ordinary egg laying through longer egg retention. The hatchling can still be highly dependent and undergo years of post-hatching brain growth.

A large retained egg is costly to provision and carry. Prolonged retention can impose maternal respiratory and mechanical costs, and high gravity does not by itself favor laying late. This alternative requires a thin or otherwise exchange-compatible shell during retention, with final shell properties established at the appropriate stage. Small clutches and protected nests are plausible but not deductions from gravity alone.

### Concept C: Biocemented brood geode

Plausibility: a scientifically motivated composite of known mechanisms. The complete brood-geode system has no demonstrated Earth analogue and should not be rated as established or highly probable.

The parent lays a yolk-rich enclosed egg in a selected mineral cup, then completes a protective mineral-organic casing around a ventilated gap. Parent-produced adhesive or biomineralized binder is the basic construction mechanism; exterior calcifying microbes can assist where their chemistry is safe, without being obligatory to every load-bearing joint. The true eggshell and vascular respiratory membranes remain the embryo’s physiological boundary. The external geode is a constructed nursery, analogous to an elaborate nest, rather than living maternal tissue or an external placenta.

The casing is heterogeneous: a dense lower cradle and irregular buttresses resist loads, while sheltered upper and lateral panels contain short connected gas-filled pores. The true egg and its inner support remain mechanically distinct. Drainage and a protected entry geometry reduce flooding; fine meshes, moisture films, and biofilms are included only where their additional diffusion resistance is tolerable. Beneficial microbes are maintained mainly on the casing or transferable nursery surfaces, not assumed to form a safe, oxygen-consuming blanket over the egg.

The simplest credible nursery has enough passive gas conductance for late incubation and a parent-accessible ventilated lid. Parents keep external channels clear and maintain the surrounding microclimate. Staged pore caps and embryo-controlled mineral excavation are optional later specializations, not required solutions. Ventilating the chamber does not overcome a diffusion-limited eggshell or a long water-filled pore. Peak oxygen uptake, carbon-dioxide clearance, water loss, and metabolic heat must be checked for the whole casing–gap–eggshell system.

The true egg contains yolk, water, vascular gas-exchange membranes, and an isolated allantois-like waste compartment using sufficiently concentrated or precipitated nitrogen waste. A suitable primary eggshell can supply embryonic calcium through evolved membrane transport. External casing minerals are not automatically accessible across the air gap and eggshell: the conservative version supplies those minerals after hatching, dissolved or finely prepared in food. An accessible weak lid or fracture seam permits emergence and parental assistance. Moisture-buffering supports must remain compatible with drainage and gas exchange.

Mineral safety depends on native physiology. Long selection can produce element-specific transporters, binding proteins, exclusion and excretion systems, resistant enzymes, and helpful microbes. A substrate harmful to Homo may be tolerated or useful here. The same chemical principles still apply: dose, chemical species, exposure route, developmental stage, and interactions with nutrients matter. Detoxification has energetic and physiological costs, and embryonic tolerance must be evaluated separately from adult tolerance.

Inherited smell, taste, texture, and moisture preferences can favor successful nursery materials before sapience. They need not identify every toxic trace contaminant. Repeated nest success, avoidance of unfamiliar deposits, social learning, and later preparation and assay traditions improve reliability. Hundreds of thousands of years of culture can make parents highly knowledgeable within familiar mineral systems while leaving rare contamination, novel deposits, and preparation errors possible.

The model remains viable as a candidate if food, gas exchange, humidity, temperature, microbial control, structural safety, waste storage, and hatching each work at the proposed scale. A small altricial hatchling and bounded incubation lower the burden; neither low clutch size nor parental care proves the budgets close. Earlier relatives must breed successfully in simpler, naturally sheltered mineral nests, without sapient engineering.

### Concept D: Shell recycling and controlled geophagy

Plausibility: strong evidence for primary-eggshell mineral use; external stone feeding is conditional on chemistry and preparation.

The embryo can mobilize useful minerals from its primary eggshell. After hatching, caregivers may supply cleaned, dissolved, softened, or finely prepared portions of compatible shell and nursery material with ordinary food. Whole sharp fragments are not a required diet, and indiscriminate clay feeding can bind useful nutrients as well as toxins.

The material supports skeletal and dental growth, supplies trace nutrients, and can adsorb some plant toxins. It does not need to be swallowed as stones; it can be biologically softened, ground, fermented, or dissolved before ingestion.

This behavior creates a direct connection between the individual’s first stone and its adult skeleton. It could also explain why a fragment of the brood stone becomes a powerful family object.

Chemical control remains necessary, but the relevant standard is compatibility with native dwarf physiology rather than an Erde list of “toxic metals.” Over tens of millions of years the dwarf-bearing lineage and its nursery symbionts can evolve selective uptake, binding, immobilization, detoxification, and excretion systems for locally common elements. Every biochemistry still has harmful doses and chemical forms, so nonsapient parents first evolve reliable sensory preferences for suitable substrates. Later dwarven cultures turn those preferences into accumulated mineral knowledge, preparation methods, assays, and inherited brood-stone traditions.

### Concept E: Nursery-stone symbiosis

Plausibility: supported analogues for parental and social microbial transmission; exact organisms, dependence, and benefits remain open.

A prepared brood stone carries a maintained exterior microbial community. Parents transfer useful partners through controlled contact, skin secretions, and feeding after emergence. The conservative model concerns gut and skin communities; deliberate inoculation of the hatchling’s airways is not required. A simple parental binder and safe nursery can function before specialized microbial partners evolve.

These symbionts could help digest tough producer tissues and cultivated fungi, synthesize nutrients, bind metals, exclude pathogens, or regulate early immune development. A juvenile raised without the correct community might survive poorly even when adequately fed.

This makes the stone a hereditary ecological organ: genes come from the parents, while essential microbial partners pass through the prepared nursery. Social and environmental transmission of obligate or beneficial microbes has real biological precedents.

The dependence should be on a maintained biofilm rather than microbes found only in one cave. Dwarves can carry starter cultures to new settlements, allowing migration while making destruction of nursery stones a serious demographic threat.

### Concept F: Extended postnatal skeletal hardening

Plausibility: strong analogues for continued postnatal ossification and load-responsive bone; the proposed degree remains bounded.

Dwarven infants are born with a functional but comparatively cartilage-rich skeleton. Secondary ossification, trabecular thickening, joint shaping, and tendon reinforcement continue during a prolonged protected nursery period.

Mineral-rich feeding supplies raw materials, while carefully increasing mechanical load directs bone architecture. The stone nursery provides graded supports, textured crawling surfaces, slopes, handholds, and resistance features suited to 1.53 g.

The infant cannot be nearly boneless or helpless for many years. High gravity would make severe under-mineralization dangerous even in a cradle. The credible version exaggerates normal postnatal bone development rather than replacing it with a metamorphic hardening event.

This mechanism can produce compact adults whose bones are strong because of geometry, internal microstructure, and load-guided remodeling, not because their bodies contain literal rock.

### Concept G: Adolescent stone maturation

Plausibility: Moderate as a secondary stage; low as a complete metamorphosis.

Puberty could produce a temporary rise in mineral appetite, bone remodeling, tooth replacement, symbiont turnover, or heat requirements. Adolescents spend weeks or months in mineral-spring chambers, warm stone dormitories, or supervised low-light retreats while receiving mineral-rich food and controlled exercise.

This would create a biologically grounded coming-of-age association with stone. It could be especially pronounced in volcanic and high-mountain species.

A sealed mineral cocoon, radical adult metamorphosis, or months of total immobility would be difficult to justify for a large-brained humanoid. The credible version is an intensified growth and remodeling interval, not pupation.

### Concept H: External stone-wall nutrient exchange

Plausibility: Speculative.

A thin-shelled embryo attaches to a wet porous wall and develops temporary exchange filaments that absorb water, ions, oxygen, and small dissolved nutrients from a parent-maintained seep. This would be an external analogue of a simple placenta.

It could evolve from an amphibious cave ancestor whose eggs already exchanged materials with water. However, maintaining sterility, preventing uncontrolled ion uptake, and supplying enough organic nutrients are difficult. This should be used only if a genuinely alien reproductive system is worth accepting lower plausibility.

### Recommended combined model

The preferred stone-dependent model combines a constructed porous nursery, controlled mineral recycling, transmissible beneficial microbes, and ordinary prolonged juvenile growth. This preserves endpoint 3 while minimizing novel physiological requirements. The dependence is primarily on a nest structure and prepared mineral materials; it need not include direct external mineral uptake by the embryo, obligatory microbial cement, or a new placental organ. Its components are grounded; their integration at dwarf scale remains a testable worldbuilding hypothesis.

The ancestral clade lays enclosed eggs. A later dwarf-parent lineage specializes in sheltered mineral nesting and progressively better constructed nurseries while retaining external embryonic development. Earlier laying may evolve relative to some egg-retaining ancestors, but is not an obligatory stage or a way to reduce fully provisioned egg volume. Other land-animal branches remain reproductively diverse.

The parent produces one yolk-provisioned egg with respiratory and waste-storage membranes and lays it into a prepared mineral cup. Organic or biomineralizing secretions bind selected grains into a casing with a ventilated gap. Exterior microbial assistance is facultative in the conservative model. The embryo obtains energy from yolk and may absorb minerals from its primary eggshell; prepared external nursery minerals become available through feeding after hatching.

Dense supports and thin porous panels separate mechanical protection from respiratory exchange. Short gas paths, a ventilated gap, drainage, compatible humidity buffering, and an accessible hatching lid are the baseline. The panels must accommodate late-incubation oxygen use and carbon-dioxide removal without excessive water loss; meshes, wet films, and biofilms add resistance. Parent maintenance improves reliability but does not repeal the transport limits.

Native mineral adaptation and accumulated parental knowledge strongly support the model. Substrate choice is specific to dwarf enzymes, transporters, nutritional requirements, and microbial partners rather than a copied Homo toxin list. It remains element- and dose-specific. An adult-safe mineral is not necessarily embryo-safe, and instinct cannot detect every odorless contaminant. Local knowledge, conservative material selection, preparation, and cultural assays reduce these risks without making the parents infallible.

The hatchling is small, altricial, and capable of breathing and receiving food. Parents provide digestible organic food or evolved nutritive secretions, safe prepared mineral supplements, warmth, and microbial transmission. It remains in the opened nursery while skeletal, digestive, immune, motor, and neural development continue. One egg per breeding attempt is the working low-clutch design; rare multi-egg clutches remain possible. Twin embryos within one egg are not treated as equivalent or routinely successful. Secure nesting and extended care precede later communal hatcheries.

A live-birth stone crèche remains a reasonable comparative alternative. The brood-geode design is retained as the preferred provisional direction because it meets the user’s stone-reproduction goal with an enclosed egg and an elaborate nest. Scientific strengthening does not require every optional microbial or active pore mechanism to become obligatory. Egg mass, incubation duration, feeding anatomy, and adult dimensions remain open rather than being inferred from the evocative word “geode.”

### Evolutionary sequence to reconstruct after selection

Aquatic ancestors attach eggs to sheltered mineral substrates with adequate water flow; microbe-rich mats are not automatically beneficial because respiration can deplete local oxygen.

Amphibious descendants bury eggs in damp porous sediment. Sticky egg coats trap selected grains, and parental chemosensory preferences for suitable sediments evolve before any complex nest construction.

Early terrestrial descendants evolve internal fertilization, large yolk reserves, water-retaining membranes, waste storage, and a porous biomineralized primary shell.

Mountain and cave-margin lineages choose sheltered stone nests. Parental secretions increasingly bind selected grains, improving protection while preserving an open respiratory route. Helpful exterior microbes may later become predictably transmitted; initial nesting success does not depend on a fully evolved microbial construction consortium.

A derived dwarf-parent lineage completes a protected mineral cup before laying and closes a ventilated secondary casing afterward. Thin respiratory panels, denser supports, drainage, and an accessible lid evolve through useful intermediate nests. Full construction competence precedes dependence on the finished casing; no stage requires parents to build a mechanically complete nursery before the behavior has evolved.

Selection can refine protected ventilation panels, parent-accessible lids, drainage, substrate choice, and cleaning behavior as eggs and investment change. Pores must remain short enough for useful diffusion; branching does not automatically increase conductance. Small clutches, dependable nest defense, and altricial hatchlings are plausible life-history responses to combined energy and survival pressures, not automatic effects of high gravity.

The hatchling remains altricial and continues development in the opened geode. Shell recycling, microbiome transfer, and load-guided skeletal maturation join incubation into one continuous stone-dependent reproductive cycle.

Only after these biological stages are established does sapient culture turn inherited substrate choice into explicit mineralogy, testing, standardized composites, managed starter cultures, and communal hatchery engineering.

### Honeycomb geometry as a dwarven design principle

Status: Selected as a recurring functional and cultural motif. Exact anatomical expressions remain subject to later design decisions.

The brood nursery offers a credible biological source for the motif. Respiratory panels may use rounded pores with approximately equal, locally hexagonal spacing and irregular cellular ribs. Thin skins must have aligned or connected openings; a stiff but sealed sandwich is not a breathing panel. Main loads pass through denser buttresses. Shell curvature requires nonhexagonal defects, and pore shape, size, spacing, strength, and conductance must be optimized together rather than assuming hexagons are universally strongest.

The pattern should be graded rather than mathematically perfect. Smaller cells can reinforce the edge of a respiratory field; larger cells increase conductance near its center; and slight irregularity, rounded junctions, mineral fibers, and offset layers can hinder a crack from propagating through the whole shell. A hatching seam can follow a deliberately weakened ring of cell boundaries.

Other biological uses should remain microscopic or regional. Epithelia, gland openings, shell plates, sensory units, or protective skin microtiles can develop rounded polygonal packing where similarly sized units crowd a surface. Trabecular bone may look cellular in places, but its struts must align with habitual stresses and remodel under 1.53 g rather than forming a universal hexagonal lattice. The dwarven body remains bilateral: three paired limbs are not sixfold radial symmetry. Cultures may associate the six extremities with nursery-cell imagery without implying a common developmental cause.

Dwarven engineering can generalize the nursery pattern wherever cellular construction is genuinely useful: light but stiff doors and floors, hatchery panels, ventilation grilles, filters, heat-exchanger matrices, furnace supports, acoustic baffles, sacrificial impact layers, and modular partitions. Circular light wells, wells, or ventilation shafts can be placed on a hexagonal lattice to cover an area evenly without making each shaft hexagonal.

At settlement scale, districts can use six-neighbor planning, offset chamber arrays, and honeycomb-like webs of retained rock pillars. Actual tunnel and cavern cross-sections should usually remain round, elliptical, or arched and must follow joints, faults, and principal stresses. Sharp regular hexagonal voids would create vulnerable corners in many rocks. In three dimensions, the design should become stacked or offset prismatic cells and irregular load-following networks rather than pretending that flat hexagons alone fill volume.

Culturally, the visible breathing fields of brood stones provide a compelling source for sixfold imagery. The cell can symbolize protected life; the shared wall, kinship and mutual support; the open pore, breath and exchange; and the repeated lattice, a settlement whose members strengthen one another. These meanings can appear in masonry, screens, textiles, clan marks, civic plans, ritual objects, and technical diagrams.

The motif should not imply an insect-like caste system, hive mind, compulsory groups of six, or identical architecture across every culture. Different dwarf species and regions can favor different proportions, levels of regularity, and materials while recognizing the same ancestral nursery pattern.

## Planetary values still to lock

### Star and orbit

Choose a specific stellar mass within roughly 0.4–0.6 solar masses.

Derive stellar luminosity from age and composition rather than selecting it independently.

Derive incident flux, distance, and synchronous period consistently from an age- and composition-compatible stellar model. Roughly 30–60 Earth days is a preferred scenario range that may change if the coupled climate requires it; it is not a result of an integration already performed.

Define the star’s quiescent ultraviolet spectrum, flare rate, and long-term activity history. “Old and quiet” means lower and less frequent activity than a young M dwarf, not no flares.

### Atmosphere and circulation

Choose exact nitrogen, oxygen, carbon-dioxide, water-vapor, methane, argon-like, and aerosol abundances.

Test whether the chosen greenhouse and clouds simultaneously support central-dayside temperatures of 340–375 K, deep-nightside temperatures of 205–230 K, and a viable surface corridor near 4,500 km.

Map the circulation’s principal ascent zones, rain belts, return flows, persistent clouds, dry subsidence zones, and mountain precipitation.

Determine whether any high-altitude or high-latitude atmospheric regions develop long-lived liquid clouds in which microbes could briefly become active.

### Water and ice

Partition the 0.15–0.20 Earth-ocean exchangeable inventory among lakes, rivers, soil, accessible groundwater, glaciers, nightside ice, and vapor. Budget deep bound mantle water separately and include fluxes between reservoirs. Check both present liquid-water distribution and long-term resistance to complete nightside sequestration.

Place the largest 50,000–150,000 square kilometre lakes where tectonic basins, inflow, evaporation, and drainage permit them.

Model nightside ice thickness, basal melting, glacial flow toward warmer margins, and the return path for water.

Quantify the recurrence distribution of subglacial outburst connections rather than treating every oasis as having the same flood interval.

### Interior and tectonics

Choose core size, mantle composition, radiogenic inventory, crustal thickness, geothermal gradient, and modern surface heat flow.

Identify when persistent subduction begins and how often the mobile-lid system reorganizes.

Map active arcs, extinct arcs, collision massifs, rifts, stable cratons, calderas, major faults, and sedimentary basins.

Test whether the preferred settlement depths encounter manageable temperature, water, stress, and gas conditions in the chosen regions.

### Moons and companion bodies

Recover or reconstruct the proposed compact moon architecture, then specify masses, densities, orbital radii, inclinations, and eccentricities.

Use orbital integrations plus dissipative tidal evolution to test stability, resonances, eccentricity damping, and satellite loss. Around the selected star-synchronous planet, ordinary prograde moons lie inside corotation and tend to migrate inward. Test uncertain tidal parameters over the system’s history; being inside the Hill sphere or surviving a short integration does not establish billion-year longevity.

Decide whether outer planets or small co-orbitals add meaningful long-period cycles. They should not be used as unexplained sources of tectonic power.

## Native biochemistry and producer branch points

### Photosynthetic machinery

Use a two-stage oxygenic visible/far-red pathway as the conservative working baseline. Consider additional serial photochemical stages only if a specified spectrum and photon budget justify their extra complexity; do not choose more stages merely because the star is redder.

Determine usable surface photon flux and native reflectance before choosing pigment colors. Black, green, purple, burgundy, and brown remain possible appearances; none follows uniquely from the star’s classification. Efficient infrared use beyond known far-red analogues remains an additional hypothesis.

Decide how phototrophs protect themselves from flares, oxidative stress, and excess dayside heat. Plausible strategies include repair cycles, shielding tissues, mineral coatings, vertical movement, and dormancy.

### Structural materials

Choose the main load-bearing materials used by producer organisms. Possibilities include cellulose-like polymers, chitin-like composites, silica reinforcement, carbonate structures, lignin analogues, or a novel mixed composite.

This choice sets decomposition rates, fire behavior, fungal enzymes, soil formation, crop processing, and the appearance of forests or scrub.

### Producer stature

Determine whether the tallest native producers are low dense thickets, columnar trunks, tension-supported fronds, interconnected clonal towers, or another high-gravity solution.

Large height should have a clear payoff in light competition or spore dispersal and a mechanically credible way to resist gravity and wind.

### Water-conducting architecture

Choose whether large producers use internal vessels, capillary sheets, contractile pumping, osmotic columns, or distributed water-storage tissues.

The permanent low-angle illumination near the terminator may favor broad vertical light-catching surfaces rather than an Earthlike horizontal leaf canopy.

## Major animal-phyla branch points

### Six-limbed internal-skeleton clade

This clade contains dwarves and can include many other large land and lake animals. Three ancestral limb pairs and the dwarven four-arm/two-leg arrangement are settled. Other fauna are not automatically reassigned to it. Decide which retain six walking limbs, which specialize particular pairs, and which reduce a pair. Skeletal mineral, digits, respiratory design, feeding apparatus, covering, and reproduction remain open.

Its internal diversity can include aquatic returns, amphibious forms, six-legged walkers, two-handed quadrupeds, four-handed bipeds, burrowers, climbers, and a possible four-legged flyer with a separate wing pair. An independently four-limbed lineage remains possible; its origin and relationship to this radiation need an explicit later decision.

### Many-appendaged clade

A separate lineage can produce the ecological analogues of arthropods, centipedes, or small burrowing crustaceans without copying their exact anatomy.

Key choices are whether appendages are serially repeated, whether support is external or internal, how growth occurs, and whether high gravity limits large forms through molting or respiration.

### Radial or modular suspension-feeder clade

Lakes and rivers can contain radial, colonial, or branching filter feeders. These organisms can build reef-like structures without being coral analogues.

Key choices are mobility, colony individuality, mineralization, symbiosis with phototrophs, and tolerance of anoxic deep water.

### Soft-bodied detritivore clade

A highly successful soft-bodied group can dominate soils, mud, caves, carcasses, and fungal beds.

Key choices are segmentation, burrowing mechanics, respiratory surface, toxin resistance, and whether some members form temporary cooperative masses.

### Wing-pair specialization within six-limbed ancestry

A creature with four walking legs and a separate wing pair can now originate within the six-limbed radiation, outside the dwarf genus but not necessarily outside its broader clade. Choose the ancestral wing pair and intermediate functions before designing powered flight. Gliding, running, climbing, swimming, and body balance offer different pathways; high gravity still imposes takeoff and structural costs despite the denser atmosphere.

## Ecological radiation questions

### Illuminated wet provinces

Define the dominant grazers, browsers, filter feeders, seed or spore predators, pollinator analogues, ambush predators, pursuit predators, scavengers, and decomposers.

Decide whether plant-animal mutualisms involve mobile gametes, spores, seed-like propagules, nutrient packets, or symbiotic microbial cultures.

### Outer dayside and salt basins

Design heat- and desiccation-tolerant dormant stages, burrowers, brine swimmers, episodic bloom feeders, salt-crust grazers, and migrants that exploit short wet pulses.

No large species should remain continuously exposed in the hottest inner lowlands.

### Twilight mountains

This is the most important region for dwarf-associated wildlife. Candidate niches include cliff climbers, tunnel-following herd animals, river fish analogues, cave-roosting flyers, root-zone burrowers, pack or cooperative predators, and species attracted to agricultural waste.

High gravity should shape locomotion, but the fauna need not all be squat. Supported climbers, swimmers, gliders, and small animals can explore different forms.

### Aphotic nightward margin

Most food chains depend on imported detritus, migrating organisms, river transport, groundwater, or dwarf infrastructure.

Potential specialists include slow detritivores, ice-edge grazers on microbial films, carrion trackers, dormant spore banks, brine predators, and animals whose active life is synchronized to irregular resource pulses rather than day-night cycles.

### Geothermal oases

Test both redox reactants, their delivery rates, microbial yield, and consumer maintenance. Finger-length consumers are a selected upper common size in productive qualifying oases, not a guaranteed inhabitant of every warm fracture. Animals can reproduce across many generations between floods; century-scale dormancy is not required. Some oases may be microbial only.

Floods can bring nutrients, oxidants, and colonists, but also scour, dilute, poison, or anoxically flood an oasis. Temporary blooms occur when the delivered resource balance is favorable. Irregular local resource and drainage cycles guide life histories without requiring the same response everywhere.

### Great-lake systems

Design large fauna around oxygenated surface and coastal waters. Deep anoxic layers support microbes, detrital specialists, dormant stages, and animals that make controlled short descents.

For selected gas-rich or strongly stratified lakes, exceptional overturn may cause severe ecological disruption; site-specific stability and gas budgets are required. Other lakes can mix harmlessly or remain isolated. Detection, tributary refuge, resistant stages, and recolonization are possible adaptations, not assured prediction of an event.

### Volcanic ranges

Native wildlife should distinguish chronic tolerable stress from acute lethal events. Plausible adaptations include ash-filtering airways, grooming, heat avoidance, metal-binding proteins, rapid recolonization, burrowing, and sensing immediate gas or vibration changes.

No organism should predict eruptions with certainty or remain biologically immune to concentrated hydrogen sulfide, carbon monoxide, oxygen displacement, pyroclastic flow, or lava.

### Natural caves

Food-rich cave mouths and shallow root zones can support sizeable communities. Deep natural systems should become progressively microbial and detritus-limited unless a redox interface or external organic subsidy is identified.

Separate the native cave fauna from engineered urban commensals that evolve around dwarven food, waste, heat, light, transit, and water systems.

## Dwarven anatomical decisions

Choose adult stature, body mass, and limb proportions for the six-limbed body under 1.53 g. Retain a compact overall silhouette while allocating adequate space to two arm girdles and avoiding unsupported claims of twice the strength. Quantify food, oxygen, and cooling demands after selecting mass and activity.

Use a dedicated ventilatory pump and separated oxygenated/deoxygenated circulation as the conservative baseline. Internally ventilated tidal lungs can support this design if exchange area, dead space, blood flow, and arm-girdle movement are compatible. Unidirectional airflow is an optional specialization with real analogues, not a necessary upgrade or guarantee of high-altitude performance.

Choose skeletal mineral and microstructure. Strength should come from geometry, composite architecture, remodeling, and joint surface area, not merely unusually dense bone.

Upper precision bias and lower power/bracing bias are retained with functional overlap. The working geometry uses two staggered girdles, distinct muscle and skeletal load paths, and a deep, moderately elongated torso; lower-hand pads permit occasional support. Exact digits, reach, shoulder excursion, and gait must be resolved together. Do not assign joint angles or digit counts merely to repeat the number six.

Choose skin covering. Options include hair-like insulation, filamentous feathers, scales with insulating underlayers, bare vascular skin in hot species, or regionally mixed structures.

Choose temperature regulation, sweating or evaporative mechanisms, kidney design, nasal exchange, and salt balance.

Quantify the preferred provisional egg-and-brood-nursery cycle: egg and hatchling masses, provisioned yolk, incubation temperature and duration, gas conductance, water balance, nitrogen-waste capacity, accessible hatching, and post-hatching feeding. Compare alternatives only if this budget exposes a concrete failure.

Choose maturation and lifespan from growth, repair, reproductive investment, parental survival, and food budgets. Large brains often benefit from long learning, but neither intelligence nor gravity sets a universal maturity age or guarantees centuries of life. Endogenous activity and breeding rhythms may persist despite the lack of an ordinary solar day.

## Species-level appearance decisions

### Central-massif species

Set the ancestral-looking combination of proportions, covering, pigmentation, altitude tolerance, and dietary flexibility against which the specialists are compared.

### Deep-massif subspecies

Keep differences modest: sensory weighting, circadian flexibility, carbon-dioxide tolerance, metabolism, and perhaps skin or eye traits. Avoid blind, depigmented, oxygen-independent “deep dwarves,” because the population remains connected to surface-fed systems.

### Sunward species

Balance water conservation against heat dissipation. Determine evaporative cooling, body covering, nasal water recovery, kidney concentration, activity timing, and use of cooled underground refuges.

### Volcanic-range species

Make this the most gracile dwarven form only in relative terms. Define heat tolerance, airway protection, toxin handling, foot protection, and behavioral evacuation. Do not grant immunity to disaster.

### Nightward species

Define insulation, extremity shortening, nasal heat exchange, brown-fat analogue, circulation, and the seasonal substitute used to regulate reproduction or migration on a world without ordinary days or seasons.

### High-mountain species

Define lung and chest form, capillary supply, hypoxia sensing, blood viscosity control, embryonic oxygen delivery, cold adaptation, and whether lowland exposure creates health costs.

## Civilization and domestication questions

Identify which native geophytes, fungi, aquatic organisms, and small animals were domesticated independently in each province.

Decide whether any large lake animal can be herded, penned, or seasonally harvested without requiring open-ocean technology.

Define the feedstock accounting for fungal agriculture and the fraction of food calories supplied by light-well crops, surface terraces, aquaculture, imported grain-like propagules, animal foods, and fungi.

Map the principal hydropower rivers, underground rail trunks, ventilation shafts, agricultural chambers, heat-rejection works, and remote nightside supply routes.

Distinguish wild cave organisms from deliberately cultivated decomposers and urban commensals.

## Quantitative checks to perform later

Run a mass-radius interior model for the final composition.

Run climate sensitivity cases across stellar flux, carbon dioxide, humidity, cloud albedo, surface albedo, and water distribution.

Run hydrological and ice-flow estimates for nightside sequestration and return.

Run N-body and tidal integrations for the satellite and companion system.

Calculate rock temperature, overburden stress, groundwater pressure, heat rejection, and ventilation for representative 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, and 2.5 km sites. Keep altitude and overburden separate. Historical mining depth alone is not evidence that the same site can sustain a residential city.

Build regional food, power, water, heat, maintenance-labor, and demographic budgets for 500,000–1,000,000 dwarves. Define capture area and usable yield before assigning agricultural percentages, and never double-count crop residues and fungal output. The isolated “3–5% of the wet core” answer in the conversation lacks its original measured quantity and remains an unresolved reference.

Estimate six-limb high-gravity blood pressure, muscle allocation, bone stress, center of mass, bipedal gait, supported scrambling, egg passage or gestation, and juvenile-growth constraints after final stature and reproductive choices are made.

Construct lake oxygen and productivity budgets before finalizing megafauna abundance.

## Recommended interactive order

First: the preferred brood-geode model’s egg, gas, water, waste, and parental-food budgets.

Second: respiratory architecture and skin covering of the dwarf-parent clade.

Third: digit count, stature, mass, and locomotor proportions.

Fourth: producer pigments, structural tissues, and maximum height.

Fifth: the first animal phylum outside the dwarf-bearing six-limbed clade.

Sixth: representative food webs for one Great Lake, one twilight mountain province, one volcanic arc, and one geothermal oasis.

Seventh in the wildlife-design sequence: detailed stellar, atmospheric, and satellite choices. Their basic consistency and water/energy constraints remain prerequisites for earlier numerical canon, not tests deferred until after every biological number is fixed.
